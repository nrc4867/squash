# Data model

One representation runs the whole pipeline: a part is an axis with a row of slices. The outlines size the slices, the slices sweep each part's solid, the character's surface is meshed from the joined solids, weights and marks address a part's coordinates on that surface, the body curve lays the joints along the slices, and contact flattens them. See [surface.md](surface.md) for the surface. Every entity below is immutable Kotlin data in `commonMain`. Storage maps it and never shapes it.

## Entities

| Entity | Holds | Kind |

| Body plan | A graph of sockets from a root spine, pair ids for mirrored sockets, the rest pose | Shipped preset, versioned |
| Character | Body plan, part instances, the data each mirrored pair shares (outlines, params and surface work by pair id), slots, palette, face sets, its working pose, and the library version it came from | Document; also its own library file |
| Part instance | Template id and version, the socket it sits on, its role and whether that is a guess, its own outlines, params and surface work while unpaired, pair id, side and that side's overrides | Document |
| Surface work | Base palette slot, area fills by area name, grain, fur rule, marks, mark groups, regions, paint, line weights and seam strokes: what a part resolves to, a pair shares or a side overrides | Document, beside outlines and params |
| Surface anchor | A point on a part's surface as (part, patch name, u along the axis, v around it, offset) | Document, inside paint, marks, line weights and touch-ups |
| Mark | A polyline of anchors, a width profile, an ink flag, a camera-locked flag, per-angle versions keyed by camera bin, a color slot, the ink's if none, a mirror flag for a part in the middle of the body, and a kind: a line, a fur stroke, which is a root alone, or a floating line, which is a root and a flight off the surface. | Patches | One or more named runs per part, each a grid of points, closed in v (a tube) or open (a sheet); a swept patch also keeps its slices: for each u a frame on the part's midline, height, width, a closed section spline and a proxy normal. | Part mesh | One grid per patch of a separate part, every vertex tagged with part, patch, bone, u and v; for a part in the field, its pads and other patches that are not runs | Derived |
| Resolved surface work | A part's marks, groups, regions, paint and expression marks with every anchor turned into a position and normal on the current mesh | Derived |
| Rig | A bone per template joint, named by part and joint, in the tree the body plan's sockets give; each vertex weighted to its part's bones by u, blended only across bands at joints and at seams; which joints stretch | Derived |
| Pose | Per bone, named by part and joint: its tip about the joint's hinge, its turn about its own length as it rests, its lean across the hinge and its twist about its own length taken before them all, its stretch and whether that stretch keeps volume, and its mode, free of its limits, within them, or locked out of every solve; the planted set and the lifted paws kept still rather than riding with the body, by part; how far the whole body has moved (`offset`); the point the head looks at (`lookAt`), or none while it looks ahead; the body curve kept as a curve (`Pose.curve`: its strokes, at most one in Side and one in Top, each its joined cubics' control points in that view's plane through the character's middle in character space, and its length factor, 1 at the body's rest length); touch-up deltas in anchor space, which join it with the tool that makes them | Document: one working pose on the character, `Character.pose`, which moves to one per shot key when Shot arrives |
| Palette | Named color slots per character (fur, cream, pads, nose, inner ear, ink by default), each with an optional toon material (a preset and the settings it changes: tones, shadow, rim, shine, see-through, glow; look.md, Materials), changed by `SetSlotMaterial`; parts, regions, paint and template color fields name a slot, never a color. A blended slot name (`blendedSlot`: two slots and a share) holds no slot of its own and reads as the first slot's colour carried that share toward the second's, as a parting's scalp tone does | Document, on the character; defaults from the body plan |
| Mark group | Marks with named roles, a draw order and controls (open, expression blend, width, mouth shape). A mouth is a mark group; eyes are painted | Document |
| Character shading | Flat or two-tone, whether far parts darken and by how much, and whether the light turns with the view or is fixed to the body (look.md, Shading) | Document, on the character |
| Character creases | How strongly the skin and the cloth crease and wrinkle, and how much shows at rest (look.md, Creases and wrinkles) | Document, on the character |
| Reference image | An image file's bytes as added, its pixel size, where it sits on the References board, and the palette slots picked from it. It never reaches a render | Document, beside the character |
| Trace | A reference image placed in one of the character's views: where it was first fitted over the character, its scale, offset and turn from there, flip, opacity, behind or over, shown and locked | Document, on the character, one per view |
| Region | A closed curve of anchors, which may run across parts, a palette slot and an ink flag | Document, in surface work |
| Seam stroke | Painted on the seam where the part holding it meets its parent: anchors, a brush (paint, erase or smooth), for paint the side whose bone it paints for (parent or child), a radius as a fraction of the holding part's axis length, a strength from 0 to 1, and whether its anchors are dabs apart. Shared by a pair with per-side overrides by id, as any surface work item; no surface draws it | Document, in surface work, in the order laid down |
| Reach | How far a joint's blend reaches, as a factor of the rig's guess, from 0.2 to 3: the part parameter `rig.reach.<joint>`, which no part's build reads, so a pair shares it and a side may override it | Document, in a part's params |

| Expression set | Marks per group and role with angle versions, shared with per-side overrides, plus a small face pose (hinge angles by part and joint name) | Document, on the character |
| Grain | A direction field in anchor space per part, default from its template; combed overrides stored as vectors | Document, in surface work |
| Fur rule | Spacing and length for a part's fur tufts; the board's defaults when a part sets none | Document, in surface work |
| Effect mark | A mark pinned to a landmark or drawn in camera space, keyed over time; sweat drops, shock lines, blush, speed lines, tears | Document, per shot |
| Scene | Set, backdrop, light, shading limits | Document |
| Shot | Camera, keys (poses by time), timing per character | Document |
| Project | The cast (character copies, each with its library version) and scenes | Document file |
| Library | Characters, later objects and sets, each as a chain of versions | Library files |
| Wardrobe | The character's garments, its wardrobe slots in order, its outfits and the outfit Build shows | Document, on the character |
| Garment | Its slot, layer, a branch per covered part (u and v range, ease, Side and Top outlines, surface work), join ink switches, hem ink, colour, body-part changes, loose ends, fit and part fits, painted areas, holes and Tuck choices | Document, in the wardrobe |

A character saves as a `.squash` file: a zip holding the document as `character.json`, with each reference image beside it under `images/` in its own encoding, so a file carries everything it shows. Plain JSON, with images inline as base64, opens too, from the open dialog or a drop on Home; the example characters are kept that way. Saving a character opened from JSON asks where to save it as a `.squash` file and leaves the JSON as it is.

Identity and versions: every entity has a ULID. A part instance pins its template id and version, and a character pins its body plan version, so a shipped template can change without silently changing saved characters. A template version that changes its ring or segment count changes only how finely the drawing is sampled, since anchors are template coordinates and not vertices.

The top three layers (library, project, character and what they hold) are document data. The derived entities are rebuilt from them and cached by inputs. Every piece of surface work reaches the mesh through an anchor, which is why paint, marks and touch-ups survive a refit.

## Hair locks

A character holds its lock groups (`lockGroups`), the scalp flows they grow by (`scalpFlows`, at most one a head) and its collision shapes (`colliders`), each set by one command: `SetLockGroup` adds, replaces or removes a group (an undone removal comes back in its place), `SetLockOverride` sets or clears one lock of a group by index, `SetScalpFlow` sets a head's flow (its whorl, parting, sweep and cowlicks) or with none returns the head to the default flow (an undone clearing comes back in its place), `SetColliders` sets the shapes, and `SetHairOutline` sets or clears a head's hair outline (`hairOutlines`, at most one a head), the silhouette its locks are fitted to. A head with no flow of its own grows by the default (`scalpFlowOn`): a crown whorl and no parting. A group names the head it grows on and its inner mass, both parts of the document; one whose head is not there builds nothing. A collider is a sphere or a capsule in body space at rest riding a part, set up from the body (`autoColliders`: a sphere in each head, a capsule along each neck, a sphere at each shoulder) when a preset first gives a head its locks or a recipe's hair asks for them. What each holds is in parts.md, Hair locks.

## Part template and part instance

The template is the recipe, in `character`, and the instance is what the user drew, in `document`. Slices are derived from the two and never saved, so a saved character is only outlines, parameters and anchors.

```kotlin
// Shipped with the app, versioned and immutable: a changed template is a new version file.
data class PartTemplate(
    val id: TemplateId, val version: Int, val name: String,   // "leg"
    val role: Role?,                   // what a part of this type is to the body; null for a generic part, whose role is guessed
    val axis: AxisSpec,                // rest direction and optional bend before anything is drawn, and where a head's face looks when not along it; the built axis is a spline through the Side midline
    val openEnd: End?,                 // the end that sits on the socket plane; null for a closed part
    val mount: MountSpec?,             // the point of a closed part that sits on its socket, in place of its axis start: a head carried under its middle
    val rings: Int, val segments: Int, // sampling of the body patch: rings along u; segments = control points of every section spline; an optional focus packs rings into a stretch of u
    val profile: ProfileSpec?,         // reads Side's up side as a profile whose short features stand out as a ridge: a nose, lips, a chin, a beak
    val blocks: List<Block>,           // Tube, Cap, Skull, ToeRow, Pad, Joint, Socket, Area, Landmark, Pocket; joints, sockets and areas are blocks; an area may lie on several patches, once on each, filled as one
    val params: Map<String, ParamSpec>, // toes 0 or 3..5, spread 0..40 deg, size; a new parameter's default reproduces the old build
    val views: Map<View, Sets>,        // SIDE sets height and the axis, TOP sets width, FRONT sets the cap or the section
    val section: SectionProfile,       // default closed section spline along u (circle, egg, keel, lens), used where no view sets it
    val generic: GenericSpec?,         // tube, blob, sheet: a part chooses its own section, views, rings and segments within these limits
)

// A block owns its behaviour, so a new block kind is one class and a regenerated schema.
// Nothing downstream switches on block kind; it reads the patches, joints, sockets and areas blocks produce.
sealed interface Block {
    fun build(ctx: BuildContext): List<Patch>
    fun lint(ctx: LintContext): List<Finding>
    fun joints(): List<JointSpec>
    fun sockets(): List<SocketSpec>
    fun areas(): List<AreaSpec>
    fun run(): Span?                   // the stretch of the axis its run of slices covers, if it builds one
    fun patchNames(): List<PatchName>  // the patches it builds, for the lint
    fun capShape(end: End): CapShape?  // the shape this block closes an end with, if it closes it
}

// In the document. Everything the user chose for one part.
data class PartInstance(
    val id: PartId,
    val template: TemplateRef,             // id and version, pinned
    val socket: SocketRef,                 // parent part and socket name; null for the root torso
    internal val own: PartData,            // the part's values while it has no pair; empty while it has one
    val pair: PairId?, val side: Side?,    // the side stays when a pair breaks, since the part still sits there
    internal val overrides: PartData,      // this side's values, replacing the ones they name in the pair's; empty with no pair
    val role: Role?,                       // what the part is to the body, from a closed list; both sides of a pair share it
    val roleGuessed: Boolean,              // the role was guessed from where the part sits and not yet confirmed
)

data class PartData(
    val outlines: Map<View, OutlineCurve>, // cubic Beziers in the part's own axes, open at the socket end
    val params: Map<String, Float>,
    val surface: SurfaceWork,              // base slot, area fills, grain, fur rule, marks, groups, regions, paint
)

data class Character(
    // ...
    val parts: List<PartInstance>,
    internal val pairs: Map<PairId, PartData>, // what each pair shares, held for exactly the pairs its parts name
) {
    fun resolved(part: PartInstance): PartData // the pair's values with the side's overrides on top, or the part's own
}

// Derived. A part is one or more patches; the mesh is a pure function of them.
data class Patch(
    val name: String,                  // "body", "membrane", "pad:palm"; anchors name it
    val nu: Int, val nv: Int,          // the template's sampling for this version
    val closedV: Boolean,              // a tube is closed in v, a sheet is open
    val slices: List<Slice>?,          // present for swept patches: the body, a toe, a muzzle
    val points: FloatArray,            // nu * nv * 3
)
data class Slice(
    val u: Float,
    val frame: Frame,                  // centre and orientation: on the midline in the plane a = constant when drawn, on the axis spline when not
    val height: Float, val width: Float,
    val section: ClosedSpline,         // exactly `segments` control points, sampled at fixed v, scaled by height and width
    val proxyNormalFrame: Mat3,        // along the axis spline's tangent at u, whichever way the ring stands
)

// Where surface work lives. Survives refits because patch, u and v are template coordinates, not positions.
data class Anchor(val part: PartId, val patch: String = "body", val u: Float, val v: Float, val offset: Float = 0f)
```

Rules the code keeps:

- **Outlines live in the part's own axes.** `a` runs along the rest direction from the socket end, `b` toward v = 0 (body up, or body forward for a part whose rest direction is within 45 degrees of vertical), `c` toward v = 0.25. A Side outline's points are (a, b), a Top outline's (a, -c), a Front outline's (c, b) looking down the axis. Patches are built in the same axes, in body-space orientation, with the socket plane at a = 0; see Placing parts. `PartBuilder.axes` gives the axes and `PartAxes.pointOnView` a view point's position.
- **Views set slices, nothing fits.** u runs linearly over the Side outline's range along a; an open start begins at the socket plane instead, and an open end finishes there. At each u a slice lies in its plane a = constant, and the outermost two crossings of that line give its centre (their midpoint) and height (their distance), so the rings' ends are the drawing's crossings: a drawn Side outline is the mesh's silhouette, up to the straight run between one ring and the next. Rings stand on parallel planes, so they cannot cross however quickly the midline moves, and the axis follows the drawing, so a digitigrade leg bends at the hock because it was drawn that way. An open outline counts as closed by the chord between its ends. A tip is a crossing with no gap, so a ring there has no height beyond a floor of 1/500 of the length, and a closed end's last ring reaches the drawn tip. A row of toes stands on the same planes. An undrawn part's rings follow the axis tangent. A drawn part with a curve of its own, on a template that sweeps, reads its drawing for size alone: its rings stand square to the curve at each u, the drawing's length is the length along it, and its midline is not read (see [surface.md](surface.md), Curved axes). The Top outline's crossings give width, and its midpoint is read only where the part sets `top.middle`: then it moves the slice's centre toward the part's side as Side's midpoint moves it up, so a lens or band drawn curved in Top wraps round what it sits on (`ViewSizing.sideAt`, and the field fit's shift in `FieldFit`). Without it a part's middle stays on its axis whatever Top's midline does, so a hand-drawn Top that is nearly symmetric moves nothing. The Front outline shapes the cap, blending in over the last sixth of the axis, or, where the template says `section`, the whole cross-section, which is how a chest gets a keel or a flat side. A missing view takes the template's section profile and the other view's extent, so the section keeps its proportions; the view present rules u. A Front outline alone changes the section and leaves the sizes to the template. `axis.length` sets an undrawn part's length, and the `size` parameter multiplies both that and a drawing, so a locked pane's map divides a stroke by it and the stroke builds under the pointer at any size. An outline with no extent, or wholly behind the socket plane, sets nothing.
- **Release does what the preview shows.** While a stroke grows, the pane draws what release would produce: the connectors to the start or the socket plane, and the start point lit when the stroke is close enough to close on it. The shade on a stroke is the outline profile itself: height and width views read the two outermost crossings, section views the whole loop, as the build does.
- **Half outline mirrors across the part's axis, or down the middle of Front.** One edge keeps to its side of the centre line and of a socket plane, its far end joins its mirror image, square across the line if it stops short, and Close end decides only the other end. The released outline is exactly symmetric: the first half's fit and its mirror image. Over a drawn outline a stroke redraws its span and that span's mirror image in one step; an image too far from the curve shows dotted and changes nothing.
- **Assists work ahead of the preview.** Stabilizer, guides and forced points change the samples or the options going in, so preview and release share them. The Stabilizer is a string the line trails on, moved only by the pointer's positions. Guides stretch the stroke's coordinate across them, so nothing breaks and no point moves past the reach. A modifier pressed during a stroke marks where the pointer is, taking up the string, and holding it marks no more.
- **A spine and width writes outlines and nothing else.** A centre stroke in Side and a half-width along it make a Side outline from the spine's offsets and a Top outline with that width along a, through one `SetOutlines`, so one undo step. The stroke takes Draw outline's Stabilizer and Snap to guides: it trails the pointer on the same string, and its far end, the end away from the socket plane, catches a guide while the rest of it stretches to follow, so no other point of a centre line is pulled onto a guide that describes the part's edge. The width drag follows the pointer directly. The spine is not stored; what remains are ordinary curves. A closed end rounds off to nothing within its own reach, so Side and Top close where the spine does, and Top takes Side's reach, so the two agree where the part starts and ends. An offset that doubles back across planes a = constant is written as drawn; the preview marks it, since the outermost crossings are all a slice reads.
- **A drawn view holds when another is drawn.** Only Spine and width writes two curves in one command, and a stroke on one view leaves the others' curves alone. A build reads Side's and Top's extents at each a, whichever rings land there, so a drawn Side or Top keeps its silhouette over the reach they share. Past Side's reach the part stops, so a longer Top is cut there and marked as a disagreement, and past Top's reach it keeps Top's last width. A Front keeps its shape, and Side and Top rule its size. A paw's waist before its toes sits at a fraction of its length, so a Side of another length slides it.
- **Refits are local.** A slice reads only the crossings at its own u, so changing a span of a Side or Top outline moves only slices within one table station (1/128 of the length) of that span. A slice's proxy normal frame reads the axis tangent, and so its neighbouring knots, which reaches two axis knots (1/32) further and moves no vertex. Two edits reach further by their nature: moving the outline's far end, or a span holding its lowest or highest x, along a rescales u for every slice, since the ring count is fixed, and a Front `section` outline applies to every slice.
- **Everything is a spline.** Outlines are cubic Béziers. The axis is a C1 cubic spline through the Side midline at fixed knots, with Catmull-Rom tangents so that moving a point changes the curve only within two spans of it, and without `top.middle` it stays in the plane of a and b. Joints and an undrawn part's rings sit on it; a drawn ring's centre is the midline itself, and the tangent sets only its proxy normal frame. With `top.middle` the midline also runs sideways, so the axis leaves that plane. Every cross-section is a closed cubic B-spline with exactly `segments` control points, sampled at fixed v, so a circle, an egg, a keel and a drawn chest outline are one type with different control polygons, and the mesh topology never changes. A part is a lofted surface: sections stacked along the axis.
- **A template can remap its views.** The `views` map says which view sets what, and an outline lies in the plane of what its view sets: Side's plane for the view that sets height, Top's for the one that sets width (`PartTemplate.planeOf`). The panes, Start from template and the build all read it, so `wing-feathered`, whose Top sets height and Side width, takes its planform drawn in Top as each slice's chord and its Side as its thickness.
- **Views disagree where one passes another's extent.** Side and Top compare where the part starts and ends along u, Front compares against Side's height and Top's width, and a Side narrower than Front never counts, since a cap is meant to be smaller. The highlight marks the stretch past the limit, which is a comparison of intervals per stroke.
- **A profile stands its short features out as a ridge.** A template with `profile` reads its height view's up side as a profile. Where the drawn up crossing stands out of the base a ball `span` of the drawn length across reaches, rolled along behind it (the crossing opened by a round structuring element), that ring's section is squeezed into the base's depth and a ridge `keel` of its half width across, a squared bell, rises from its middle to the crossing (`ProfileKeel`, in `ViewSizing.slice`). So a nose, lips, a chin or a beak stand out narrow, a forehead or a muzzle longer than the span swells the whole ring as any drawing does, and a curve rounder than the ball, a round face, stands out nowhere. The ridge is part of the ring's section, so it shades and outlines as the ring does.
- **Rings can focus.** A topology's `focus` puts `share` of a run's ring spacings inside its u stretch and spaces the rest evenly outside it (`RingFocus`), so a face gets most of an upright head's rings; joints still land on rings.
- **Rings and segments sample the drawing.** Ring i maps to u and segment j to v, so a part's slices are found by arithmetic, and the counts set how finely the drawing is sampled into the solid and into a separate part's own mesh. Anchors are held in u and v, not in vertices, so they land on the surface whatever the counts. A generic part chooses its own: its `generic.rings` and `generic.segments` change its sampling.
- **A generic part shapes its own build.** A part of a template with `generic` holds its choices as parameters: `generic.section`, the place of its profile among circle, egg, keel and lens; `generic.view.side`, `.top` and `.front`, 1 where that view sets it, Side height, Top width and Front the section, at least one on; and `generic.rings` and `generic.segments`, kept within the template's limits (`GenericParams`, `PartTemplate.shapedBy`). A part that has set none builds as its template says. `asBuilt` gives the template a part builds with: turned and its joints moved by Move, shaped by those choices, and, for a generic part on no socket, its open end closed round. The slice build, the assembly, the panes and the outline tools all read it (`CharacterSession.builtTemplate`).
- **Open ends close at the socket plane.** The open end's slice sits on the parent's surface at the socket, and the parent's slices are untouched. A stroke that closes anyway is cut at that plane. A closed stroke short of the plane opens around its nearest point, no deeper than it lies from the plane, and reaches the plane along x from there. An open stroke's ends reach the plane along x; if that would cross the stroke, the stroke counts as closed by its chord and opens the same way. With Close end off a stroke's ends stay where they are, and only what passes the plane is cut.
- **Smooth runs from a light blur to a strong one.** The filter's passes run as 8 per unit of Smooth up to the default, 45%, then climb as a cube to 512 at 100%, a blur of 32 screen pixels standard deviation on samples 2 pixels apart, whatever the zoom. A stroke's corners hold still through the blur only if they are sharp across 4 samples and still turn 60 degrees across a window as wide as the blur, so jitter and round bends are taken and a real corner stays.
- **Fit sets how few points a stroke keeps.** Release fits the smoothed stroke, between its corners, to joined cubics that stray from it by at most the Stroke section's Fit, a share of the curve's longest span so a small part or a far zoom is no coarser than a large one: 16 three-hundredths of the span at Smooth, falling evenly in scale towards 1 at Faithful, about 3 at the default 60%, which draws a limb in four to eight points, and never under a screen pixel. The last cubic reaches back from the stroke's end as far as stays within that, then cubics from the start each reach as far as stays within it up to there, found by bisection on where they end; a join whose two cubics fit as one goes, and a join whose handles differ more than twice in length slides between its neighbours to where they are most even while both cubics still fit, so a round tip has even handles. Each join leaves along the stroke's direction there, so every join but a corner is smooth, and a section's ends leave along the stroke 16 screen pixels in, past the hand's jitter at a corner or a closing seam, and corners closer than 32 screen pixels along the stroke, or that near an open end, count as one, so a kink where the hand closes a loop or turns back makes one point rather than a bunch of short sections. At Faithful the fit instead keeps within one screen pixel, splitting each cubic at its worst point, at the cost of more points. While a stroke is drawn, the curve release would store shows thin over it with its points. Oversketch, Half outline, Mirror and the Smooth tool fit at the same setting; Start from template, Spine and width and the pose's body curve fit faithfully. A saved outline keeps its points until Simplify refits it.
- **Simplify refits an outline on request.** Simplify outline refits the picked outline, the selected point's or the active view's, and Simplify part every outline of the part, as one undo step. Corners, where the two sides of a join turn by more than about five degrees, and an open outline's ends stay where they are, each run between them is sampled and fitted as a stroke is, leaving and reaching them along the old directions, and the new points keep to the kept side of the socket plane. Each refit strays at most half of Fit's share of the outline's longest span from the one before, and Simplify refits again until a refit drops nothing, so a second Simplify changes nothing. An outline with nothing to drop is left as it is.
- **Points are added and taken out by hand.** In Edit curves an Alt press on the curve, away from its points, adds an anchor there by splitting its section, so the shape stays exactly, and selects it. Delete point, on Del or in the selected point's section, takes the selected anchor out and refits its two sections as one leaving and reaching their outer anchors along the old directions; an open outline's ends and a closed outline's last three stay. Each is one undo step.
- **A held modifier snaps a stroke.** While Ctrl is held the stroke stands as a straight line from where it began to the pointer, and while Ctrl and Shift are both held as the ellipse filling the box between them; Alt and Shift pressed during a stroke force points along a freehand stroke, so neither snaps. The snapped shape takes the path a held shape takes, with no smoothing, mirroring or forced points.
- **A middle part mirrors across the line of symmetry.** A part with no pair and no side runs down the body's middle, so in Top and Front, which show its left and right, its outline is symmetric about its centre line, the line of symmetry. There the line shows faintly in Draw outline and Edit curves, a stroke's ends and extremes catch it as they catch the guides, and an anchor dragged within the guides' reach of it lands on it. Alt held as a stroke starts, or during a drag, works free of the line and the guides. With Mirror on, which it is by default, a stroke there draws half the outline and its mirror image, as Half outline does. Side shows a middle part's front and back, so Mirror is off there and its tooltip says the view has no left and right. A paired part mirrors as a pair in every view.
- **The Smooth tool brushes an outline in place.** A drag works on the curve as samples 2 screen pixels apart, each dab pulling those inside the brush toward a blur along the curve as wide as half the brush's radius, by the strength and how near the brush's centre they lie. Every section outside the stretch that moved keeps its values, the sections at its edges are cut where it starts and stops, and the stretch is fitted anew from the old curve's directions there, so the outline can gain or lose sections. An open outline's ends do not move and its new points keep to the kept side of the socket plane; a closed outline's seam moves as one point. A corner inside the brush rounds. A straight run has nothing to move and writes nothing.
- **A held stroke becomes a clean shape.** A stroke whose pointer rests is read as a line along its chord, or, if it comes back to its start, as the ellipse of the same area moments or the capsule on the same axis, whichever it strays from least, and stays freehand if it fits none. Where a view has a socket plane and Close end is on, an open stroke whose ends both pass the plane or come within closing reach of it, with nothing else of it on the part's side, is read as the ellipse or capsule that a cut at the plane would leave: the line from the middle of its mouth through the centre of what it encloses is a diameter of the ellipse and the axis of the capsule, and the ellipse's centre is searched along it, so a U that flares or narrows towards the plane still reads. The whole shape reaches behind the plane and takes the usual cut. Short of the plane, in Front or with Close end off, such a stroke stays freehand. The rest is timed around the wait for the next pointer event. Until release the pointer moves a line's end or sizes a closed shape, and the shape then takes the path a stroke takes, so its ends are handled alike and it is stored as an ordinary outline.
- **Start from template makes an ordinary outline.** It reads the outermost crossings of the part's swept patches, as built with nothing drawn in that view, and fits them as a stroke is fitted, so what follows is an edit like any other. An outline sets only what its view reads, so the view's silhouette comes back within tolerance and the others can move: a Top alone sets a straight axis, which loses a muzzle's drop in Side; a Front sets only a section's shape; and a drawn paw with toes shares its width among them where undrawn ones fan out, with a waist where its tube closes. A paw with no toes comes back as it was.
- **Heads are blocks too.** Skull is a block whose slices run front to back, with a muzzle Tube and eye and ear sockets. Face marks anchor to Skull's u and v like any other mark.
- **Mirroring is shared data plus overrides.** A pair stores one set of outlines, params and surface work; each side's overrides replace the values they name. `Character.resolved`, or `resolvedShape` for its outlines and parameters alone, is the only way to read a part's values, since the fields behind it are internal to `document`, and it is what the slice row node builds from, so both sides rebuild when the pair changes and a part rebuilds only when what it resolves to changes. `SetOutline` and `SetParameter` write the pair for a paired part, or with `thisSide` that side's override; clearing an override shows the pair's value again. Breaking a pair copies the shared data, under each side's overrides, into both and clears the pair id; each part keeps its side. A pair's data is created with its first part and goes with its last.
- **Surface work follows the same rule, by item.** Base slot, area fills, grain and fur rule replace by name; marks, groups, regions and brush strokes replace by id, and an item only a side holds goes after the shared ones. A pair holds its anchors as its left side reads them. `resolved` reads them for the asking side: an anchor on a sided part moves to the twin on that side with v unchanged, and an anchor on a part in the middle of the body reads 1 - v from the right. A write from the right turns them back, so the same drawing stores the same from either side; a command's `stored` flag marks anchors already in that form, as an undo's are, so undo restores them exactly. A side's override holds anchors as that side sees them. An expression's marks keep the same shape, shared with `left` and `right` on top, read through `Character.expressionMarks`, and breaking a pair moves them onto each side. A side cannot hide a shared item, only replace it. The slice row node builds from `PartData.shape`, so surface work never rebuilds a mesh.
- **Poses stay re-editable.** A pose keeps the body curve as a curve and the planted set as flags, so re-sampling after a character update uses the same inputs. Touch-ups are deltas in anchor space, which is why they survive Update from library.
- **Detail beyond the sweep is anchored, not modelled.** Branching shapes are other blocks or parts. Grooves and muscle lines are a later shape touch-up layer: push and pull offsets stored in anchor space at build time, the same mechanism as per-shot touch-ups, so they survive refits.

## Placing parts

One graph node assembles the character from the built parts, so `Settled` covers it. Nothing is stored: where a part sits is derived every time from its socket and its parent's built surface.

- **The root sits at the origin**, along the plan's rest, which lint holds equal to its template's. Body space has +X forward, +Y up, and -Z the left side. An upright plan roots on a template that rests up: the biped's root is `torso-upright`, the torso's blocks resting along +Y with its up the body's forward, so v = 0 is its front, v = 0.5 its back and v = 0.75 its left side. The bird's root is `torso-bird`, the same blocks resting about 50 degrees up and forward, perched; its rest is within 45 degrees of vertical, so its up is the body's forward too, and its upward share, 0.77, makes it upright.
- **A socket reads the built surface.** A child's socket plane sits at its socket's (u, v) on the parent's built patches, so a redrawn parent carries its children with it. A paw on a shorter leg rises with the leg's end.
- **A garment never moves a socket.** While a worn garment reshapes a part (a Redraw of its outlines or shape, or a Swap), the part's sockets are read from its skeleton: the part built bare, from its own data with the worn placement (`move.` parameters) kept, so it turns as the part shows (`skeletonRowNode`, the assembly's `AssemblySource.Skeleton`). A socket's point on the skeleton is then carried from the middle of the skeleton's ring at its u to the middle of the shown part's ring at the u the garment gives that socket, where it names one (`move.socket.<name>.u`, `socket.<n>.u`): a garment that only thickens a part leaves its hips and shoulders where they were, and one that draws it longer, as the woman's skirt draws her torso into a bell from the hem up, carries them along with the rest of the part without moving them out to its wider surface. While no garment reshapes a part its skeleton builds nothing, and its own build is read.
- **A part sits slightly inside its parent**, so no gap shows. It sinks back along its own axis, or along the surface's normal where the socket says `outward: normal`, by a fraction of its template's first ring, or of its widest where it has no open end, times the socket's `sink`, 1 when left out; a hand-made socket's is its `socket.<n>.sink`, 0 or more, and a template's own socket's is moved on the parent part by `move.socket.<name>.sink` (with `.left.` or `.right.` for one side), read where the socket's place is, 0 to 4. A hand-made socket's `socket.<n>.outset` first stands the part that far off the surface along the surface's normal, in body units, so a part a garment owns sits on the cloth with an outset past the garment's fit and sink 0, a pocket or a button standing just proud of a shirt. A limb on the side of a round body needs more than the template's own depth, or the outer half of its open ring stands clear of the body: the plans' hip, shoulder and leg sockets sink their parts 1.6 to 2.2 times. The depth reads the template, never the drawing, so a stroke on a part cannot move the plane it is drawn from.
- **A closed part can hang from a mount.** A template with no open end may name a `mount`, a (u, v) on its surface, and the part is carried back so that point, not its axis start, sits on the socket: the first round head's mount is under its middle, so it sits over the shoulders instead of running forward from them, and its `neck` joint is at the same u, so it turns where it hangs. The upright round head, `head-round` version 2, needs none: its axis start, the chin's height, sits on the neck's end, and its drawing places the face, skull and jaw round that point. The offset reads the template's undrawn build, as the depth does, so a part drawn longer or shorter than its template seats by the template's proportions.
- **Lint holds every socket's part inside its parent.** `SocketFit` places each template a plan socket, or a template's own socket, takes, undrawn on its undrawn parent through the real assembly, and fails the plan when the ring on the part's socket plane, or a closed part's mount or axis start, lies outside the parent's runs. A template's own sockets are checked by test over the shipped templates, where the lower beak's socket on the upper beak is the one known exception: its ring sits inside the head, which a pairwise check cannot see.
- **Placing is translation.** What a part sits on never turns it: its socket only carries it, and only a pose rotates it. Which way it points is its own: its rest, then Move's lean and outward, then its roll, below. The rest direction is the part's own: its template's, or for a hand-placed part the one it chose, `move.rest`, the place of forward, back, up, down or out (away from the body's plane on the left, mirrored on the right) among `RestDirection`'s entries, set in Move's Place part for a generic part on a socket. It replaces the template's rest before Move's angles turn it, its up following, so a tube on the body's rear end rests back along it; what it sits on still never turns it.
- **A part can be moved from where its template puts it.** The Move tool writes parameters under `move.`, so pairs, overrides, undo and saving treat them like any other. A socket's place is stored on its parent, `move.socket.<name>.u` and `.v`, which is why an empty socket and the planned part on it move too. A single parent has one socket for both its children, so with Mirror off a side writes `move.socket.<name>.left.u` and so on, which win over the shared place, and a move of both clears them. A stored v always reads as the left side's. A part's own angles are `move.outward`, away from the body's plane on the left and mirrored on the right, and `move.lean`, toward the part's up, each up to 85 degrees either way: each adds its tangent onto the rest axis, so neither can reach square across the rest, and a part pointing further round takes another rest. They turn the template's rest axis before anything builds, keeping the up it had, so drawings keep their meaning, the panes tilt as they do for an ear or a tail, and the topology does not change. `move.roll` then turns the part's up about its turned axis by any angle, toward its v 0.25 side, so a sheet resting out faces forward at 90; outlines, sockets, joints and the bones built from them follow. Place part shows Outward, Lean and Roll as sliders. `move.joint.<name>` sets where a joint between a part's ends sits. An undrawn part's bend follows the joint on it, and a drawn part keeps its silhouette, its rings shifting along it to keep one on the joint.
- **A socket can be placed by hand.** The Move tool's Add socket stores a socket on the part it is pressed on, as that part's parameters `socket.<n>.u`, `.v`, `.outward`, `.sink` and `.pair` (`HandSockets`), with `<n>` the next number the part has not used, so pairs, overrides, undo, saving, a template version change and Update from library treat it like any other parameter, and placing one is one `AddParameters`, which fails on a part that already holds any of its names, so a socket is never placed over one in use; `HandSockets.move` moves one. It is named `socket.<n>` and read as socket n: Library's footer names it socket 1 while it is targeted. It names no `takes`, so it offers every newest loaded template, and an empty one shows its dot and no planned part. Its outward direction is the surface's normal turned `outward` degrees toward the part's end, 0 being `outward: normal`. With `pair` set it is two sockets mirrored across the body's middle, its v stored as the left side reads it, and a part added on it is a pair placed in one `AddParts`; on a paired part it is mirrored across the pair as any socket is. Clearing its parameters deletes it, one undo step, and only while nothing sits on it. Its parameters never reach the part's own build.
- **A socket on a digit.** A hand-made socket with `socket.<n>.digit` lies on that digit's patch: its u and v are read on the patch (`SocketSpec.patch`), so it rides the digit's last bone that reaches its u and follows the digit as it curls, a ring on a finger. The number names the digit as `DigitPatches` does: 0 on are the numbered digits of the template's first row, `fingers:2` for 2, and -1 down its named digits in the template's order, -1 for `fingers:thumb` on `hand` 7. Without it a socket's u and v lie on the part's first run reaching u, which past a hand's palm is `fingers:0`. Move's Add socket sets it when the press lands on a digit; a recipe socket names the patch (`patch: "fingers:2"`), which fails when the template has no such digit and warns when the part as shaped does not build it (a hand of four digits has no `fingers:3`). A template's own `socket` block takes `patch` the same way, which Save as template keeps. Regions and marks name a digit's patch in their anchors as any patch; a recipe warns of one on a patch the part does not build, and of a garment print on a digit's patch, since a garment's prints are laid on the cloth of a part's first run, whose (u, v) its cells read.
- **A row of sockets by hand.** A hand-made socket whose `socket.<n>.count` is above 1 is a row (`HandSockets.specs`): that many sockets evenly spaced from its `.u` to `.to` along the part and from its `.v` to `.toV` round it (its one v when unset); a row whose `.toV` lies a whole turn from its `.v` closes round the part, its sockets a turn's share apart, as studs round a belt, named `socket.<n>.<i>` from 0, with its outward angle and pair flag. Each socket carries a `scale`, the size a part placed on it starts at: `.size` on the first, falling off linearly to `.falloff` of that on the last. Move's Add socket places one from the press toward the part's further end when its Count is above 1, at its First size and Last size, one `AddParameters`. Add part on any socket of a row fills every empty socket of the row that takes the template in one `AddParts` (`placeOnRow`), each part's `size` its socket's scale, so the parts are then each the user's to resize; the row's numbers never resize a part already placed. A recipe's part on a row's socket starts at that socket's scale where it gives no size of its own, as Add part's does. A row's sockets are not dragged one by one; picking one picks the row, which Outward angle and Delete socket act on whole, Delete only while none of its sockets holds a part.
- **Copies of one part.** A hand-made socket with `socket.<n>.spread` or `socket.<n>.repeat` is one socket that shows the one part placed on it as many copies (`HandSockets.copiesOf`, `CopySpec`): the part is built once, its slice row, mesh and surface, and the assembly places that build again for each copy (`PartCopies`, each `CopyPlace` an origin and a rotation and scale of the part's own mesh), so editing the part changes every copy and a few hundred copies cost one build and a placing each. A part on such a socket shows only as its copies: it leaves the one surface (`isFieldPart`), draws as one mesh instanced at each copy (`MeshDraw.instances`, uploaded once), has its lines found on each copy's patches (`partGrids`) and no crease, and frames, boxes and floors by its copies. `.spread` copies are scattered over the area from `.u` to `.to` along the parent and `.v` to `.toV` round it, a whole turn round it a band that wraps past v 1, picked by area so a wide stretch gets as many as a narrow one, from a seeded sequence (`.seed`) that picks the same places every build, each kept only where it stands `.spacing` from every copy kept before it, so fewer show where no more fit; each stands along the surface's normal tilted `.outward` toward the part's end, leans up to `.leanJitter` degrees a random way, turns up to `.turnJitter` about itself, sinks by `.sink` of the part's depth or stands `.outset` off, and sizes at `.size` falling to `.falloff` over the area's outer `.edge` share, varied by `.sizeJitter` either way; it picks its base slot among the part's own and its `shades` (`SurfaceWork.shades`, `SetShades`). With `.rows` the copies lie in that many rows evenly from `.u` to `.to`, `.spread` of them evenly round each (round the whole part, a row closes on itself), every other row shifted round by `.stagger` of the gap (0.5 by default), and the seed picks only their jitter: plates and scales, laid back toward the part's end by `.outward` near 90 degrees so each lies over the root of the next row. `.repeat` copies lie along the smooth curve from (`.u`, `.v`) through `.via.<k>.u` and `.v` to (`.to`, `.toV`), v taking the short way round, each standing on the surface with its axis along the normal and its up along the curve, rolled `.roll` degrees more than the one before; with `.sag` the curve droops that far at its middle under gravity, a catenary between its two ends, lying on the parent wherever the droop would take it inside. `.repeat` 0 places as many as fit at `.pitch`, or with no pitch at the part's own length along its up, end to end, and for a closed ring (`ring`, a `loop` block) at its inside length, so each link passes through the next. A copy's place on its parent is picked at rest and read again on the parent as a pose shows it, so copies ride the parent (`posedBy`). At most `CopySpec.MAX_COPIES`, 1000, a socket; a recipe asking for more fails. With `.digit` its area or path lies on that digit. Add socket's layout (Sockets, Spread, Path) places one with the press; its fields set the count, seed, area, spacing, size, jitter, pitch, roll and sag, the picked socket's own while one is picked, each one undo step.
- **Joints and landmarks by hand.** The Move tool's Add joint stores a joint on the part it is pressed on as that part's parameters `joint.<n>.u`, `.hinge` (the place of x, y or z), `.low` and `.high` (its limits in degrees) and `.stretch` (`HandJoints`), and Add landmark a landmark as `landmark.<n>.u` and `.v` (`HandLandmarks`), each named by its number, `joint.1` and `landmark.1`, since parameters hold only numbers; placing one is one `SetParameters`, deleting one clears them, and a pair shares them like any parameter. `asBuilt` adds them as blocks to the template the part builds with (`withHandAnatomy`), its joints listed in u order after its other blocks, so the slice build, the rig, Pose and the lint read them as the template's own: each is a bone named by part and joint, `PartBuilder.mesh` tags vertices by them, the Rig stage shows their dots and seams, and Turn holds them within their limits. A part with a hand-made joint and no joint at its end gains `tip` there, limits -30 to 30 degrees, so each hand-made joint owns the span to the next and the tip carries what sits on the end; a generic template's fewest rings rise to what its joints need. A joint is placed between 0.05 and 0.95 of u and no nearer than 0.05 to another, and not where the built template would lint worse, which holds the joints ordered and enough rings for them. Add eyes on a part with a hand-made landmark sets the starter eyes about the first one, else about its template's `eye` landmark, the other eye across the middle (`eyeLandmark`, `newEyesCommand`, in `character`). A landmark on the head's side, the bird head's at v 0.26, gives side-set eyes, one facing out from each side.
- **Pairs by hand.** `JoinPair` makes two unpaired parts the sides of a new pair as one step: the left's outlines, parameters and surface work become the pair's, the right side takes the left's role and the socket given, and what the right held of its own that differs from the left's stays as its overrides, so nothing it drew is lost; `SplitPair` undoes it exactly. Pair with… pairs parts of one template on one parent, the right side moving onto the left's socket, or on the two sides of a paired parent on sockets of one name. A part's surface follows its side, so the right side mirrors as soon as it joins.

- **A foot or hand is as thick as its limb's end.** A template that names the parameter `limb` (`foot` 10, `hand` 9, `paw` 12) takes, whenever the part leaves it unset, the radius of the end of the part it sits on as a share of its own length (`LimbEnds`): half the limb's mean drawn thickness, Side's and Top's, one radius back from where its outline closes, read with no mesh from its outlines, or from its undrawn silhouettes where it has none. It is derived as each build's input is made and never stored, so moving the limb's outline rebuilds the foot. The template's ankle or wrist capsule takes that radius and ends at the socket, its dome inside the limb's end, so the limb runs straight on into the foot or hand.
- **Legs stay level as they lean.** A part whose template sets `axis.bearsWeight` reaches down to its child by its length along its tilted axis, and by what lies off that centre line: its socket plane sinking along the tilted axis, the point of the far socket's ring standing off the axis, and the child sinking along the surface's normal there. `LegLengths` measures each of those with the Move angles and without, in the part's rings at its far socket, drawn or not, from its outlines and parameters with no mesh; each part's measure is its squareness `|along · down|` plus the off-centre change as a share of its length. The smallest measure among the parts, placed or planned, is `k`, and a part with measure `m` builds at `1 - (m - k) / square`: 1 for the part that sets `k`, and exactly 1 for all when none leans, since a part with no tilt measures nothing. So every part's reach is `k` of its centre line's length, and a leg drawn short shortens nothing and legs of different drawn lengths keep their ratio. A paired part with Mirror off counts for its side alone. The factor is derived on every build and never stored, so undo, leaning back and saving hold nothing of it. It is an input to the part's build: outlines scale along a as the build begins and an undrawn axis's length scales by it, while thickness, bend and everything else across the axis stay, so slices, mesh, sockets, joints, the paw on the end and surface work all read the shorter leg. A lean reruns the other legs' builds and a surface edit none. A socket plane reads its template, so it does not move, and the ground follows the lowest paw, so the body drops. The paws agree with each other to within the normal a drawn end bends, read at full length; a thick leg leaning back far enough can put its own far rim under them, which then sets the ground. An undrawn part's rings keep the up its Move angles keep, so a paw's socket stays on the same side of the leg at any angle.
- **The right side mirrors across the body's plane.** Z and the triangle winding flip, and the socket's v reads 1 - v on an unmirrored parent. A mirrored parent already reflects what sits on it, so a socket on it keeps its v. Both sides build from the one `Character.resolved` of the pair.
- **Bipeds rest in A-pose.** A plan socket's `spread` is the degrees a part placed on it starts leaning out from the body's plane: Add part starts the part with `move.outward` at it (`OpenSocket.spread`, `placeOnRow`), and a planned part on it is built so. The biped's shoulders spread 45 degrees, so its arms are built, weighted and dressed out from the body; its legs stand straight down from its hips, since a leg that bears weight rests straight down. A hand-made figure such as the woman sets the same angles with Move. The character's display pose brings the arms down; see The rig.
- **An empty socket shows what it would take.** The plan's empty sockets each place a planned part, its first `takes` template built with nothing drawn and leaning as its socket spreads, recursively, stopping where a plan would loop. So a character with only a torso shows the whole body in its rest pose. A planned part is never in the document. A character's floor lies under the lowest vertex of everything shown.
- **Tip sockets.** A toe row with `tips` gives each digit it builds a socket `<row>:<i>.tip` (`DigitTips`) at the end of the digit's span, `outward: axis`, whose `patch` names the digit, `<row>:<i>`. A socket with a `patch` lies on that patch's surface at its u and v, not on the first run reaching its u, so a digit's tip socket sits on the digit's last ring, its tip's pole, and a part there sinks along its own axis into the digit. The assembly reads which digits have tips from the patches the part's build made; Add part, Parts, roles and Left behind, which hold no build, read the digits the row's count would build from the part's parameters over its template's defaults, as a part's areas are read, and both name the same sockets. A tip names no template unless the row's `takes` does, so it shows its dot and no planned part. Add part on a tip fills every empty tip of that row on that part that takes the template, in one `AddParts`, as it fills a hand-made row (`placeOnRow`). Move does not drag a tip. The rig carries a part on a socket with a `patch` by the bone that patch follows at the socket's u, so a part on a digit's tip rides the digit's last joint; see The rig. `paw` carries tips from version 4, and the `claw` template sits on them.

The node holds, per part, its mesh, silhouettes and extents, each derived in the graph from the part's build.

## Roles

A part's role is what it is to the body: body, spine, neck, head, jaw, ear, arm, hand, leg, foot, tail, wing, fin, horn, hair or other, a closed list (`Role`). The pose tools, Add part's tile art, the Face stage and the rig read roles, never template ids; a template id is read only by the templates, the plans and the catalog.

- **A part holds its own role.** `PartInstance.role`, with `roleGuessed` while it is a guess. `SetRole` sets it on a part and the other side of its pair, as one undo step; a role chosen in the inspector is confirmed, which clears the guess. A part saved before roles reads its template's (`PartRoles.of`, `PlacedPart.role`), and a part with neither has its role guessed when read.
- **A plan's root is the body.** `newCharacter` places it with its template's role, or body, confirmed.
- **A typed template gives the role.** A template's `role` is the role a part placed from it takes, set by the same `AddParts` that places it (`placeOnSocket`, `PartRoles.placed`). A leg on the front half of a body part, ahead of its middle along its rest, or above it on an upright one, takes arm, and a foot on an arm takes hand, so a four-legged plan's front limbs are stored as its arms and hands, as a biped's would be.
- **A four-legged character names its arms as legs.** A character is four-legged when one of its arms bears weight (`PartRoles.fourLegged`); there an arm reads Front leg and a hand Front paw, and the stored roles stay arm and hand.
- **An upright character names its limbs by role and side.** A character is upright when its root part rests up, its rest's upward share at least 0.7 (`PartRoles.upright`, `Rig.isUpright`), as the biped's torso does. There the Pose stage names a limb by its end's role and its side, Left foot, Right hand (`limbNames`), its status bar counts feet, and its arms reach and never plant.
- **A part whose template gives no role has it guessed** from where it sits (`PartRoles.guess`), stored with `roleGuessed`: the root is the body; on the body or a spine, a part whose rest points down (its downward share at least 0.7) is a leg, or an arm on the front half, a part leaving the rear end (u up to 0.15) back along the body a tail, one leaving the front end along it a head, and a sheet (`PartTemplate.isSheet`) on its top or sides (v more than 0.2 from 0.5) a fin; on a neck, one leaving its end is a head; on a head, a part on its top (v within 0.2 of 0) is an ear and one on the muzzle's underside (u from 0.6, v within 0.2 of 0.5) a jaw; on a leg's or an arm's end, a foot or a hand; on a tail's end, more tail. Anything else is other. A socket's place is where the Move tool has put it.
- **A beak's lower half shares the head's jaw.** A part of role jaw that holds no mouth, `beak-lower`, takes on its own `jaw` joint the degrees the nearest part it hangs from that holds a mouth gives its jaw: the shown expression's angle for that part's jaw, and the mouth's open run up to the lower beak's own limit (`shownJoints`). Its jaw sits at its base with no v, so it turns the whole part about its base, and the bird head's jaw and the lower beak's share their limits, so both turn by the same degrees.
- **Pose reaches with legs, arms and wings.** A part whose role is leg, arm or wing is a limb (`Role.LIMBS`), dragged by the dot at its third joint, the paw's or the wrist's; legs plant, and arms plant on a body that is not upright (`plants`, `Limb.plants`), so a wing, and a biped's arm with its hand, has a dot and no pin and no Planted row.

## Surface work on the mesh

Positions are never stored and never computed in composition. Graph nodes derive them, so `Settled` covers them, and everything that draws surface work reads them.

- **`CharacterSession.surfaceWork(part)`** gives one part's `ResolvedSurfaceWork`. It follows what the part resolves to, including its side's reading of a pair's anchors, and the surface of every part its anchors name, so a border that runs onto a parent follows that parent's redraw too. A surface edit reruns this node and no mesh; an outline or template change reaches it through the surface.
- **`CharacterSession.bodySurfaceWork`** gives every part's work in body space, in the document's order of parts, through the assembly's placement. A redraw of a parent moves a child's work here and leaves the child's own node alone.
- **`CharacterSession.heldColor(part)`** gives one part's color shapes cut to the surfaces they land on, following its `surfaceWork` and those surfaces at the current flattening step; **`CharacterSession.bodyColor`** gathers them into what lies on each part, in layer order. A shape that did not change keeps its cut, and a palette color is not an input. See [look.md](look.md), Color is vector.
- **`CharacterSession.bodyLines`** gives what lines read, with nothing about a camera in it: each part's patches in body space as grids of positions and face normals, weighed by the line weights that name it, its crease with its parent and its marks' weights. Grids and creases come from nodes that follow the assembly alone, so a surface edit reweighs them and finds neither again.
- **`CharacterSession.bodyFur`** gives every part's fur strokes in body space, each with the way the grain runs at its root, the slot under it and the part's radius there, camera aside. A comb or a rule reruns it and no mesh.
- **Everything anchored on the surface follows the pose.** Wherever a view shows the pose, what lies on the surface resolves on the posed grids. **`CharacterSession.posedBody`** is the assembly in the shown pose, which `SessionPose` hands the session (`showPose`): each part the pose moves shows its posed mesh, its patches' points and proxy normals read from it with their slices kept, so u and v land on the same rings, and is turned by its first bone (`PlacedPart.turn`); a part at rest is the same object, and with none moved it is the assembly itself. A pose is shown only on the assembly it was made for: each posed mesh names the rest mesh it was posed from (`PosedParts.rests`), and since a new assembly arrives before its rig and pose, a run of `posedBody` on an assembly whose pose was made for another waits for the next. **`posedSurfaceWork`**, **`posedLines`** and **`posedFur`** follow it: a part whose work names a part the pose moved resolves its anchors again on the posed surfaces, the same resolution over different grids; a part the pose moved, or whose parent it moved within reach of the part, finds its grids and its crease again on the posed body, so a crease moves with its socket and still lies where the part leaves its parent; a fur stroke on a moved part roots again with its grain read on the posed surface, keeping its room and slot, which are the body's own. Every other part keeps what it has at rest, and while the pose leaves every part at rest each of these is the rest node's own result. **`furView(view, posed)`** reads `posedFur` for a view that shows the pose.
- **`CharacterSession.furView(view)`** gives what fur shows through the camera `showFurFrom(view, camera)` last gave that view, as wedges and the meshes that fill them. See [look.md](look.md), Marks. An orbit reruns it every frame, so these nodes live in `viewGraph`, apart from `graph`, whose settled and progress say the character is being derived; a test reading a view waits on both.
- **The shape keeps the document's.** The base slot and area fills stay as they are. Marks with their camera bins' versions, regions and brush strokes keep their ids, names, slots, flags and draw order, with each anchor a `ResolvedPoint`: the anchor, its position and its normal. Groups stay the document's `MarkGroup`. Expressions a part shows are listed in the document's order by id and name, then by group and role, for the groups the part holds. Grain has no anchors and stays in the document.
- **A point lies in the space of the part its anchor names.** In a part's node that is the named part's surface space, mirrored on the right as its mesh is, with `PartSurface.shown`. In the body node it has moved by that part's origin. Placing is translation, so a normal is the same in both.
- **An anchor lands on the field, off along the normal there by its offset.** The offset is outward when positive, in the part's own units (`FieldAnchors`; see [surface.md](surface.md), Parts as coordinates). A u past a run's rings holds along the end ring's axis.
- **A patch with no slices reads its grid.** A pad's rings take evenly spaced u from 0 to 1, so its anchors land the same way.
- **A dangling anchor drops.** An anchor naming a part not in the document or a patch the part did not build is left out. An item left too short to be one drops with it: a mark or a mark version under two points, a region under three, a brush stroke with none. A group stops naming a mark that dropped, and keeps its name, roles and controls. The document keeps every anchor, so a part or patch that returns brings them back.

## The rig

A vertex of the character's surface takes each part's weights times that part's share of it ([surface.md](surface.md), The rig on the surface); the rules below give a part's own weights and the weights of a separate part's mesh. The rig is derived from the assembly by one graph node, `SessionRig.rig` in `rig`, which follows `CharacterSession.assembly`, so `Settled` covers it and a redraw, a moved joint or a new part rebuilds it. Nothing of it is stored. Only parts in the document are rigged; a planned part has no bones.

- **Every template joint is a bone,** a joint placed by hand too, named by part and joint (`back-leg-l/knee`, `leg/joint.1`), never by index, so a template version that adds a joint shifts nothing a pose names. A part's joints in u order each own the part from their u to the next joint's, the first also owning what lies below it; with two or more, the last owns nothing, since it is the part's end, and carries what sits there: a leg's ankle carries the paw. This is the rule `PartBuilder.mesh` tags vertices by, through `JointTree`, which the rig reads too.
- **Digits branch inside a part.** A toe row's `joints` give each digit it builds a chain of bones, `hand-l/fingers:1.2` (`JointTree.digits`). A digit's first bone hangs from the part's own bone that owns the u where the digit starts, the wrist on a hand, paw or bird foot, and each later one from the one before. The digit up to its first joint follows that bone, as the palm does; each digit joint owns the digit from its u to the next, the last to the digit's tip. Its pivot is the middle of the digit's ring at the joint, and it points to the next joint's or to the digit's tip. Its hinge is the joint's, read in the part's axes, so a positive tip turns the digit toward the row's underside; a digit turned back takes it reversed, so the same tip closes it toward the underside too and a bird's hind toe grips against the front toes. A stretch moves only the next joint of the same chain, so nothing on the part's own chain pulls one digit. Bones list the part's own chain first, then each digit's in row order (`Bone.digit`). Digits overlap the palm rather than share its vertices, so no palm vertex follows a digit, except on a `digits` row joined to its field, where every vertex rides the runs round it; see the block reference.
- **The sockets give the tree.** A part's first bone hangs from its parent's bone that carries the socket: the last joint at or below the socket's u, as the Move tool has placed it, or for a socket on a digit, a tip socket, the digit's joint owning the socket's u, its last. Each later bone hangs from the one before it. Bones are listed parents first.
- **Rest comes from the built parts.** A bone's pivot is the middle of its part's ring at the joint, in body space; it points to the next joint's pivot, or the ring at its part's end. Its hinge is a direction of the part's axes, and on the right side the mirrored direction turned the other way, so one angle poses a pair as mirror images. Limits and the stretch flag are the joint's.
- **Weights come from ownership.** A vertex follows the joint owning its u, a pad's at its site, a digit's vertex the joint of its own digit owning its u. Two bands blend, and nothing else does: across each joint inside a part, the two bones blend with a smooth step over the part's radius at the joint either side (`JOINT_REACH`), held to half the way to the joints around it, so a turn up to 90 degrees keeps the inner side from folding; across each digit joint the same, over the digit's own radius, the first blending the bone the digit branches from into the digit's first, held to half the way to its root, its other joints and its tip; and at each seam. A vertex follows at most four bones.
- **A seam blends both sides halfway.** Where a child meets its parent, the seam's radius is the child's widest ring up to u = 0.25, held to the parent's widest ring within 0.125 of the socket. On the child, the parent's bone takes half at u = 0 and fades out over one seam radius along the child (`SEAM_REACH`), held to half the way to its next joint. On the parent, the child's first bone takes half within one seam radius of the socket's point and fades out over one more. Several rims on one vertex share at most half.
- **Painted seams plug in at one place.** `SeamPaint` is asked, one part at a time for each seam that part is a side of, with every vertex's address, its place in body space and the share the band guessed there, and answers the shares to use. With no strokes it answers the guess. `StrokePaint` replays the seam strokes each part resolves to, stored on the seam's child, in the order laid down, on every rebuild: each acts at its strength in its middle fading smoothly to nothing at its radius from the line through its anchors (or from each anchor, for dabs); a paint stroke moves the share toward its side's bone, an erase hands it back toward the guess, and a smooth moves it toward the mean share within the radius of its nearest point. Where several painted rims meet on one vertex they share at most half, or the most one was painted to.
- **Reach widens a joint's blend.** A joint's reach (`rig.reach.<joint>`, 1 unset) scales its band: inside a part, the joint's half-width before it is held to the room around it; at a seam, the child's first joint's reach scales the seam's band along the child and its rim on the parent.
- **Tidy hands stray patches over.** A side claims a vertex when its bone holds more than half of it. On the child, a piece of the parent's claim that does not reach the socket end is stray, and so is every piece of the child's own claim but its largest that the parent's claim borders; on the parent, a piece of the child's claim that does not come within the seam's reach of the socket, and every piece of the rest but its largest that the child's claim borders. Pieces join across triangles and where two patches' vertices meet. `Rig.strayPatches` finds them; Tidy adds, as one `AddSeamStrokes`, a stroke of full-strength dabs on each patch's vertices for the bone on its border.
- **Seam commands.** `SetSeamStroke` adds, replaces or removes one stroke; `ResetSeam` clears a seam, the pair's and both sides' strokes or with `thisSide` one side's; `SeamToTwin` writes the strokes one side shows onto its twin, mirrored, as the twin's overrides; `AddSeamStrokes` adds several as one step; each undoes through `PutSeamStrokes`.
- **The pose is a document value.** `Character.pose` is the character's one working pose; when Shot arrives it moves under a shot key and its commands keep their shape. Bones are named by part and joint, the planted set by part. `SetBoneAngle`, `SetBoneStretch` and `ResetBone` change one bone, with `bothSides` its twin's too: the twin takes the same number, which its mirrored hinge turns the mirrored way, so the pair poses as mirror images. `SetBoneTurn` sets a bone's tip, turn and lean at once, `SetBoneMode` sets it free, limited or locked, and `SetKeepVolume` whether its stretch narrows the span, each with `bothSides` too; a twin's turn and lean take the same numbers about its mirrored axes, so the pair still poses as mirror images. A bone back at rest, limited and keeping volume drops out of the pose, and `ResetBone` keeps a bone's mode and volume rule. `SetPlanted` plants or frees a paw, `SetRiding` whether a lifted paw rides with the body or keeps its place, `ResetPose` rests every bone, moves the body back, frees every paw and looks ahead, and `SetPose` sets the whole pose, which is every pose command's undo. Each sets an absolute value, so a drag's previews in one `History.begin()` transaction are one undo step. Joint names are not checked against the templates, since `document` does not see them; a bone the rig lacks is ignored.
- **The display pose sits under the working pose.** `Character.displayPose`, saved with the character and set whole by `SetDisplayPose`, is the pose it is shown in as it is meant to be seen: a biped's arms brought down from the A-pose it is built in. What the session shows is the working pose laid over it (`Pose.over`, `Character.shownPose`): every bone the working pose names, then each bone of the display pose it does not, so `ResetPose` shows the display pose and a pose that bends an elbow keeps the display's lowered shoulder. A bone back at rest drops out of the working pose, so the working pose cannot hold a display bone at rest; a pose that needs the A-pose sets the display bone to the opposite angle. The Pose stage's tools solve from the working pose and do not see the display bones under it. The locked Side, Top and Front panes outside Pose show the rest pose, the A-pose, to draw on.
- **Hair hangs from the head alone and lies on the body.** A part of role hair takes no part of the body further up its line of joins than the part it sits on: a hair part built as its own mesh is carried by its own chain, its socket parent's bone fades in only over its root band, and it gives its parent no rim, so a scalp never follows the hair; a vertex of a hair run in the field weighs only that run and its socket parent's (`SurfaceSkin`), so a cap over the nape follows the head and not the neck or torso. In each pose each hair part built as its own mesh is pushed out of the body it lies against (`withHairPushed`, by `StrandPush` in `geom`): every vertex of its run finds the nearest point of the posed parts other than hair, the part it sits on and the other parts on that part, and needs to move out along that point's normal to stand `HAIR_CLEARANCE` (0.012) off it; each ring moves whole by the largest need among its columns, and the strand swings as a rod held at its root ring, each ring pushed carrying every ring past it and those between it and the root in proportion to their distance along it, so a head turned over a shoulder slides the fall over the shoulder rather than through it.
- **The body curve bends the spine and tail.** `solveBodyCurve(rig, pose, curve)`, in `rig`, gives the pose with the body chain laid along the curve's Side stroke and the curve kept on it; the tool that draws the stroke sends the result with `SetPose`, so the solve is a function, not a node. The chain is found by role: it runs from the end of the root part's unpaired child of role tail, through the root part's joints from that child's carrier to the carrier of the root part's last unpaired child of role spine, neck or head, into that child: on the fennec the tail's tip to the tail's root, the torso's hips to chest, and the head's neck. Each span is a chord from its joint to the next. The stroke is scaled so these chords, laid along it from its start, end at its end, then each bone from the root part's first span outward turns about its hinge, within its limits, until its next joint lies on the stroke; the part ahead lies along the stroke's end tangent, turned from it as the part ahead turns at rest from the root part's rest axis (`RigPart.axis`): not at all on the fennec, whose head continues its spine, and a quarter turn on the biped, whose upright torso carries a head facing forward, so the face keeps facing forward as the spine bends. On the biped the chain runs from the torso's hips to its chest and into the head's neck. The root bone keeps its place, so the stroke is carried to meet it (`BodyCurveFit.scale` and `offset` say where it lies). The length factor is a stretch on every joint of the spine and tail, each span lengthening by it and narrowing by its square root; withers, the torso's one `stretch` joint, cannot carry it alone, since the head sits across the chest's span. The head keeps its size. Legs, the jaw and the ears keep their pose, the ears riding with the head; `BodyCurveFit.paws` gives each planted paw's place before and after for the limbs' pin solve. Solving the same curve after the character changes lays the new chain along the same stroke. The Top stroke then leans the chain the same way seen from above (`BodyCurveFit.top` says where it lies): it is scaled so the chain's steps as seen from above, laid along it as chords, end at its end, and carried so the root part's first step starts on it, and from the root part outward each bone leans across its hinge until its step ends on the stroke, the part ahead along its end, each lean held to the reach the bone's limits give a lean (the tip's nearer limit either way, none on a one-way joint) and a locked bone keeping its own; since leaning changes how long a step looks from above, the scaling, the carry and the walk run five times. The Side bend is kept: the lean composes with each bone's tip, which the Top solve never changes, and with only a Top stroke the chain keeps the tip it had. A stroke may be drawn either way; the solve runs it from the part behind to the part ahead. `PoseSolver.bendTo` chains the solve with `holdPaws`, each planted paw carried back by its leg to where and as it stood (`PawShift.before`, the joint the paw hangs from), a paw not planted riding along; a paw no leg can reach stays as near as it gets and the report says how far. An upright body turns on its planted hips (`onPlantedHips`): the bent pose is carried by the offset so the middle of the planted legs' hips on the root bone stands where it stood, as a pelvis turns on its legs, since its root bone's pivot lies below the hips and a turn about it would lift them past the reach of legs standing straight. A body on all fours keeps the root bone's place, its bent legs taking up the hips' swing. The tool edits the curve and nothing else: a stroke replaces its view's, a handle moves an end or bows the middle, Length changes the factor, and each change solves the whole curve again from the pose as it stands. `PoseSolver.clearCurve` removes the curve, rests every bone of the chain and every joint its length stretches, and holds the planted paws again from their legs' rest, an upright body again on its planted hips.
- **Skinning is linear blend.** `Rig.posed` takes a `Pose` and gives every rigged part's vertices in body space. A bone turns by its angles, held to its limits unless the pose frees it, as its parent has turned it, through its pivot as its parent has carried it: twist about its length first, then tip about the hinge, then lean about the axis across the hinge and the bone's length, then turn about its length as it rests; a mirrored bone takes the last two axes the other way. So a turn at a bent joint swings the bend about the bone's rest line as well as rolling it, while a twist rolls the bone where its bend points, as a forearm turns the palm; a twist is held as a turn is. A root's pivot moves by the pose's offset. A joint's tip is held to its template limits; its turn and lean either way as far as the tip's nearer limit, and not at all for a joint whose limits bend it one way only, as elbows and knees do. A bone that carries on its part's chain from the bone before it (an elbow, a knee, not a part's first bone, which may carry a rigid part) spreads the twist its turn and twist make about its own length along its span: its rotation is split into a swing and a twist about its rest length, the twist taken first (the quaternion's part along that axis), and each vertex skinned by it takes the share of the twist its place along the bone's span gives, none at the joint and all past its far end (`BoneTwist`). So a band across the joint blends the bend alone and the limb keeps its section, where blending a whole twist would pinch it, and what hangs past the bone, as a hand, takes the whole turn. An upright torso turns about the vertical the same way: a turn of its root bone turns the body whole with the legs on it, as hips turn in a walk, and a turn at a spine joint spreads up its span, so the chest turns back against the hips with the arms on it. A stretch on a joint scales its distance from the joint before it on the part: the bone owning that span lengthens along it by the factor and, while the joint keeps volume, narrows across it by its square root, so the span keeps its volume, and the stretched joint and all past it ride along. A template's `stretch` marks the joints a stretch handle offers; the body curve's length stretches every joint of the spine and tail.
- **Curl closes every digit at once.** `Rig.curled` tips every digit joint of the parts given to a share of its farther limit, 0 straight to 1 at the limit, so a digit whose limits reach further negative closes that way, keeping each joint's turn, lean and stretch and a locked joint's tip; `Rig.curlOf` reads the mean share back. The Pose stage shows it on a limb whose end part has digits, or whose own part has, as a membrane wing does, and sends it as `SetPose`, so nothing of it is stored but the tips.
- **Poses are solved.** `PoseSolver`, in `rig`, makes a pose from a handle and a target, always from the pose it is given, so a drag solves from where it started at every step and sends the result as `SetPose`. A locked bone is left out of every solve. A limb reaches by two bones: the knee tips about its hinge until the end stands as far from the hip as the target, taking the bend its limits allow nearest its own, so a knee folds the way the template says, then the hip turns the shortest way to carry the end onto the target, a few rounds so each holds to the limits the other met; the paw keeps its turn in body space. A planted paw held where it stood, by a body move, a look or `holdPaws`, keeps its leg's end bone's turn in body space as well as its own, so the paw, which hangs from the end bone sunk above its joint, stands as it stood, while the end joint's limits allow. With stretch on, a target past the straight limb's reach lengthens the lower bone by its end joint's stretch, where the template offers one, up to 1.5 times. A pole target swings the limb about the line from hip to end, the end and the paw staying put. Moving the body sets the offset and solves each planted paw, and each lifted paw kept still, back to where it stood; firm stops the body at the largest share of the move every such paw can follow, otherwise a paw past its leg's reach slides along the ground toward under the hip by as little as lets the leg reach it, and the report gives how far each slid. A tail curls by one tip added to each of its joints, the same share each, chosen to bring its tip nearest the target. A look turns the neck by `NECK_SHARE` (three tenths) of the turn toward the target, the neck being the bones of any part between the root and the head or else the bone the head hangs from, and the head the rest, each within its limits, then brings every planted paw back, and keeps the target as `lookAt`. `holdPaws` is the pin solve on its own: a pose, a target per paw and the pose the paws stood in, each leg carrying its paw back, for any solve that moves bones above planted paws, such as the body curve's.
- **The posed character is a node.** `SessionPose.posed`, in `rig`, gathers `SessionRig.rig` and the character's pose and gives each rigged part's posed vertices in body space, the mesh the renderer draws, in the part's own space with its normals and proxy normals turned by the blend of the bones' cofactor matrices and made unit again, how its first bone turns, and the rest mesh each was posed from. A part the pose leaves where it rests keeps its rest mesh itself, so a pose at rest draws exactly the built meshes. The skinning runs on the CPU in this node; the renderer takes the posed meshes like any other. It is the pose the session shows, so `CharacterSession.posedBody` and what follows it read it; see Surface work on the mesh.
- **The face pose sits under the rig's pose.** An expression's face pose and a mouth's open bend the head's own surface at the jaw (`shownJoints`), so the rig is built from the head as the face holds it and the pose turns that head; the jaw owns no span of the rig, so no rig pose bends it.
- **Hair locks follow their own chains.** Each lock of a lock group (parts.md, Hair locks) is a chain of its own bones, `locks/<group>/lock-<index>-<k>` from 1 at its root, along its guide at rest, the first hanging from the head's bone that weighs most the head's vertex nearest the lock's root (`lockRigs`). A vertex of the lock follows the bone whose span holds its u, shared with the bone before it over the first half of the span, so the root follows the head. A group's band, and a parting's strip of scalp, ride that head bone whole. The rig keeps each chain (`RigChain`: its bones root to tip, the bone it hangs from, its physics and the chains either side of it in its group) for a solver; nothing drives them yet, so they stand at rest and follow the head.

## Garments

Clothing is drawn on each character from the body it covers, with no garment templates. `Character.wardrobe` holds the garments, the wardrobe slots (`GarmentSlot`, named by the user and ordered; a palette slot is a `SlotName` and never meets one), the outfits and `shown`, the outfit Build shows, or none for the bare body.

- **A garment is branches.** Each covered part gives a garment one `GarmentBranch`: the part, the u and v range the box of its painted cells takes (past 1 it continues past the part's end, below 0 before its start, where the cells reach that end), an ease, its own Side, Top and Front outlines and its surface work. How far it stands off is the garment's: `Garment.fit`, snug 0.006 for a new garment, or a part's own in `partFits`, keyed by a pair's leader, each 0 or more; a garment with no fit stands off by each branch's ease, 0.012 where a branch sets none (`Garment.easeOf`, read into each branch by `GarmentCover.of`). A part's stand-off can vary along and round it: `Garment.eases`, keyed by a pair's leader, holds an `EaseProfile` over that part, its `EaseKey`s in order along u, each an ease at the front and, where it differs, at the back, read as one monotone cubic through them, smooth and never past a neighbouring key, flat past the first and last key, the sides taking the mean of front and back (`Garment.profileOf`, else the fit everywhere). `Garment.hang` (`ClothHang`: a limit in body units and a flare, 0 to 0.5) lets the cloth over each upright part fall from the highest place it covers rather than follow the body in; `Garment.drops`, by a pair's leader, 0 to 0.5, moves the seam where a part's cloth meets the cloth of the part it grows from down that part, as a dropped shoulder; a `GarmentArea.gather` is the ease that area pulls the cloth in to, as a ribbed cuff or hem; a `GarmentArea.fold`, 0 to 0.03, makes the area cloth turned back over the garment at its hem, as a rolled cuff, a rolled jeans hem or a turtleneck's fold-over, standing out by that thickness with its far edge inked and its colour the cloth's back face (see surface.md, Garments on the surface). `SetPartEase`, `SetGarmentHang`, `SetGarmentDrop`, `SetAreaGather` and `SetAreaFold` set them, from Dress's Ease along, Hang, Flare, Drop, Gather and Fold rows. An opening (`Opening`, in `character`'s shape package: a round or V cut, how far past a part's root ring it reaches and how deep its front drops) is a set of cells, not a field of the garment: the cells round where a part comes out, read on the body at rest (`ChartPlaces.opening`), which a recipe's `opening` stroke and the Round and V buttons of Dress's hole rows (`openingCommand`) unpaint or paint as any stroke's cells. A recipe with a negative fit, part fit or ease stops at that path with an error. `Garment.inkWeight` is the weight of its silhouette and inked edges as a share of the skin's line weight, 0 to 2, 0.7 where it sets none, so cloth reads thinner than skin; `Garment.hemRoll`, 0 to 0.03 body units, rolls its hems, neck openings and cuffs out past the cloth (see surface.md, Garments on the surface). `SetGarmentInkWeight` and `SetGarmentHemRoll` set them, from Dress's Ink weight and Hem roll rows. `Garment.creases`, `wrinkles` and `drape`, 0 to 2, 1 where it sets none, are how strongly it creases at joints, wrinkles where it bunches and hangs in folds, times the character's cloth creases (look.md, Creases and wrinkles); `SetGarmentCrease` sets each, from Dress's Creases, Wrinkles and Drape rows, and `SetCharacterCreases` the character's, from the Color drawer's Skin creases and Cloth creases rows under Shading. A branch on a paired part covers both sides, the right one mirrored, as a pair's data is shared. A branch is built, rigged and anchored as the part id `branchPartId(garment, part)`, `garment/<garment>/<part>`, which no part of the document takes.
- **A garment is painted on the body.** `BodyChart` lays the body's parts out as grids of 64 by 32 cells over their first runs' (u, v), joined where a part's root row meets the cell of its parent it sits on, a pair's other side read as its leader. A garment's `areas` (`GarmentArea`: an id, a name, blank until named, the cells painted, by part, as runs row by row, the first run not painted, and a palette slot of its own or none, which shows the garment's colour, and an opacity and a net that make it see-through, look.md, See-through cloth) are masks on that chart (`SurfaceMask`); areas of one garment never share a cell and may touch. Its `holes` (`GarmentHole`: a part, a pair's leader, and whether it has a cuff) let a part out at its root. The cloth is every area's cells but those of each holed part and every part beyond it (`PaintedGarment`). Paint lays a stroke into the picked area, taking its cells from every other area, or with Unpaint takes them from every area; a brush stroke is the cells whose middle at rest lies within the brush's size of a place the drag passed (`ChartPlaces.within`, either side of a pair), kept off the parts above the one it starts on (its parent and theirs) until a dab is centred on one with no cell of the start part within the brush's size (`BrushReach`), a line the same along a straight line on screen, and an ellipse, box or lasso the cells whose middle shows inside the outline, front and back alike (`strokeOf`). Mirror lays it on both sides of the middle parts too (`SurfaceMask.mirrored`), and Clean edges smooths the area's edge near the stroke, each cell taking what most of it and its neighbours are, up to four rounds. Grow and Shrink move an area's whole edge a cell out or in. An area's edges are found as each closed run of cell sides between its cells and the rest on one part, the side of a part's root ring against its parent's cell counted (`edgesOf`), each with its cells on its left, then moved onto the curve the cloth is cut on (`onLevel`, `areaLevel`: the part's cloth field less its hem level, and where areas meet the area's own field less the most of the others'), the points on a root ring kept there; an edge along another area's cells is a seam. So Dress shows a hem as the smooth curve the garment is cut on, not the cells' steps. Each edge is fitted with as few joined cubics as stay within half a cell of it, at least four round a closed edge (`HemHandles`, `fitFewCubics`); their joins are Edit curves' handles. Dragging a handle bends the curve through it, the cubic handles either side moving with it, and repaints the cells either side of where the curve moved (`repainted`: the cells the area's edges cut differently with the curve before and after the move, by `redrawn`, take what the moved curve gives, every other cell stays); Smooth eases points and repaints the same way, and an erased edge's far side joins its area (`withoutEdge`). The painted cells an opening will cut, those of a parent within the root ring of each part the cloth leaves out and the garment does not hide on a socket inside the region its parent's cells paint, show as dots in the warn colour where they lie (`cutCells`). Straighten makes an edge a level ring round its part at its mean u. `shapeCommands` writes the cloth into the garment as one step with `SetGarmentPaint`: a branch over each part the cloth reaches, its u and v range the box of cells the cloth holds of the part (`ChartBox`, half a cell onto the walls beside them; `SetBranchRange`, `SetBranchAround`), which records their bounds and paints nothing, a range running past an end kept while the cells reach that end, a `Through` on each part on a socket inside the region the cloth's cells paint (`ClothReach.holds`) that the cloth leaves out, with a cuff where its hole has one, and an inked join (`SetJoinInk`) on each part whose root cells lie in one area and its parent's cell in another, so a seam between areas on a part and the part it sits on is inked with the hems. A change to a part the cloth says nothing of, before or after, is kept, as a hood's to the hair under it; one with no cloth covers nothing.
- **Every garment's cloth is its cells.** A branch over a part no area paints has its box painted into the garment's first area, `area-1`, made where it has none (`Garment.withBoxesPainted`): the cells whose middles lie inside its range along the part, cut to the part's ends, and round it, at least two rows and two columns (`boxCells`, `LEAST_BOX_CELLS`), since one row alone blurs below the cloth's hem level; the branch keeps its range, within half a cell of its cells' edges. `AddGarment`, `CoverPart` and `SetWardrobe` paint this way, so a recipe's covers and a script's boxes become cells as they arrive, and a document saved in format 18 or before is painted on load (format 19), dropping the edges and fill a garment saved before areas held. A painted part's branch is left as painted. The chart's size, the cells' run-length form and `ChartBox` are the document's (`GarmentChart.kt`), since saved areas are written in them. The chart, its places and the masks are in `character`'s `shape` package and the shapes on screen in `screens-core`'s `surface` package, which know nothing of garments. The painted garment, its hem handles, `shapeCommands`, the Tuck items and the paint, grow and area commands are in `character`'s `dress` package, so a recipe paints a garment as Dress does.
- **A garment's strokes can be kept as shapes.** `Garment.shapes` holds strokes of its areas kept as vector shapes in the order laid (`ClothShape`: a part, a pair's leader, the area whose stroke it is, whether it unpaints, and its `ClothOutline`: a box, the ellipse in a box, or a closed path through few points, straight or one smooth curve turning sharply at its corners), as a recipe's exact strokes lay them. Each also lays cells (`drawnStrokeCells`): painting, every cell whose middle lies within half a cell of it, so a shape narrower than a cell still holds a cell across it; unpainting, every cell more than half a cell inside it; a box reaching within half a row of an end of its part runs on over that end. The cells decide everything the cells decide for any garment (its branches, reach, holes, cuffs, tucks and mesh); a part its shapes lie on takes its cloth's edge from them wherever its cells are what they lay, and from the cells near cells painted otherwise (surface.md, Garments on the surface). `SetGarmentPaint` keeps the shapes of every area it keeps, so repainting in Dress gives cells' edges only where it paints. Export writes them back as exact strokes on a part whose cells are just what they lay, and as cells elsewhere.
- **Small parts inside a garment tuck.** A small part the cloth holds whole (an ear, tail, horn, spike, claw or fin, by role and template) and the toes and claws at the end of a foot it reaches are Tucked, smooth cloth over them, or Shaped, the cloth following them: by the garment's `tucks`, else toes, claws, tails, horns and spikes tucked and the rest shaped. A tucked part is hidden and has no branch; tucked toes or claws are a `Redraw` setting the foot's `toes` or `claws` parameter to 0. A hand's fingers, on a template that fuses them (`merge` and `spread`), are shaped by default, each in a glove finger of its own over that digit (surface.md, Garments on the surface), and tucked are a mitten: a `Redraw` fusing them to the tips and folding them together (`merge` 2, `spread` 0.15), the thumb left free; on any other hand they stay shaped. Shaped toes and a hoof's digits are covered digit by digit the same way. The Tuck rows show the items of the garment as its painted cloth holds them, and with a part selected only those on it, beyond it, or on the part it hangs from (`shownTucks`).
- **Outfits pick per slot.** A garment sits in at most one slot; an outfit picks at most one garment per slot, and only one sitting in it, so garments mix and match. The garments worn are the shown outfit's picks, innermost layer first (`Wardrobe.worn`). A garment's layer is its own, so its order holds in every outfit.
- **A worn garment changes the parts under it.** `Garment.changes` holds, by part, `Hide`, `Through` (a cuff, its size, its ink), `Redraw` (a `PartData` laid over the part's own on its own template) or `Swap` (another template and its data). A change named on either side of a pair holds for both, its anchors read for each side as a pair's are. `Character.wornPart` resolves a part: its pair's values, its side's overrides, then each worn garment's change to it, innermost first, each replacing what came before, so the outermost garment changing a part wins and taking a garment off gives the part back exactly.
- **Commands.** `AddGarment`, `RemoveGarment` (with every outfit's pick of it and every part it owns, in one step), `SetGarmentSlot` (dropping every pick of it), `SetLayers` (an order, innermost first), `AddGarmentSlot`, `RemoveGarmentSlot` (its garments left in no slot), `RenameGarmentSlot`, `AddOutfit`, `RemoveOutfit` (the bare body shows if it was shown), `RenameOutfit`, `PickGarment` and `ShowOutfit` change the wardrobe and undo through `SetWardrobe`; `RenameGarment`, `CoverPart`, `UncoverPart`, `SetBranchRange`, `SetBranchAround`, `SetBranchEase`, `SetBranchOutline`, `SetBranchSurface`, `SetJoinInk`, `SetHemInk`, `SetGarmentFit`, `SetPartFit`, `SetPartEase`, `SetGarmentHang`, `SetGarmentDrop`, `SetAreaGather`, `SetAreaFold`, `SetAreaOpacity`, `SetAreaNet`, `SetGarmentPaint`, `RenameGarmentArea`, `SetGarmentAreaColor`, `SetGarmentRegion`, `SetGarmentMark`, `SetTuck`, `SetGarmentColor`, `SetBodyPartChange`, `SetLooseEnd` and `RemoveLooseEnd` change one garment and undo through `SetGarment`. Showing another outfit is one command and one undo step.
- **A garment can own parts.** Rigid things a garment carries, such as the frames and temples of shades, a heel, a button, a buckle, a lapel or a hat brim, are ordinary parts in `Character.parts` with `PartInstance.garment` naming the garment that owns them: generic blobs, tubes or sheets on hand-made sockets of the body part they sit on, or of another part the same garment owns, drawn, coloured and anchored like any part, so every command, node and tool that keys on a part reads them unchanged. `Character` holds that an owned part's garment is in the wardrobe and that only a part the same garment owns sits on an owned part, so taking the garment off never strands a body part; `RemoveGarment` removes its parts with it. `assemblyParts` leaves out a part whose garment is not worn, with every part on it (`hiddenUnderGarments`, `Character.isWorn`); its nodes stay cached, so showing the outfit again is cheap. A placed owned part carries its garment as `PlacedPart.owner`. It reads its socket on its parent as the parent shows, not on the parent's skeleton, since it belongs to the shape the garment gives (a heel on the shoe a Swap makes of the foot); a socket's `sink` of 0 seats it on the surface. It stands outside every garment: no branch cuts a hole or a cuff for it and nothing hides any of it. A body part that passes through it overlaps it, as parts overlap at sockets. How it moves is its binding, `PartInstance.bind` (`PartBind`) and `PartInstance.stiff`, which both sides of a pair share (`SetPartBind`; Bind and Stiff in the Dress inspector's section for each part the picked garment owns; a recipe part's `bind` and `stiff`). Bound `single`, the rig gives it its template's root bone, which follows its parent's bone at the socket and which no example poses, so it moves rigidly with the bone it sits on, in every pose and in the display pose. Bound `follow`, each vertex takes its bones from the body under it (`boundOwned`): the weights the body parts' own vertices carry within one and a half mean edge lengths of the part it sits on past the nearest one, eased by distance (`BodySurface`) and then eased along the part's own mesh over four passes (`BodyWeights`), so neighbouring vertices never take weights from two overlapping body parts apart, read on the body part it sits on (past any owned part between), that part's parent and the body parts on it that are neither hair nor hard, so a boot shaft, a knee band or a shell round a limb bends with the joint it crosses as the skin under it does. `stiff`, 0 to 1, raises each vertex's weights to the power 1 / (1 - stiff) and shares them out again, gathering the bend toward the joint as a stiff shaft does; from 0.95 each vertex keeps its heaviest bone alone. Left unset, the binding is the rule's: a part follows when it goes round the limb under it, the directions of its vertices across the nearest body bone, averaged, shorter than half, and crosses a joint there, two bones each holding 0.6 or more of at least three in a hundred of its vertices; every other owned part rides one bone, so a heel, button, buckle, lapel, pendant or pair of shades, which lies to one side of the bones under it, stays rigid. A part that follows keeps how far each of its vertices stands off the body at rest (`BodyHold`), read on the body's nearest point, on its triangles round the nearest body vertex. In each pose, after skinning, its own bone's turn on the bone it hangs from carries it about that bone's posed pivot, so posing the part's own bone still moves it over the skin; then each vertex the pose has brought closer to the body than three quarters of its standoff is pushed out along the posed body's normal, read the same way on whichever body it is now near, so a thigh closing on a boot's top in a deep bend pushes it as the shin under it does, the push eased over the part's triangles so it moves as one piece, each vertex still pushed at least as far as it needs, twice over, each push reading the body again where the last left it (`heldOnBody`). Its weights are four bones a vertex, as every part's are, so they go with the rig and any pose moves it; the binding is worked out once a rig, and a pose only skins, turns and pushes. A pair of owned parts mirrors as any pair does. The Parts tree lists an owned part under the part it sits on, as any part, whether its garment is worn or not.
- **A branch is the body's slices grown.** `ShellBuilder.shell`, in `geom`, lofts a branch over its part's first swept patch: `BRANCH_RINGS` rings evenly spaced over the branch's u range, each the part's slice there (between its two neighbours) grown by the ease in height and width, at the part's segment count, so a branch's v runs as its part's does and its topology is the garment's alone. A drawn Side outline sets a ring's height and moves its middle up or down where it stands off further than the ease, a drawn Top outline its width, both read like a part's outlines in the part's own axes; where a drawing hugs the body the ease holds. Past either end of its part a branch carries on along the end slice's frame at the size of the ring nearest that end that is not a pole, unless the part is built by a `field` and the end is a pole the branch runs past while staying within u 0 to 1, as a blended foot's round toe short of u 1 is: then it closes on the pole, its rings drawing closer together toward it as a sphere's latitudes do, so a shoe over a round toe follows the toe and closes over it. A pole of the part stays a pole. Otherwise the branch is open at both ends.
- **A branch can cover part of the way round.** A branch's `fromV` to `toV`, read round its part's own v, is the whole way round when it spans 1 or more; under 1 the branch is open round, its columns evenly spaced over the span and as many as the part's over it plus one, and its open edges are hems, as a crown cap or the back of an open-front jacket. A `Covering` keeps the part's column under each branch column, which weights read.
- **On a one surface a branch drawn or run past its part reads a shell.** A branch with a drawing, or running more than 0.08 in u past either end of its part (`drawsShell`), reads a shell built the whole way round its part, past each end of its range by that 0.08, whose solid joins the garment's field; a branch part of the way round is cut from it by its v range in the coverage, as its grown run is, so a cape over the back flares and trails past the rump and a bun open over the back is drawn rounder and longer than the body. A Front outline is every shell ring's section, drawn in -1 to 1 as a part's is, so a sausage over an egg-sectioned body is round. Where the branch's own range runs past an end of its part that does not point down at rest (`endPointsDown`, more than 30 degrees below level), no body lies under a ring to hold it open: it takes the drawn size exactly, smaller than the ease too, and a ring past every end of the drawing closes to a point, so a drawn end past a level body's rump rounds off. Past an end that points down, as below an upright torso, a ring keeps at least the ease.
- **Cloth hangs past an end that points down.** On a one surface a branch the whole way round hangs free (`WornCover.hangsFree`), and flares as a cone hinged at its top in each pose (`hemFlare`, rings read across world down), only where it runs past an end of its part that points down at rest; past the rump of a body lying level it runs on. A garment's branch hangs over what comes out of its part (`hangsOver`) when it hangs free, or when it is part of the way round with a shell of its own, as a cape or a bun: in each pose it is pushed out of the parts on sockets inside its range that it does not cover, legs and a tail, along their normals until it clears them by 1.5 of its ease (`hangingPushed`).
- **A garment's own shapes are painted on it.** On a one surface each branch's regions and brush strokes, anchored in the (u, v) of its part's first run, are painted on the garment's surface over its colour, over each area in a palette slot of its own, which is painted where its blurred cells stand above every other area's and the hem level (`AreaShare`, a `ColorShape.Level`), so its colour meets the next area's along the inked seam (`garmentShapes`, `GarmentPaints`), kept to what of it is drawn, and posed and hidden with it, so a squiggle of mustard is a stroke on a sausage. A branch's marks are not drawn.
- **A garment prints on its cloth.** `Garment.regions` and `Garment.marks` are the body's own `Region` and `Mark` (lines only), their anchors on the body parts under the cloth, in the (u, v) of each part's first run, which the garment's vertices carry, a head's face places turned into those by the recipe: a chest symbol, a stripe, a pocket's outline, stitching. A `mirrored` print shows again on the other side of the body, across a part in the middle or on a pair's other side (`Character.mirrorOf`; a region's `mirrored` holds on a body part in the middle too). `GarmentCover.of` reads them, with their other sides, as its `prints` (`GarmentPrints`). A region is a shape on each part its border lies on, painted over the areas' colours; its inked border and each inked mark are lines, laid every half cell along their anchors on one part and, for a border on one part, along the curve its fill follows (`UvShapeDistance.edge`), resolved on the garment's own field (`FieldAnchors`), so they lie on the cloth at its fit; each line is cut to the places where its part's cloth stands over the hem level (`clothCut`), so a print shows only on painted cloth and ends where the cloth does. Posed, the lines are carried with the garment's vertices (`SurfaceGarment.movedTo`), and they go with it when it is not worn. They draw at the marks' weight in their slot's colour, the ink's where none. What a garment prints and its areas' colours are left out of what its grown surface and its cut are kept by (`GarmentCover.unprinted`), so changing a print paints the kept garment again (`SurfaceGarment.reprinted`) and builds nothing. In Dress, Print region and Print line draw on the picked garment's cloth as the Color stage's Region and the Lines stage's Stroke draw on the skin: a press finds the cloth in front of the body (`garmentHit`, on the garment's grown surface where its cloth is) and anchors by the body's part, u and v there; Prints lists them, each with its cross, and an area's row holds a swatch for its colour.
- **Holes and cuffs come from the socket tree.** A body part on a socket inside the region a branch's cells paint (`clothBranch`: along the rows the cells take, from 0 or to the branch's own end where they reach an end, round from the edge of their first column to their last; a painted garment and the same cells given as boxes decide alike), which the garment neither hides nor covers with a branch of its own and which does not hang inside it (see A branch can hang free), comes out through it: the branch's faces inside the part are left out, the branch leaves the part whole, and unless the garment's `Through` change for it turns the cuff off, a cuff is built over the part, `cuffPartId(garment, part)`, from its root to past the u where its first ring's middle leaves the branch by `cuffSize` of that, and by at least one and a half of the part's radii there (`CUFF_BEND_RADII`), so a thick part such as a tail bends at its root inside its cuff, at the branch's ease and `CUFF_RINGS` rings, weighted by the part it rings and coloured as the garment.
- **A branch can hang free.** A branch the whole way round that runs more than `HANG_PAST` (0.1 in u) before its part's start or past its end, not both, hangs free there and is held over its part at the other end (`Covering.hangsFree`), as a skirt hangs from the waist below the torso. A part on a socket inside its region that leaves it by that free end rather than through its wall, its first ring outside the branch lying past 0.85 of the way from the held end to the free end, hangs inside it (`leavesByFreeEnd`, `Covering.hangsOver`): it is covered as any skin under the branch is, the strip joining it hidden with it, and gets no hole or cuff.
- **Layers fit and hide.** The worn garments are built innermost first. A branch stands off the layer below rather than the skin: each inner garment's branch the whole way round the same part, over any of the same span, adds its ease to the branch's. An inner garment's hems an outer one hides are cut away with it, and the outer one's own hems are lifted off it as off skin. Every face of a layer that an outer layer covers, by the test that hides skin, is left out of its mesh, at rest and in every pose. The fit holds at rest.
- **Worn parts are what builds.** The slice build reads each part's template and data from `wornPart`, and builds nothing for a hidden part; `assemblyParts` places each part on the template it wears and leaves out a hidden part with every part on it; surface work, colour inputs and fur read the worn data. The rig follows the assembly, so a hidden part has no bones while the garment is worn.
- **Garments are placed parts.** `CharacterSession.assembly` ends in `dressedNode`, which follows the assembly and `dressingOf(character)`, the garments worn and each part's twin. Each branch is a `PlacedPart` with the branch's id, its part's template, side, origin and turn, its surface built from the branch alone, and a `Covering` naming the garment, the part, the part's patch it was built over and the ring of it under each branch ring. Lines read branches as parts. Their colour is the garment's slot, else the branch's own base slot (`garmentColors`, laid over the body's colour by `dressedColorNode`). Surface tools pick through garments to the body.
- **Covered skin is hidden.** A see-through garment hides nothing. What a branch covers (`GarmentCover`): for a branch the whole way round, what lies inside it out of sight of its open ends (`ShellInside.covers`): between two of its rings along their axis, inside the nearer ring's polygon, and no nearer to an open end, along the branch, than to its wall, so skin seen through a hem or a neck opening stays; for a branch over part of the way round, its own part's surface over its u and v range. `ShellInside` reads each ring from the shell's grid as it stands, so the same test reads a posed shell. Every body triangle whose corners are all covered is left out of its part's mesh; the grid and every vertex stay, so anchors still land. A cuff covers the skin under it as a branch does. A part that comes out through a branch is not hidden by that branch, since the branch's hole leaves it open there. A fur stroke rooted where a branch or cuff covers is kept apart (`PartFur.covered`) and not drawn. A line grid marks the points no kept face has as a corner, so no contour runs along hidden skin and a crease is cut where it crosses it.
- **What is hidden is decided again in every pose.** Each part hidden under a garment keeps what hides it (`Hiding`): its mesh's faces with none left out, the vertices a branch over part of the way round hides by u and v, the shells that hide what they cover of it, and the parts whose whole inside cuts a hole in it. `CharacterSession.posedBody` ends in `hiddenAgain`: a part the pose carries, or one whose hiders it carries, is tested again against those shells as they stand in the pose, a part not carried only at its vertices near where a carried hider lies now or lay at rest; a part with the same faces shown stays the same object. So skin and inner layers a pose carries out of every garment show again, what it carries into one is hidden, and a hole follows the part through it. `posedFur` sorts the strokes of each part the pose changes again by the garments as they stand, so a stroke carried out of a garment shows. A part shown more or less of but not carried keeps its surface work and grids, and finds its lines again.
- **Weights from the body.** The rig builds no bones for a garment. Nothing moves a garment after skinning but the push of a branch that hangs free: on the inside of a deep bend a branch keeps about half its rest offset from the body or more and goes on covering what it covered, so garments drawn to overlap stay overlapped in every pose.
- **Loose ends** are kept on the garment: a branch, a place round its hem, a bone count, Follow-through, Spring and Collide body. Each is built as a flat strip with closed ends hung from that place on the branch's far ring, down and a little out from the body, a fixed share of its covered part's length per bone and a fixed share of the hem's way round wide, as its own placed part `garment/<garment>/end/<id>`. The rig gives it a chain of its bones, `end-1` on, the first hanging from the body bone that moves the branch most there, each hinged across the strip; each strip vertex follows the bone whose span holds it, shared with the one before over the first half of the span. The pose holds its bones by that part id like any part's.

## How a pane looks at a part

A locked pane looks straight along the normal of its view's plane, so an outline's plane is never foreshortened and the map from plane to pane is a rotation and a scale. Rings stand on planes a = constant, so a Side or Top outline drawn there is the mesh's silhouette on screen. `PartAxes.framing` says which pane holds which view, the arrangement that shows every view's plane the most squarely; `PartAxes.basis` gives the pane's axes.

- **A label names the direction, not the outline.** A Sheet pane's label, and Single's Snap view, HUD and thumbnails, name the direction looked from, so Top is always the top-left pane. The inspector's outline rows and the status bar name outlines, so a leg's or an ear's Top pane writes its Front outline, and its Front pane its Top outline.
- **The camera tilts with the part.** Each pane looks along the normal of its view's plane, from the side of its plain body view, with that body view's up made as up as the plane lets it be. A part square to the body gets the plain body views exactly. A tail 37 degrees above horizontal runs diagonally in Side, whose plane is the body's, runs straight across in Top, seen from 37 degrees off vertical, and shows Front from below the ground, looking up its axis. An ear 16 degrees off vertical stands straight in Side, leans in Top, whose plane is the body's front, and shows Front from 16 degrees off vertical.
- **Everything goes through the one camera.** The selection, the ghosts, their depth order, the template hint, the outlines, a tool's strokes and handles all do. Silhouettes are derived along the part's own axes, so a hint is the silhouette of the view's own camera. The ground is the disc under the character as the camera's tilt shows it, never flatter than the thin ellipse a level camera has, and is always drawn behind. The pane looking along the body's up has none.
- **A shortened leg's outlines stay in stored coordinates.** A locked camera takes the factor its part was built at, see Legs stay level as they lean, and multiplies an outline's x by it on Side and Top. Front has no length along the axis and takes none. Every outline, handle, stroke and pointer position crossing to a pane passes through that one map, so the outline lies on the mesh, a stroke builds under the pointer, and what is stored is the leg's length before the factor. Comparing views compares stored curves, so it is unchanged by the factor; what those comparisons and the guides draw takes the factor on the way to the pane. A tool that measures reach on the curve, Smooth and Erase, works in the camera's shown plane, where x already has the factor and a pixel is the same both ways, so what it touches is what its ring shows; sections it leaves keep their stored values exactly. Other tools' tolerances in plane units are in y's pixels, so along a shortened axis they cover that much less of the screen.
- **Each pane shows the part's middle at its own middle.** That point lines up across panes, and every pane keeps one scale. Other points line up only as far as the two planes share a direction: a plane tilted from its neighbour's shows the same length longer than the neighbour's projection of it, so a tilted part gives up the row or column it does not share. The ear's height direction is one screen axis in Front and Side, so that column holds; its Top leans, so the row with Side does not hold. The tail keeps neither. A stretched pane that held the alignment would be foreshortened again, and a stroke would not land.
- **Guides are the part's own lines.** A guide or a disagreement's limit is the line square to a part direction at a point of the outline's extent, and it is drawn at whatever angle that makes on screen. In the tail's Side the guides run on the diagonal.
- **Where a stroke cannot land by construction, the view sets something else.** The axis stays in the plane of a and b, so Top's and Front's midpoints in c are not read: a lopsided Top stroke builds evenly about the axis. A Front outline alone leaves the sizes to the template, and a `cap` Front shapes only the last sixth of the axis, so it reaches a paw's tube when the paw has no toes and nothing on a paw with them, whose tube stops short and whose toes keep their own section.

## Trace images

A trace lies flat in the plane of one of the character's views, side, top or front, through the character's middle, so it lines up with the body in rest pose. A locked pane shows the trace of the view its camera faces most squarely, so a part that sits at an angle, such as an ear, shows it at the part's angle and it still lines up with the body; Full shows none. It draws in grey at its opacity, behind the character or over it, and only the outlines are read by the build, so nothing depends on having an image. A new trace is fitted as tall as the character stands in that view, centred across it, its bottom on the lowest point; Scale, X and Y are kept from that first fit, X and Y in the image's own pixels at that size, so drawing a part over it never moves it. The panes keep each image at most 1024 pixels on its long side.

## Which pane takes which tool

An outline is drawn in the locked camera of its own plane, so only a locked pane takes one. Surface work lands from any camera, so Full takes it too.

- **A tool says so.** `PaneTool.takesFreeCamera` is off by default, and the pane calls `fullInput` only when it is on. Surface tools and Move turn it on.
- **A refused press shows in place.** With any other tool armed, Full first shows a Surface strokes only chip for as long as the press is held, and nothing is written.
- **The orbit keeps its own input.** Stretch's `orbitNavigation` takes the middle button, the wheel and two fingers, consuming them first; the left button, one finger and the pen are the tool's, as `isToolPress` reads them. Neither ever sees the other's drag.
- **A pointer becomes a ray.** `FullView.ray` gives the line of sight through a pane pixel in body space, through the camera as it is at that moment. A part's triangles lie at `SheetPart.origin` plus `SheetPart.mesh`.

## Adding parts without refactoring

A new part is one of three things, and each slots in without touching what exists or what is saved.

| New part is | What changes | Old characters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A template file over existing blocks (hoof, nose, horn; `wing-feathered`, `arm`, `hand`, `foot`, `head-round`, `torso-upright`, `torso-bird`, `head-bird`, `beak`, `beak-lower`, `leg-bird`, `foot-bird`, `tail-feathers`, `wing-membrane`, `spike`, `plate`, `horn`, `antler`, `hair` and `hair-cap` ship) | Nothing in code. The part picker lists templates from the folder and the Shape inspector renders parameters from the template's metadata | Untouched; they pin their own template ids and versions |
| A block kind (membrane, socket row, per-digit overrides) | One sealed subclass with its `build`, `lint`, `joints`, `sockets` and `areas`, and the schema regenerates. No `when` over block kinds exists outside the block classes and the schema generator, so nothing else changes | Untouched; their files never mention the kind |
| A surface that is not a sweep (a membrane between fingers, a sphere eye) | A block whose `build` returns a patch that is open in v, or closed with poles. Anchors, marks, weights and lines already address patches by name, u and v; a patch spanned between two others says where on each its vertices take their bones (`Spanned`) | Untouched; their anchors name the body patch |

The sheet is such a surface: a tube run with `openV` is cut open at v = 0, a column more with the last on the first, so its two faces meet at an edge (`Patch.openedInV`). Its edge columns are no hole to the watertight check, and lines read its edge as any place round a closed patch (`Patch.meetsAround`). The blob is a skull run closed with a pole at each end.

Four rules make saved characters keep working, from the first commit:

1. **Template versions are immutable and ship forever.** A changed template is a new version file; the old file stays and old characters keep building with it. An upgrade to the new version is offered by Update from library, never applied by itself.
2. **A new parameter's default reproduces the old build exactly.** The golden tests build every shipped template version with default parameters and compare against its stored fixture, so a default that moves a vertex fails the build.
3. **References are by name, never by index.** Poses name bones by part and joint name, anchors name patches, slots name sockets, marks name landmarks. Adding a joint, a patch or a socket to a new template version shifts nothing stored.
4. **Documents carry a format number and a migration chain.** Each step from one format to the next is one function with a fixture saved in the older format, and `readDocument` runs the chain. A document from a newer app is refused with a clear message, never half-read. Shipped templates stay strictly parsed; documents tolerate only what a migration has accounted for.

### Update from library

A part pins the template version it was built with until it is moved on by hand. Update from library moves it to the library's newest version of the same template, the library being the shipped templates and the saved templates' folder as loaded (`TemplateFolder.newerThan`). It is one `UpdateFromLibrary(part, version)`, which moves the part and the other side of its pair, and whose undo is the same command back to the version it left.

- **Everything stored stays.** The command changes the version pinned and nothing else: outlines, parameters (hand-made sockets, joints and landmarks among them), overrides, surface work, the role, the socket the part sits on, the parts on its sockets, the seams' strokes and the pose. Each reaches the new build by name, as the rules above make it, so whatever the new version builds that they name carries over: the winged fennec's torso moved from version 1 to 2 keeps its hand-made socket holding the wing, which rigs from a torso bone and poses.
- **What the new version does not build stays stored and shows in place.** A part on a socket its parent no longer offers (`unseatedParts`) sits at the body's origin, and its row in Parts carries the socket's name on a warning chip. A joint the pose sets that the part no longer builds (`posedJointsMissing`) is left out of the rig and listed by name on a warning chip under the part's joints in Anatomy. An anchor on a patch the new version does not build drops, as any dangling anchor does. A parameter the new version does not declare stays stored and sets nothing. A version or a hand-made socket that offers the missing piece again, or the undo, brings each back.
- **It is offered only over an older version.** The part's Template menu leads with Update from library while the library holds a newer version of the part's template, the root included, which has no other template to change to; the menu shows only where it offers something. A saved template is written at version 1 under an id of its own, so a newer version of it comes only from a file placed in the folder by hand.

## Part template file format

Templates and body plans are JSON files, one per template, shipped under `templates/` with a JSON Schema beside them under `schema/`, and parsed by kotlinx.serialization into the sealed classes above. JSON is the only text format with a `commonMain` parser, a schema language that IntelliJ validates as you type, and a diff that reads in a pull request.

The `templates/` folder is the single source. The build copies it into `character`'s resources with a generated index of its files, and the app loads them from there through `TemplateFolder.shipped()`, once per process, so a new file needs no code change. Lint, golden and schema tests read the folder itself, and a test holds the resources equal to it.

| Format | Why not |
| --- | --- |
| YAML | No `commonMain` parser; indentation errors are silent |
| TOML | Nested arrays of blocks read badly |
| Kotlin DSL | Code, not data: nothing outside the app can validate or diff it, and a user-saved template would need a compiler |
| SQLite | Not diffable or hand-editable; right for the library and caches, wrong for shipped recipes |

### One source of truth

The Kotlin model is the truth and the schema is generated from it, never written by hand. The generator in `schema-gen` walks the kotlinx.serialization `SerialDescriptor` tree, which is the same structure the parser reads, so the schema cannot accept what the parser rejects or the other way round. `@SerialName` on each sealed subclass gives the `kind` discriminator and the subclasses give the `oneOf`; nullability and defaults give `required`; two annotations in the `annotations` module, `@Range` and `@Doc`, give `minimum`, `maximum` and `description`. The `$param` union is a value class with its own serializer and schema fragment (a number, or a string matching `^\$[a-z][a-zA-Z0-9]*$`).

The `generateSchemas` task writes the files under `schema/`. They are committed so IntelliJ and CI validate without a build, and a unit test regenerates and diffs them so a stale schema fails the build. victools is the fallback if the descriptor walk hits a wall; it works from reflection, so it would need Kotlin metadata for nullability. Generating the model from the schema is rejected: generators produce poor Kotlin, lose sealed classes and value classes, and move the truth into a file no IDE can refactor.

### Readable

One file per part. Comments and trailing commas are allowed (kotlinx `allowComments` and `allowTrailingComma`). Every block has a `kind` and most have a `name`, so a file reads as a parts list. Angles are degrees in files and radians in code. Positions are fractions: `u` runs 0 to 1 along the axis from the socket end, `v` runs 0 to 1 around it with 0 on top and 0.5 underneath. Lengths are fractions of the part's own axis length, so a template never states an absolute size; the drawing or the `size` parameter does.

### Fast

A template is a few kilobytes. All of them parse once at startup into immutable data and are keyed by content hash, so an edited file invalidates exactly the derived caches that read it. Parsing cost is nothing beside building slices, and the format never touches the build.

### Verifiable

| Layer | Catches | Runs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| JSON Schema | Structure, required keys, ranges, enum values, `$param` reference syntax | Generated from the model and committed; validates in the editor and in CI over every shipped file; CI fails if stale |
| Typed parse | Unknown `kind`, unknown keys, wrong types; sealed classes make every kind exhaustive in code | On load; a failure names file, path and line |
| Semantic lint | an axis that bears weight resting straight down, `u` ranges inside 0 to 1, joints ordered along the axis, exactly one run reaching the open end when `openEnd` is set, sockets of templates and plans where a run reaches, no two blocks building a patch of one name, a views map where each reading has one view, some view sets height or width, cap and section are not both set, and only Front sets a section or cap, paired sockets symmetric, `$param` names exist, a parameter's `values` ascending, holding its default and in place of `min` and `max`, a tube's `fills` naming a toe row that ends past it, ring count enough for the joint count | On load and in CI |
| Golden build | Each template built with default parameters and no outlines: watertight except the open end, no degenerate triangles, every vertex of every patch matches the fixture to 1/10000 of the axis length; random parameters inside their ranges, or among their values, also build. A missing fixture fails; `./gradlew :character:desktopTest -PupdateGoldens` writes fixtures for review in the diff | JUnit, when a template or builder changes |

### Extensible

`format` is the grammar version of the file and changes rarely. `version` is the template's own and changes when what the template builds changes, since saved characters pin it. Blocks are a tagged union on `kind`: a new block kind is a sealed subclass and a builder function, and the schema regenerates from it. Parsing is strict, so a typo is an error and not a silently ignored key, except keys starting with `x-`, which are reserved for experiments and ignored by the app. Parameters are referenced as `"$name"` strings anywhere a number is allowed, which the schema checks by pattern and the lint by name.

### Parameters

A parameter has a `default`, a number or a flag, and optionally `unit`. A number either moves between `min` and `max`, on `step` if it has one, or takes only the ascending `values` it lists, which stand in for the limits. The Shape inspector's slider runs from the lowest to the highest and lands on the nearest allowed value, so a list with gaps skips them, and Tool knows reads a list by its stretches: `[0, 3, 4, 5]` as 0, 3–5.

### Block reference

A template's optional `role` is the role a part placed from it takes, one of the closed list in Roles; a generic template leaves it out. A generic template carries `generic`, the fewest and most rings and segments a part may choose (`"generic": { "rings": { "min": 8, "max": 96 }, "segments": { "min": 6, "max": 32 } }`); lint holds its topology inside them, enough of the fewest rings for its joints, a named section, and each view setting what a generic part's does. The shipped generic templates are `tube`, `blob` and `sheet`, version 1. Every block takes `kind`, an optional `name`, `if` (a boolean `$param`) and `x-` keys. Blocks build in file order, so a toe row or a pad reads the runs listed before it. The axis takes `rest`, an undrawn `length`, and an optional `bend` and `bendAt`, which set the shape of the undrawn axis: how far it sits toward the underside at that u, which also moves the joints along it. `bearsWeight`, off by default, says the part stands on the ground, so it shortens to keep level with a leaning one; see Legs stay level as they lean. Such an axis rests straight down. A closed template may carry `mount`, the (u, v) that sits on its socket; see Placing parts. The axis may carry `faces`, the body-space direction a head's face looks at rest when it is not along the axis: the angle versions, the gaze and the look-at read it (`PartBuilder.facing`). The topology may carry `focus` and the template `profile`; see Everything is a spline.

Each digit's joints are `name:i.k` from 1, owned and hinged as a toe row's are, so a pose curls each; its u runs along it from the template's numbers, so the joints stay on its rings whatever the parameters. `count` builds that many from the first, so a shoe sets a foot's toes to none; where digits list `counts`, a count builds the digits that list it instead, in order, so one row holds a spread of three toes, another of four and another of five; with `countsNamed` the count holds the named digits that are on, so `hand` 7's `digits` 4 with its thumb on builds three counted fingers and `fingers:thumb`, and with the thumb off four fingers. With `join` (a share of the axis length) the digits are not runs of their own but solids joined to the part's field by a smooth union of that radius, each digit's root by its own digit's `join` factor times it, and the field and digits loft as runs on that one surface (`JoinedDigits`, `geom/field/DigitLoft`): the field's run is cut where the digits first rise out of it, and each digit's run is planar rings across its axis whose rays stop where another digit's axis lies nearer, so two neighbours' runs meet on the line between them, the web's middle; points a ray finds still inside, and the digit's rings sunk in the palm, are buried, built into no face and inked by no line, and the run shading them is the one that shows the surface there. `merge` (in radii, default 0.2) blends each digit into its neighbours, so a small value leaves separate fingers and a large one a mitten of shallow lobes; `knuckles` sets a ball at each root toward the back as a share of the radius, and a digit's own `knuckle` scales it, 0 for a thumb. Every vertex of the field and the digits takes its bones from the runs round it (`Rides` on the patch), easing from the field to a digit over its root and shared between two touching digits, so a curled finger pulls its web and the palm next to it and nothing tears. A digit's `roll` turns its rings about it so v = 0.25 faces its back. `nail` (`name`, `at`, `length`, `width`, `color`, `claw`, `curl`) paints an oval area `nail:i` on each digit's back, a palette slot until filled; with `claw` a joined digit's solid stands out past its tip by that share of its length, curling `curl` of it toward the underside, and the area runs to its point, so nails and claws are one setting; `nail.if` names a flag that turns them off, a cat's claws retracted, and `claw` and `curl` may be `$param`s. `pad` (`name`, `if`, `at`, `length`, `width`, `color`, `rise`) paints an oval area `bean:i` on each digit's underside, v = 0.75, and with `rise` (a share of the radius) joins a ball under it into the field, a toe bean. A claw's point is part of its digit's reach, so it rides the digit's last joint. A row whose `count` builds none leaves the field to loft alone, closed at both ends, as a shoe's foot. `hand` 5 to 7, `foot` 4 to 7, `paw` 5 to 8 and `foot-bird` 4 join their digits this way |

### Save as template

A part saved as a template writes the same file from what it builds with, so there is one format and one validator for shipped and saved templates alike (`savedTemplate`). The file takes the name typed, an id from its words in lower case joined by dashes, made free with a number after it where a loaded template has it, version 1, and the part's role. It holds the template the part pins as the part builds it on a socket: turned by Move's rest and angles, its joints where Move put them, a generic part's section, views, rings and segments fixed and its `generic` field dropped, its parameters' values as their defaults with the limits widened to hold them, and its hand-made joints, sockets and landmarks as blocks named `joint1`, `socket1` and `landmark1`, with the `tip` a hand-made joint brings. It carries no outlines, since no template does. It is written into the saved templates' folder, `templates` in the app's data folder, and loaded at once into the app's catalog and the open character's library, which only ever gain templates, each under a ref none had; `TemplateFolder.withSaved` loads that folder after the shipped templates at every start, leaving out a file that does not load or names a loaded template, so a saved template lints clean on load or is not offered.
