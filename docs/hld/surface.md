# The surface

A character's body is one seamless surface, meshed from the joined solids of its parts. Each part's drawn outlines sweep a solid, each solid joins the solid of the part it sits on by a smooth union, and the whole is meshed at once. A join has no edge, no seam and no buried point. Parts are not meshes of their own; they are coordinates on this surface. Thin and hard parts are the exception and stay meshes of their own. [data-model.md](data-model.md) holds the entities, [architecture.md](architecture.md) the graph, the field reads and the cell levels, [look.md](look.md) how the surface is shaded, coloured and inked.

## The field

The character's field is `JoinedField` over its members, in `geom/field`, built by `CharacterField` in `character/surface`.

- **Members are runs.** Each swept run of a part (a head's skull and muzzle, each digit of a paw or hand) is its own member, a `SweptSolid` read from the run's slices. A part's later runs join its first run by the part's `merge` parameter, else a share of the least extent of the joining run's box (`RUN_BLEND_SHARE`), and the part's first run joins the first run of the part its socket is on by the socket's blend: `PartInstance.blend`, else its template's `join` share of its length, else the larger of a quarter of the run's least box extent (`SOCKET_BLEND_SHARE`) and, for a run that starts open (a limb, a neck, a tail), 1.4 times its root ring's radius (`ROOT_BLEND_SHARE`), so a neck flows into the shoulders and the roots of the arms and legs round over as wide as they are thick. A head's default, either its template's or the larger of the two, is taken at its character's neck style's share (look.md, Necks): all of it blended, 0.4 between, 0.08 defined. That rounded default is held to one and a half times the narrowest gap between the run, past its root and the blend's reach, and its parent's field (`roomyBlend`), since a smooth union fuses surfaces less than half its blend apart: an arm hanging close beside the hips is not webbed to them. Siblings never blend: two legs, or a tail and a hind leg, meet by a plain union.
- **A join blends by the angle its surfaces meet at.** A part's socket join, unless its template gives a `join` blend, is a gradient-based blend (Gourmel, Barthe, Cani, Wyvill, Bernhardt, Paulin, Grasberger, "A gradient-based implicit blend", 2013): at each point the smooth union takes a share of its blend set by the angle between the child's and the parent's own gradients there (`JoinAngles`, `angleShare`), none where they lie within about 10 degrees of each other and the whole blend from about 32 degrees on, eased between. Where the two surfaces meet at a crease (a leg out of the hips, an arm under the shoulder, a neck out of the shoulders, a tail out of the rump) the crease fills as wide as the blend; where they run alongside each other or lie on each other (an arm's top along the shoulder line, a thigh's outside along the hip) the union is their plain union and nothing swells. The shares are sampled once a join, on a lattice a third of the blend apart over the box where the blend can act, from each member's own field by central differences, and read between lattice points trilinearly, so the mesher, the vertex placement and the anchors read the same field; a part's share of the surface (Parts as coordinates) takes the join's whole blend. Template joins (hands, feet, ears) and the merges of a part's runs keep a plain smooth union.
- **A root on a closed end goes on into it.** A part's first run that starts open and sits on its parent's closed end (a neck on a torso's top, a tail on a rump) carries on past its root ring, back along the run, by its root radius, or 0.75, 0.5 or 0.25 of it, the longest at which that ring lies wholly inside the parent (`rootReach`); its u carries on there as past any end. A neck wider than the top it stands on so rises out of the torso and blends into the shoulders round its whole root, with no flat root standing out over the top's edge.
- **Cuts stay inside a part.** A part's own cut, such as a paw's flat sole, is a smooth intersection applied to that part's field before the union, so it flattens nothing else.
- **Each member's field is distance-like**: negative inside, near the true distance close to the surface. It is read only near the surface.
- **A ring is read round from its middle.** A run's solid takes each ring's radius in each direction round from the middle of the ring's box, or from its section's centre where the section names one (`ClosedSpline.centre`): a point inside from which every direction meets the outline once, so a ring with a hollow dished into one face, an ear's, is read whole and its hollow stays outside the solid.
- **Which parts are in the field.** A part whose template says `"surface": "field"` (the default), that no garment owns, that its socket does not show as copies (see data-model, Copies of one part), and each of its swept runs that no block of its template marks `separate`. A part marked `separate` builds as its own mesh. A template's `join` gives its parts' socket blend as a share of the first run's length (`JoinSpec.blend`) and whether the join is inked by default (`JoinSpec.ink`), where the part sets neither.

## The mesher

`AdaptiveMesher` (`geom/surface`) is a dual mesher on a sparse grid of blocks of 8 by 8 by 8 cells: one vertex in each cell the surface crosses, one quad across each grid edge it crosses, a manifold split for a cell that holds two sheets of surface (two toes in one cell each get a vertex). A sheet thinner than a cell that crosses a cell face along two of its corners, a talon's point or a sliver grazing a sole's floor, gives the cells either side one vertex each whose edge all four of the face's quads share; `pinchSplit` splits both vertices into one copy per fan of triangles round them, as the face's other pairing of its crossings would, so the mesh stays closed and manifold, pinched there. A vertex starts at the mean of its cell's edge crossings, is relaxed toward its neighbours inside the cell, and is projected onto the field along its gradient. The quads are near-square and the vertices inside their cells, which is the mesh the contour and the colour cuts read. The methods are those of surface nets (Gibson), dual contouring and its manifold form (Ju, Losasso, Schaefer, Warren; Schaefer, Ju, Warren) and, for the contour, Hertzmann and Zorin.

- **The cell** is `CELL_SHARE` (0.1) of the least half-extent of the root part's first run, so it does not change with what the character wears; the fennec's body meshes at 0.024. Heads, jaws, hands and feet take half of it and digits a quarter (see Cell levels in architecture.md), so a limb, a torso or a tail is coarse and a face, a palm, a sole and each toe or finger fine; the dressed fennec's surface is about 42,000 to 62,000 triangles, the dressed woman's about 33,000 to 42,000.
- **Blocks are the unit of an edit.** A vertex is identified by its cell, so a block an edit did not reach keeps every vertex and every per-block cache. An edit meshes again only the blocks within the changed members' old and new boxes grown by their reach, and each block keeps every member's own field at its grid points, so only the changed member is read again and the union recombined (`BlockReuse`, `ChannelSource`).
- **A coarse surface while a gesture is open.** While a part is dragged the surface meshes at twice the cell (`COARSE_FACTOR`), and the full surface follows when the gesture ends. A gesture that leaves the placed character as it was, such as a pose drag, keeps its full mesh.
- **Reads** go through the `FieldReads` seam, on the CPU or on the GPU (architecture.md, Field reads). Batches of blocks are meshed in parallel on the graph's dispatcher and a stale run stops between batches.

## Parts as coordinates

Every vertex of the surface holds, for the at most four members with the largest shares of the surface there (`SurfaceShares`, `VertexCoordinates`):

- **Its share**: how much of the surface there is that member's. Across a smooth union of a child with its parent and blend k, the join's whole blend whatever share of it the angle gives the surface, the child's share is the union's own mixing weight, clamp(0.5 + 0.5 (d_parent - d_child) / k, 0, 1), and the parent holds what its children leave. Shares sum to one, and change exactly where the surface changes from one part's shape to the other's. A part's share is the sum of its runs'.
- **Its place on the member**: u along the member's spine and v around it, found from the member's swept solid, which gives for any point the two rings whose planes hold it, how far it lies between them and the direction round the ring. Past an end ring u carries on along the end ring's axis, so a point on a join below a leg's first ring reads a u a little under 0.

A stored anchor is `(part, patch, u, v, offset)`. The patch is the member: a run by its name (`body`, `muzzle`, `pad:<name>`, a digit `toes:i`). u and v are template coordinates, defined by the rings swept from the drawing, so a redraw moves the surface and leaves each anchor meaning the same place on the part. Nothing stores a vertex, a cell or a mesh; every vertex's coordinates are computed again from the solids.

**An anchor becomes a position on the field, not on the mesh** (`FieldAnchors`): it starts at its point on its member's own swept surface at (u, v), moves along the joined field's gradient to where the field is zero, and off by its offset along the normal there. The gradient is read by central differences a quarter of the cell the anchor's run meshes at apart, so a face's anchors read the field as finely as the face is meshed. On a part's own share it lands where it would without the join, moved a little by the blend; where parts blend it lands on the blend. Resolved surface work therefore does not wait on meshing. Picking goes the other way: a ray hits a triangle of the surface, the part is the member with the largest share at the hit, and the anchor is that member's (u, v) interpolated across the triangle, with v unwrapped across its seam and a pole read in a small flat chart round it.

Sockets and landmarks read the parent's own swept surface at (u, v), so placing parts does not wait on the character's mesh. A socket at a closed end, where the ring is a pole, sits where the run's axis leaves the surface (`PlacedPart.poleAt`, which leg lengths read too), wherever the drawn outline's furthest point leans, so a neck on a torso's closed top stands on its middle.

## Surface work on the surface

- **Brush strokes** measure their radius and fade by distance in space. A stroke resolves its anchors on the field, and each vertex's opacity is its distance from the resolved curve, cut along each triangle where that distance crosses the radius. No chart is involved, so a stroke crosses a join as it crosses the middle of a part.
- **Shapes within one part** (areas a template names, pads, nails, beans, regions drawn on one part; a border that runs once round a closed part along one edge and back along another is one band round it, its two seam stretches no edge, `UvShapeDistance`): each vertex where the part has a share takes its signed distance from the shape in the part's own (u, v), scaled to body units by the part's length per u and its girth, and the shape is cut where that crosses zero. v wraps across its seam; near a pole the test is made in the pole's flat chart. A triangle a corner of which lies nearer the shape's border than its longest side is split 4 by 4 for the cut (`PaintSplits`, `TriangleSplits` in `geom`), each new vertex taking the mix of its corners' values moved by how far the shape's distance at its own (u, v) lies from the mix of theirs, so a stripe narrower than a cell, or a border bending inside one, is cut where it is drawn; points on a side two split triangles share are one vertex of both.
- **Shapes across parts** (a sock from paw to leg, a face mask from muzzle to skull): the border's anchors resolve onto the field, the curve between them is laid on the surface, and the mesh is cut along it, the inside the side the border's order gives.
- **A part's base colour** is the part's own section: everything where its share is the largest, cut along the curve where its share equals its neighbour's. Shapes on a part are kept to the same section. Where two parts meet at a slant that curve is sharp and the join's ink follows it, so the cut stays on it; where they meet nearly level, the section lying within the waver of its field reads across more than a cell, or where three parts meet in a wedge finer than a cell, the curve is lost in the waver and the cut is held a little past it, ending where the shares part (`SectionMargin`).
- **Marks** resolve their anchors on the field and lay the curve between them on the surface. A floating stroke keeps its frame from the part's coordinates at its root.
- **Fur** roots resolve as anchors; grain reads the owning part's frame at the root, blended by share across a join; the fur rule's spacing walks the surface.
- **Line weights** act by distance in space. **The face map** casts its rays at the field.

## Ink

- **The contour** is found on the character's mesh, the garments' meshes and the separate parts' meshes, along edges between faces toward and away from the camera, interpolated across faces from per-vertex facing. The body has no buried corners, cast points or end caps, so no exception for them exists. The ribbon's smoothing of the edge path applies.
- **Joins** are inked by default: the line is the curve where the two parts' shares are equal, eased and faded where the surface turns away. The joined part's `join.ink` parameter set to 0 turns it off; where a head sets none, its neck style says (look.md, Necks).
- **Separate parts** meeting the body draw the overlap crease: the curve on the part where it leaves the body's field.
- **Hems and openings** are a garment mesh's open edges, which lie on smooth level curves of its coverage; their ink is on by the garment's hem switch.
- **Region borders** are their cut curves.
- **Prints on a garment** are lines resolved on the garment's own field and cut to its cloth (data-model.md, A garment prints on its cloth).

## Shading

A vertex's shading normal (`SurfaceShading`) is each owning run's own shading normal at the vertex's (u, v), read from that part's slices and weighed by the run's share and by how near the vertex lies to the run's own surface. Where a run's own shading holds a vertex whole, the vertex shades exactly as that part does; across a join the joined surface's own normal takes over, so two parts' normals are never mixed into a normal of neither. Normals are then smoothed along the quads (`ShadingSmoothing`), more across joins than inside a part. The shadow edge therefore runs over a join as one curve. Far-part darkening is a per-vertex amount, the shares of the paired parts on the far side of the body's plane (`CharacterSurface.positiveZ` and `negativeZ`), so a far leg darkens and its join with the body ramps. The body and each paint layer draw their far sides in their own pass, each side's tone mixed over theirs by its share (`LayerSides`), so no side draws the mesh again.

## Eyes

An eye is painted on the head's surface (`PaintedEye`, `SurfaceEyes`), a parametric eye whose shapes are made from an `EyeLook` instead of drawn per view.

- **Where it sits.** Its middle is an anchor on the head, resolved on the field, and its facing is the surface normal there turned `lookOut` degrees outward. The head's eye landmark is the default place; the panes' handles move, size and turn it.
- **How it is painted.** Each shape is an oval in the plane square to the facing, its chart running along the head's coordinates toward the front and the top, and each vertex facing the eye is projected along the facing into that plane and painted where it falls inside. The paint foreshortens and wraps as the head turns and never stands past the head's outline, since nothing stands off the surface.
- **Its look.** Lengths are shares of the head's own u and v at the eye's middle, so an eye keeps its look on a larger or smaller head: opening width and height, lower lid and front narrowing, opening tilt and whole-eye tilt, look out, iris size, ratio and offset, round or slit pupil, highlight size and offset, and a lid line, up to three lash flicks and a brow of its own thickness as ink lines; top-lid flatness and back-corner sharpness; and, off by default, a lid mass, lash clumps, iris tones and a lid shadow; and its lids' closed line, the upper lid's own colour, fade and marks (see [look.md](look.md), Eyes and gaze). The white, iris, pupil and highlight name palette slots, and an optional front look takes over as a view turns to the front (`fromFront`). Its detail is drawn the same way, each shape read at each fragment in the eye's chart: the lid shadow as the opening kept outside itself widened and moved down, the iris's darker top, lighter crescent and rim as the iris kept inside or outside a second oval, and the lid mass and lash clumps as one ink layer whose edge is measured square to the lid curve, widths counted in heights by the opening's own proportion on the head, so the mass wraps round a steep corner and follows a slanted opening, its flick and lashes tapering curved strokes rounded into the bands where they meet, each band thinning away by how steep its open lid runs as the opening lies on the head (the flick rooted at the upper lid's shoulder, the lower band growing in from its own, a thin line joining them round the back corner); its layer is cut over the head within the mass's reach, so it never stands past the head's outline either. The fills draw in order white, lid shadow, iris, crescent, darker top, rim, pupil, highlight, the skin the lids cover as they close, shaded in the slot of the head's paint the eye sits in, the lid's fade-into colour and its own colour, its marks, lid mass; a lid mass takes the lid line's place.
- **Lids** close the opening as the eye's lid pose says (`ChartLids` on the almond): each lid's edge is its open curve moved part way to the closed line, and every shape reads the almond with those edges at each fragment, so the fills are cut off past a lid, the lid shadow and the mass follow the edges, and the lid's own colour and marks (`ChartLidPaint`) are read from the moving top edge, the colour from it up to the open lid and its `above`, a mark from its root on the lid. The lid line and lash flicks are placed on the edges as ink marks. The layers are cut once to reach every pose, the lid's within the mass's reach of the whole opening and above it, so standing the lids changes only the shapes the layers are drawn to (`PaintLayer.withLids`), and a paint node's `EyeMemo` keeps each side's layers between paintings of the same surface.
- **Gaze** moves the iris, pupil and highlight within the opening in the eye's plane, clipped by the opening: `gazeForward` and `gazeUp` are shares of the opening's half width and half height.
- A mirrored eye is painted again on the other side of the head's plane, its anchor's v read as 1 - v.

## Thin and hard parts

Some parts are thinner than any useful cell, or need edges the field rounds off: ears up to version 2, membrane wings, webs between digits, membranes between parts, fins, loose ends and the older `hair` template's locks (thin); claws, nails, horns, a beak, teeth and parts a garment owns (hard). A template marks such a part, or a block of it, `"surface": "separate"`. A separate part is a mesh of its own built by its blocks (a lofted run, `membrane`, `pad`), placed at its socket, read on the parent's own swept surface and sunk into the body by the socket's sink, so its root lies inside the body's field and the body hides it. Its ink is the overlap crease. Its rig is its own bones, with the parent's bone fading in toward the root; a part a garment owns that follows the body takes the body's bones under it instead (data-model.md, A garment can own parts). A pad with a thickness, such as nose leather, is a small mesh laid on the surface.

## Membranes

Skin spanned between parts is a thin sheet of its own, never a run of the field: `MembraneBuilder` (`geom`) spans it across from one rail to another, its rings running from inside the first rail to inside the second, each going along one face from the rails' roots to the free edge, round a thin lip and back along the other face, so it is closed and has no buried point. It is as thick as its `thickness` share of the thinner rail half way between them and swells toward each rail into a fillet, so where it meets a digit or a limb it rounds into it rather than standing on it; its free edge, between the rails' far ends, dips toward its middle by its `sag` share of the rails' reach (a scallop) or runs straight at 0; its middle may billow off the plane between the rails, nothing at the root and most at the free edge, toward the ground where the sheet tilts less than about 66 degrees from level and away from the body's middle where it hangs beside it. Each vertex rides both rails (`Spanned`): it takes, from each, the bones the part there gives its place along that rail, blended by how far across it lies, so the sheet bends, stretches and turns as the digits or limbs it spans spread, curl and pose, and adds no bone. A membrane draws no overlap crease where it meets its rails: its own contour, which is its free edge seen face on, is its only ink, and the fillet carries the surface into the part it meets with no line.

- **Webs between digits** are the `membrane` block of a template, on a `digits` row joined to a field or a `toeRow`: `hand` from version 8, `foot` from 9, `paw` from 10 and `foot-bird` from 5 build one between each two neighbouring counted digits across the row while `webbing` is on, a digit pointing back and a named digit (a thumb, a hind toe) left out. Each rail is the digit's straight axis from a little inside where it comes out of the palm or sole to `webReach` of the way on to its tip (1 a full web, 0.5 a half web), as thick as the digit there and tapering as it does; `webEdge` is the scallop and `webThickness` the share. The webs are pieces of the part that are not runs (`separatePieces`), in its `webbing` area, so a recipe's `areas` colours them apart.
- **Membranes between parts** are the character's own (`Membrane` in `document`, data-model.md): each rail a chain of edges on parts, from (u, v) to (u, v), a socket point an edge whose ends meet, read along its length at rest. A rail's middle at each place lies `sink` of the way from the part's surface into the middle of its ring there, its half height how deep that lies, held to one and a half times the thinner rail's middling one so a sheet swells into a torso no more than into the limb across from it. The sheet's root runs between the rails' first ends, so rails that start on a flank root it there inside the body, and its free edge between their last ends. A mirrored membrane is built again on the other side, each paired part's twin in its place and v mirrored on a part in the middle. `withMembranes` adds each one to the assembled character as a part of its own (`PlacedPart.spanning`), its mesh in body space, after the garments are dressed; it is not in the field, a chart or the hair's push, takes its colour slot or the base slot of the part its first rail starts on, and is rigged by `membraneRigs`. One whose parts are not all there builds nothing.

## Ears in the field

An ear from version 3 is a run of the field, one surface with the head. Its tube's section is a shell (`TubeShell`, `ShellSection` in `geom`): a full superellipse thicker toward its back, `thickness` deep for its half width, with a hollow dished into its front between two lips a `rim` in from either side, `cup` of the thickness deep. The section blends from flat to hollow and back over the stretch of u the shell names (`SectionAlong`), so the hollow deepens up from the base and closes before the tip, round or pointed. Its control points lie in two stretches, the front between the lips over v 0.85 to 0.15 at every depth and the rest round the back, so the template's `inner` area over those v is the hollow wherever the cup lies, and the `back` area the back. Its section names a centre below the hollow's floor (see The field), which bounds the hollow at under half the thickness (`SHELL_MAX_HOLLOW`). The template's `join` blends it into the skull over 0.3 of its length, so the skull rises into the ear with no edge, and leaves the join unlined; a part's own blend and `join.ink` override both. Ears mesh at half the body's cell, as the head does (architecture.md, Cell levels).

## Pockets

A part whose template holds a `pocket` block (`Pocket`, the `pocket` template) is a recess into the part its socket is on (`withPockets`, `PocketField`). Its first run is the hollow, from its mouth at the socket into the parent along the part's rest, closed round at its far end; its solid runs on out of the mouth by 2.5 times the mouth's radius (`MOUTH_REACH`), so it opens through the surface wherever the surface curves.

- **The cut.** Each run of the parent part whose box comes near the hollow is cut by it and a wall round it (`Hollow`, `CarvedSolid`): its field is the larger of its own and the wall less the hollow's field, so nothing of it lies within the wall's thickness of the hollow.
- **The lining.** The pocket's member is the lining (`HollowLining`): the shell the wall's thickness round the hollow, kept to where the parent's uncut runs reach, joined by a smooth union to a rolled rim, a tube of the rim's radius about the curve where the hollow meets the parent's surface, lying just outside the hollow. So the hollow's surface and the rim are the pocket's share and read its coordinates, u along the hollow from the mouth and v round it, and the parent's surface outside the rim is the parent's. The lining joins the parent's run by the rim's radius, so the join's ink rings the mouth just outside the rim.
- **Paint.** The pocket's own areas paint the inside: the template's `inside` area from a little in from the mouth to the bottom shows the `nose` slot until filled, and the rim takes the part's base colour, the body's where the recipe gives it.
- **Sizes.** The rim's radius and the wall's thickness are the block's shares of the mouth's radius (0.25 and 0.6 by default, the wall at least the rim's width). The lining meshes at the digits' level, a quarter of the body's cell, so a nostril's rim is several cells across.
- **Reads.** A carved run or a lining is no swept solid, so its field is read on the CPU (architecture.md, Field reads); everything else reads as before.

## Soles

A field whose floor cuts across its axis (`FieldFloor.across`, a hoof) with `hollow` above 0 dishes the sole of each run standing on it (`withSoles`, `SoleField`): a run whose full rings reach the part's floor, its furthest stretch along the axis, gets an ellipsoid (`TurnedEllipsoid`) centred on the floor under its widest ring lying on the floor, as wide as that ring less `rim` of its half width each way, and `hollow` of its narrower half width deep. The dish is cut out of every run of the part it reaches (`Hollow` with no wall, `CarvedSolid`), its edge rounded into the rim (`CarvedSolid.blend`), so each toe of a cloven hoof has its own hollow inside its wall, and the surface stays one closed mesh. A point the dish shapes is placed as the sole straight above it, so the hollow takes the sole's paint and shading. The part's own patches stay flat-soled; only the body's field is dished.

A digit joined to such a field whose axis leaves the solid through the floor ends level with it (`DigitSole`): the rings that come within 0.6 of its radius of the floor are cast again from where the digit's axis crosses each level, their frames turning a step at a time until they lie flat on the floor, down to an edge ring just above the floor, then an inner ring and a pole on the floor itself. The side's rings rise evenly in u to just under the floor's u, so the paint keeps an even scale along them. The sole is one flat plane, its edge one ring, and its u runs past the floor's u, so an area from the floor's u paints the sole and nothing on the wall.

## Mouth recesses

A mouth whose lips part is cut into the part that holds it (`mouthRecesses`, `MouthRecess`, `withRecesses`, `RecessField`; look.md, Mouth). Its shaped opening is read across at 25 places along the mouth line, corner to corner: at each the opening's top, middle and bottom in the mouth's flat frame are found on the patch, each walked to from the place before so a mouth round a muzzle's tip keeps to one side of it where the flat frame folds, and a place the walk misses by more than 2% of the mouth's width is left out. A place becomes a station (`RecessStation`): the middle on the surface, the outward direction square to the mouth line and to the opening there, the opening's half height and how deep the recess goes. The recess (`RecessSolid`) is a half ellipse at each station, as tall as the opening and that deep behind the surface, with a slot running out in front past the lips' curve, swept from station to station, the stations where the lips meet only at its two ends.

- **The cut.** The recess is cut out of the run the mouth lies on with no wall (`Hollow`, `CarvedSolid`), its edge rounded into the lips by 0.35 of the opening's tallest half; out of the part's other runs it reaches it is cut wider by the blend they join by, so the walls are the mouth's run's own and its paint is not split between runs.
- **The walls' paint.** A point the recess shapes is placed in the opening it is seen through from in front (`RecessSolid.placeInOpening`, `CarvedSolid.placeCut`): the back at the opening's middle, a wall toward the edge on its side by how far across it lies, the stations' places mixed the short way round in v, or across the flat chart round a closed end where both lie near it (`mixedPlace`). So the opening's inner fill lies on the walls, and a loop laid in from the edge (`RecessSolid.deeperThan`) paints the back.
- **Lines across it.** An anchor whose place on its run lies across the opening, or on the lips' rounded edge, stays where its run puts it rather than landing on the field (`CharacterField.inRecess`), so a lip's line stays on the lips. A landing elsewhere goes no further along its line than four times the field where it starts, or the cell, so a place where the field flattens does not throw it across the body.
- **When it is cut.** The recesses are their own node, from each mouth holder's surface work and surface, which the one surface's mesh node reads beside the placed character; a closed mouth, a painted one and a beak's seam cut nothing.

## Curved axes

A part whose template sweeps along its axis (`PartTemplate.sweeps`: every run a `tube`, and no skull, field, digits, toe row or membrane; a `ring` is a closed loop of its own and does not) follows a curve when it has one: its template's curve places (`tail-curve`), or its own, which replace them. Its own are places (`curve.<n>.along`, `.up`, `.side`, 2 to 24, in the part's own axes as fractions of its length) or a spiral (`curve.spiral.turns`, `.pitch`, `.shrink`, `AxisSpiral`), whose places `spiralPlaces` gives: leaving the socket along the rest and coiling its turns toward Side's +y (toward its underside when negative) about a line toward v 0.25, moving `pitch` of its first diameter that way each turn, its radius falling to `shrink` of the first by the tip. Places may turn back on themselves and leave the plane, so a hook, a helix or a loop is one part. Copies of a curved part on a spread or path socket are instances of its one curved build.

- **The curve** is the natural cubic spline through the socket point and the places by chord length (`NaturalSpline`, `arcLengthAxis`), its bend continuous through every place, scaled about the socket point to the part's length, u the share of length along it.
- **Rings.** Each ring stands square to the curve, its up carried along from the first ring without twisting (parallel transport), so a helix does not spin its section. A curved run takes more rings than its template's where its curve turns more than 6 degrees a ring would allow (`BuildContext.rings`, up to 256): a horn of two turns builds over a hundred rings where its template has 24.
- **Undrawn**, the template's radius and taper set each ring's size along the curve. **Drawn** with a curve of its own (`TubeSpec.followsAxis`), the Side and Top outlines give each ring's height and width at its u, the drawing's length is the length along the curve, and the drawing's midline is not read: the outlines give the width along the length, the curve the path (`ViewSizing.sliceIn`). A template's curve shapes the undrawn part only: drawn without a curve of its own, a part's rings stand in the planes along its rest that the drawing sets, its midline the path, so a silhouette traced from the undrawn part builds it again.
- **No folds.** Where the curve bends tighter than the part is thick there, rings narrow smoothly to within 0.85 of the bend's radius, each ring's share taken as the least over its neighbours three either way and averaged over the same, so neighbouring rings never cut into each other. A drawn ring's reach is taken along the way the curve turns, so a flat ribbon bending across its thin side keeps its width.
- **Said, not silent.** `CurveFit` reports how a part's own curve lands: ignored on a template that does not sweep, and otherwise the least share of their size its rings keep (`SliceBuilder.bendHold`); under 0.75 the curve counts as clipped. A recipe warns at the part's `curve` or `spiral` for either, and Shape's Curve section shows a warning chip.
- **Meshing.** A tail or a horn joined into the field whose tip is under three body cells across meshes at the head's level (`digitLevels`), so a curl's round end and an antler's branch are several cells across. A hand's or a foot's own run whose last fifth of rings is under three of the head's cells across at its thickest meshes at the digits' level, so a slim drawn toe end or mitten keeps a smooth outline.

A child on a socket whose `outward` is `along` sinks back along the parent's own direction where the socket sits (`PlacedPart.alongAt`), not along its own rest: an antler (version 2) on an antler's `tip` seats inside the beam's end and continues it, curved or not.

## Creatures

A creature needs no join rule, only parts with solids, blends at its sockets and a separate mark on the parts that are thin or hard.

- **Paws and feet** (`paw`, `foot`): the foot from version 8 and the paw from version 9 rise into the leg (an ankle capsule with ankle bones swelling across it, a wrist), sit their heel back by the ankle's radius and blend into the leg by their template's `join`, so the leg meets an ankle and a heel, not a step. The palm and each toe are members of the part, the toes joined to the palm by `merge`, so a mitten is a large merge and separate toes a small one. Claws are hard parts on the toes' tips. Beans and the main pad are areas in the part's own coordinates, one chart over palm and toes. The sole's flat cut applies to the part's own field.
- **Hooves** (`hoof`): a crown and one or two hoof digits joined to it, the digits widening to the sole, all cut flat by a floor across the part's axis (`Floor.across`), since the part rests down. The part is keratin whole, so the inked join to the leg's end is the coronet.
- **Hands** (`hand`): fingers are members joined to the palm by the web's blend; nails are hard parts or painted areas on the fingertips.
- **Hair**: lock groups are separate meshes, never in the field (parts.md, Hair locks). `hair-volume`, `hair-clump` and `hair-tuft` are runs in the field, one mass blended into the head (parts.md, Hair); a clump shown as copies is its own mesh. The older `hair-cap` is a run in the field riding the head's bone; `hair` parts (a fall, locks) are separate meshes pushed out of the body in each pose.
- The dragon's wings, the koi's fins and tail fin and the toucan's wings and tail are thin; the toucan's beak and every horn and claw are hard.

## Garments on the surface

A garment covers the cloth painted on the body as its areas ([data-model.md](data-model.md), Garments), each part's painted cells read as a smooth field over the part's (u, v) (`ClothField`); a part let out through a Hole, and the parts beyond it, are not covered, and a Hole's cuff is a band at that part's root, reaching `CUFF_BAND_U` (0.12) along it or, on a part thick for its length, two and a half of its root's radii (`rootCuffU`, at most a quarter of it), so a tail bending at its root bends inside its cuff. Every garment's cloth is cells: a branch given as a box has its box painted (data-model.md, Every garment's cloth is its cells), and a cuff's band is the cells from its part's root to its size. A part no cell paints is not covered. A branch's own range is read only where it runs past an end of its part the cells reach, below 0 or past 1, as a skirt or a cape trailing past the rump. Its surface is the zero level of g(p) = min(f_covered(p) - ease(p), drawn(p)) (`GarmentField`, `SurfaceGarment`).

- **f_covered** is the joined field of the runs it covers, a part's digits among them where its cloth reaches the part's far end the whole way round (`WornCover.digits`): each finger, toe or hoof grown by the part's ease and joined to the part's first run as the body joins it, so a glove parts between the fingers as the hand does and each glove finger takes that digit's bones; each part round its painted rows two rows further each way, rounded out to steps of 32 rows, half the part (`ClothReach`), with their blends, narrowed in the garment's ease so a sleeve stays apart from the body under the arm. A sleeve joins the body's run as a leg joins a torso. Where a covered part's cloth ends within four rows of a socket on it holding an uncovered part, short of the part's end, as a trouser hem just above the ankle, the field also holds that uncovered part's run over the quarter of it by the join, grown by the same ease and blended as the body blends them (`joinedNear`), so the cloth follows the body's swell at the join and no skin stands out through it there; that part's share cuts the cloth away as any uncovered part's does. A part coming out through a hole is left to it, and so is one coming out through an end the cloth reaches that does not point down at rest, which the cloth closes over, as it does a neck at a torso's top; a part coming out through an end that points down, a foot below a legging painted to the ankle, is grown over as any other, so the hem follows the body's blend into it at fit 0. Where a garment's ease over a covered part varies and its cloth starts within half the part of its root, the part it sits on, when uncovered, is held the same way over a quarter of its u either side of the socket, so a snug collar on a neck widening into the shoulders follows the widening and no skin stands out through it.
- **ease(p)** is the branch's ease at the vertex's coordinates on the part under it. Where a garment's ease over a part varies, the part's run in f_covered is grown ring by ring (`GrownRuns`, `ClothEase`): its rings are first refined to one a chart row apart, then each ring's radius in each direction round grows by the ease there less the branch's least ease, which the run's member adds all round, so the hems, open ends and coverage read that least ease as they read a fit. The ease at a ring's u and a direction's v and side is the part's profile, the front's toward +x, the back's away from it and their mean at the sides; past a dropped seam's u it eases over 0.03 of u from the stand-off of the cloth over the part it grows from at its socket to the part's own, pinched by 0.35 of the lesser at the seam; over a gathering area's cells, read two rows either side, it eases to that area's gather; and over a folded area's cells, read half a row either side so its edge rises over about a row, it adds the fold's thickness, so a rolled cuff or a turtleneck's fold-over stands proud of the cloth beside it as a step whose edge is the areas' inked seam, and its hem rolls by the garment's `hemRoll`. A layer's ease adds the ease of each inner garment's cloth over the same part at each place. What a ring grows past its own radius, by its ease or its hang, stops short of every other part's run by 0.002 (`Clearance`), taking half the gap where that part is covered by the same garment, since its cloth grows toward it too; a part the ring's surface lies inside, as at their join, is no obstacle, so a loose top's side stops at an arm hanging beside it at rest and two sleeves' worth of cloth never fuse into one. A garment whose ease is the same everywhere and does not hang grows no run of its own and reads the body's member channels as before.
- **Hang.** With a hang, a covered body, spine or leg run, never a sleeve, whose rings' axes point within about 37 degrees of up or down hangs below its highest covered ring: ring by ring down, each direction's radius reaches at least as far out, level, as the ring above it does that way, plus the flare times the drop between them, and no more than the hang's limit past its own grown radius at the front and back, a quarter of it at the sides where an arm hangs beside the body, so a top falls straight from the chest and shoulder blades over the waist, and shorts and trouser legs from the seat and thigh, while the cloth still follows any body that swells further out.
- **Dropped seams.** A part a garment drops a seam on carries the ease of the cloth above it down to the seam and is inked there: the zero curve of the vertex's u less the seam's, over vertices held at least half by that part, drawn with the garment's seams where it inks its hems. A drawn Side or Top outline that stands off further than the ease becomes a swept solid of the branch's own (drawn(p)), joined by a plain union, so a skirt hanging free from the waist is the drawn solid below the body.
- **Layers**: an outer garment's covered field is the inner garment's surface, so eases add.
- The garment is meshed by the same adaptive mesher as the body over its covered region, each member at a cell level of its own (`CellLevels`, architecture.md), and each of its vertices carries the body's coordinates at the point it grows from. The garment's own level is that of the finest run it covers, no finer than half the body's cell, but a garment over digits at the digits' own quarter of it, so a shoe or a hood is as fine as the foot or the head under it; each run's cloth meshes at that level, halved again, to a quarter of the body's cell at most, while the cloth's narrowest band over that run, along it or round it, spans fewer than four cells, so a belt or a strap keeps its width; a part's band is its painted cells' span. In a garment with any drawn cloth (see Cloth below) every run's cloth halves on to an eighth of the body's cell, so a painted collar band beside drawn cloth is several cells across, and over drawn cloth while the narrowest drawn piece or gap between pieces spans fewer than four cells, that width read as twice the distance from the outline along each ridge of the drawn cloth's distance, so a strap, a tie and the gap between two cups are several cells across. A narrow band refines only the space round its own run: a collar on the neck leaves the rest of a sweater at the sweater's own level. The garment meshes from the cell of its coarsest cloth, so a garment whose cloth is all at one level meshes as one mesh at that cell, and its mesh reports the cell of its finest cloth, which its coverage, cut, hems, charts, anchors, wrinkles and gathers are sized by, as when the whole garment meshed there. An uncovered run the cloth grows over by a join meshes at the level of the covered run beside it. Easing coverage near the hems, a vertex of cloth meshed coarser than the finest moves less in proportion to its cell, so the plane its farther neighbours give does not carry it across zero into islands.
- **Cloth** (`ClothField`, `clothOf`): a part's chart of 64 by 32 cells, each cell any area paints of it held as 1 and the rest 0, read on a pair's leader, blurred by 0.8 cells along and round, rows held at the part's ends and columns wrapping. Its half level is a smooth curve along the painted cells' edge, with no steps: a slanted hem is a straight line through the cells' stairs. A line one cell wide reads under the half level and is no cloth; two cells wide it stays. Each area of a garment of several is blurred on its own as well; all areas are blurred together for the cloth, so areas that touch leave no gap, and their seam is where one area's field equals the most of the later areas' (`ClothSeams`), inked with the hems. Fields are equal for the same cells in the same areas. **Drawn cloth** (`DrawnCloth`, `ClothOutlineDistance`): on a part the garment's shapes lie on (data-model.md, A garment's strokes can be kept as shapes), each area the shapes paint is read from the shapes instead, less the cells a cell stroke took from what they lay and with the cells one added, each blurred as painted cells are (`CellEdits`), so an opening or a brush keeps its cells' edge and the shapes keep their outline everywhere else; an area no shape paints keeps its cells, run on under the cells the shapes of later areas touch within two cells of it, less what those shapes lay over it (`ClothBeneath`), so a body painted as cells meets a collar's shape along the shape's outline and an opening cut as cells keeps its own edge, and exact shapes and cell strokes live on one part; in the part's (u, v), as signed distances laid in order: the cloth is every painting shape's inside less what each later unpainting shape takes, an area's its own shapes' less what each later shape of another area or any unpainting shape takes. The distance near the hem is measured along and round at the part's length per u and girth, so the hem is the shapes' outline exactly, a tie's point or a strap a fraction of a cell wide included; the field is the blurred straight edge's at the same distance in cells, so seams, gathers, folds and prints read drawn cloth as they read painted cells. A shape is read only where it can change the result, past its box's distance it is skipped. Union of layers takes the most of each cloth.
- **Coverage** (`Coverage`) is one smooth function on the garment's surface: for each run the garment covers, the signed distance, in body units, inside its cloth: near the hem the distance a blurred straight edge gives for the field's value (0.8 cells times its probit, along its slope), so the hem is the field's smooth half level, and further off the distance to the painted cells' edge, the two blended between 0.35 and 0.45 of the field's range off its half level, so skin under the cloth stays shown within the hiding margin of a hem; short of where its branch says past an end it runs past (`alongOf`), and a digit of a part whose digits it follows counting as inside by the whole reach; for each part it does not cover, minus the distance into that part's share; the share-weighted sum is zero along the hem, the neck opening and the cuffs, which therefore follow smooth curves and not cells. Where a part comes out through the garment's wall with no cuff, its share counts for nothing, so the cloth runs up to it however wide it joins the body, and the hole is cut where the cloth lies inside the part itself; a cuff is a band beside it whose ease grows by the cuff's size. Before it is cut, coverage within four mesh cells of zero is eased toward a plane fitted through each vertex's neighbours, no value moving more than a quarter cell, so steps the body's coordinates leave within a cell do not reach the hem; where a drawn garment meshes finer than half the body's cell it is eased over half the body's cell, in as many more rounds as reach across it. Its seams between areas are eased the same way, their values read as distances in body cells, and its wrinkles and gathers are sized by a cell no finer than a quarter of the body's, so a strap or a collar's point folds as cloth meshed at that cell does.
- **Opening shapes are cells.** A neckline is painted, not built: an opening round where a part comes out holds every cell whose middle at rest lies within the opening's reach past the part's base ring, read across the part's axis and in each direction round it as far as the ring itself reaches that way (`RingOutline`), so the opening round a flat ring is flat too (on the parts round it no further along it than that, on the part itself all the way up), and on the front, toward the body's forward across the axis, every cell no deeper below the ring than the cut's drop at that place across: a round cut drops as a half ellipse, a V straight to its point (`ChartPlaces.opening`). The base ring is where the part leaves its flare into the body, the first of its rings from the root no wider than 1.15 of its narrowest in its first half, so a neck drawn flaring into rounded shoulders keeps its flare as cloth past the reach and the neckline follows the flare and the shoulder slope, and with the cells at the torso's top taken the cloth no longer reaches the torso's end and closes over no flat cap. A collar band round it is the opening grown by the band's width painted and the opening unpainted.
- **Openings cut the cloth.** An open end a part comes out through and a hole cut the coverage, so cells painted over an opening are not cloth. An end is reached where the cells reach its row or the one beside it (`ClothReach.reachesLow`, `reachesHigh`); a part comes out through the cloth, with a cuff or none, or hangs inside it, as read on a shell built over the region its cells paint (`clothBranch`: the rows the cloth runs over, `alongOf`, and round from the edge of the cells' first column to their last, `ClothReach.fromV`, `toV`), so a garment painted in Dress and the same cells given as boxes decide alike; a part counts as covered whole, its separate pieces hidden, where every cell of it is painted (`ClothField.paintsAll`); a shell is built over those rows; the inner layers a garment stands off are those whose cloth goes the whole way round over any of the same rows; and a fur root lies under a garment where its cloth field is over the half level (`coversAnchor`).
- **Open ends close over the cap.** Cloth reaching an end its part's run has, through which a part comes out, ends at the plane across the end's ring, moved out by the garment's ease where the end does not point down at rest, so the cloth grown over an end's cap (a torso's shoulders round a neck) covers it whole and only the part coming out, through its hole, opens it; a sleeve or trouser leg ends at its ring over the hand or foot below.
- **Hems stand off the skin.** Once a garment's coverage is read, its grown surface is lifted along its normals near its hems, neck openings and cuffs (`hemLifts`): by at least 0.15 of the body's cell less the ease there, over the band of skin the garment keeps shown under its edge and a cell past it, so a garment at fit 0 never meets the skin, or the garment under it, that it ends on; and by its `hemRoll`, full over the first fifth of its width at the hem and rounding down to the cloth over two and a half rolls and two cells, so a cuff or a rolled hem stands proud as cloth with a thickness. The surface as grown is kept beside the lifted one, and a cut made again starts from it.
- **Hem ink is the cloth's.** A garment's hems, seams and inked joins are only where it shows: one an outer garment hides draws no ink. Its silhouette, hems, seams and inked joins are drawn at its `inkWeight` of the skin's line weight, and a piece of hem as short as two cells draws, so a cuff round a thin wrist or ankle is inked.
- **Hidden skin** (`HiddenSkin`): a body triangle is hidden where its corners lie inside the garment's coverage by more than the hiding margin, so skin seen through an opening still shows. Over a part every garment there paints, the garments together cover the most of their cloth fields. Posed, the test runs again on the posed meshes, and a vertex inside a garment's coverage at rest that the pose carries outside its surface is hidden too (`PosedHiding`), read against the nearest of that garment's vertices that is cloth and lies over the same body part, so an arm lowered against a loose top is read against its sleeve, not the cloth over the torso beside it, and skin near a hem against the cloth before the hem, not the cut surface past it.
- **Kept between builds.** A painted part grows over its painted rows' steps, so painting more or less of it within the same steps, at the same cell, keeps the garment's grown surface and only cuts it again. Coverage read on the same mesh by the same reader is taken as it was (`CoverageMemo`), with its values smoothed near zero and the level cut of what shows, so an edit that leaves a garment's cover alone reads none of it again.
- **Folds in a pose.** With creases on, a garment's posed mesh rises along its normals beside each bending joint's crease and on each wrinkle's crest, before what the pose pushes through it is found, and its shade and ink are read on it as worn (look.md, Creases and wrinkles).
- Owned parts and loose ends are separate parts.

## The rig on the surface

A vertex's weights (`SurfaceSkin`) are each part's share of it times that part's own weights at the vertex's (u, v). Across a join the shares give the parent-to-child blend. A vertex keeps at most four bones. Only runs in the line of joins through the vertex's own run count (the runs it grows from, those growing from it and those growing from the same run), so a vertex on one paw never follows the other paw however near it lies. A hair vertex follows only its own run and the part it sits on. A part's own weights are the rig's for that part, with its joint and seam bands and its painted seams (data-model.md, The rig). A head's seam band is held to its neck style's share of it: all of it blended, half between and none defined, so a head on a defined neck turns whole (look.md, Necks). Garments take the body's weights at the coordinates they grow from; separate parts keep their own, except a part a garment owns bound to follow the body, which reads the body parts' weights under each of its vertices.

The rig follows the assembly, so an edit builds weights again for the new mesh and a pose only skins. Skinning gives a posed mesh with the same vertices in the same order, which everything reading the pose (posed surface work, lines, fur, colour cuts, hiding) reads by vertex. Quads whose corners belong to runs no line of joins relates are contacts, and a pose that parts them leaves them out. A vertex takes weights from the runs that hold it in its own run's line of joins, a run growing from the same run as its own only where that run holds the vertex too: two legs share their crotch, while two socks or trouser legs meeting between the ankles each follow their own leg rather than stretch across as the legs part.

## Implicit skinning

The surface and each garment are posed by implicit skinning (Vaillant, Barthe, Guennebaud, Cani, Rohmer, Wyvill, Gourmel, Paulin, "Implicit Skinning", 2013), implemented from the paper's text: linear blend first, then each vertex near a bend moved back onto the level it lay on at rest in the posed field (`ImplicitSkin`, `rig`).

- **The posed field is the rest field cut at its joints** (`PosedField`). Each member is cut into one piece per bone of its own run with a length, at planes across the joints between them, square to the mean of the two bones' directions. Neighbouring pieces overlap by the member's thickness at the joint and each end is rounded by it, so at rest their union is the member exactly and posed alone a piece ends in a round cap. A piece is read through its bone's inverse map; a member with no bones of its own never moves.
- **One blend operator** (`posedBlendShare`): a member joins its parent posed by its blend times a share read from the angle between the two posed members' gradients, the rest field's `angleShare`: none within about 10 degrees, rising to all of it where the two meet square, so a limb turned away from what it joins (the top of a lowered arm beside its shoulder) meets it by little more than their union and does not swell where the two lie close, while a crease a pose opens (a raised arm's underside against the body) fills round. It falls away again where the two surfaces face each other (from about 120 degrees to none from about 145), the gradient-based operator's contact case: a limb laid against what it joins meets it by their plain union. Members not joined meet by their plain union. The operator acts relative to the rest: a vertex reads each join, at rest, by the share the rest field gives at its rest place (`JoinAngles`), as its surface was meshed, and posed, by that share moved by as much as the operator's own share moves from the angle at rest to the angle posed (`RestJoins`). So every vertex's level is the surface it was meshed on, a fillet where a limb meets the body at a shallow angle (a foreleg beside a chest, a short sleeve beside a top) included, and a pose that turns such a join square fills it while one that lays it flat gives up its blend.
- **Which vertices move** (`ChosenVertices`): a vertex whose bones, or the pieces of the members it reads, take it to different places. Every other vertex keeps its linear blend place, and a pose that moves every bone of a surface as one refits none of it. Members with no join between them that a pose lays against each other (an arm against the hip) meet as linear blend leaves them: flattening one against the other pushes skin under the clothes over it. For the same reason a vertex that linear blend buries more than half a cell inside a member holding no share of it, as a lowered sleeve's underside sunk into the cloth over the side, stays where linear blend leaves it rather than being laid over that member's surface.
- **Projection and relaxing.** A moved vertex takes Newton steps along the posed field's gradient (at most 8, each at most 4 cells) to the level the same reader gives its rest point, so where a pose changes nothing nothing moves; it stops where the gradient turns more than 55 degrees between steps, the paper's contact stop. Then three rounds of relaxing: each moved vertex goes half way toward where its neighbours place it by mean value weights read in its rest tangent plane (`RestRings`), the paper's relaxation, which rebuilds the rest spacing rather than bunching vertices into a crease, then two steps back to its level. A vertex on a garment's border relaxes along the border instead, toward the point between its two border neighbours that its rest distances to them give, so a hem keeps its spacing through a crease.
- **Tracking from one pose to the next** (Vaillant, Guennebaud, Barthe, Wyvill, Cani, "Robust Iso-Surface Tracking for Interactive Character Skinning", 2014). Each skin keeps its last refit with the bone maps it was made for; a pose whose maps lie near them (every entry within 0.1, every offset within two cells), the next step of a drag, starts each vertex from its last offset rather than from linear blend, so most vertices start on their level, and only those the step moves further than the relaxing threshold, and their neighbours, are relaxed again, one round. A pose far from the last starts again from linear blend.
- **A per-vertex correction of linear blend.** The result is an offset per vertex from where linear blend puts it on the one rest mesh (`Refit`), with the posed field's gradient as its normal; a pose's refit can be baked as a corrective shape on the rest mesh. Ink marks carried by a vertex, anchors resolved in a pose and fur rooted on them move by the nearest vertex's offset (`VertexShifts`), and garments are refitted to their own fields the same way before they are hung and hidden.
- **Timing.** The drag tool's `pose-report=true` prints each stage of the posed surface node by gesture: linear blend, choosing, projecting, relaxing and writing per surface, per garment, hiding, creases and the whole node, with the field reads counted (`PoseBreakdown`); `skinning=bones` poses by linear blend alone.

## Pose keys

Some poses need shaping that skinning cannot give: a raised arm's fuller deltoid, a T-pose shoulder top drawn as one curve. A **pose key** is the body's shape corrected in one pose and blended in as a pose nears it, pose space deformation (Lewis, Cordner and Fong, "Pose Space Deformation", 2000) built from the paper's text: corrections are made in the pose, stored at rest and skinned from there, and interpolated over pose space by radial basis functions with the rest as a sample that corrects nothing.

- **Stored as part settings, not points** (`PoseKey`, `Character.poseKeys`, document; data-model.md). A key holds its name, its pose (the bones it turns, over the rest) and its changes: per part, each view's outline scaled across its axis by a run of `(x, scale)` places along it, each side of the axis by its own run (`OutlineScale`), and its join's blend scaled, a part with no blend of its own scaling the one the build gives its join. The changes are read over the part's own settings at build time (`keyed`, `KeyedFields`, `character/keys`), so an edit of the rest shape keeps every key.
- **A shape per key** (`PoseKeyBuilder`, `rig`). For each key the character's field is built twice through the real slice build and assembly, as it is and with the key's changes (`KeyedFields`). The one rest surface is posed in the key's pose as the app poses it (linear blend refitted by implicit skinning); each vertex near a changed part's members moves from the level it lies on in the plain posed field to that level in the keyed posed field (Newton steps along the keyed field's gradient, both fields read with the joins' gradient shares, as implicit skinning reads them), and the move and the normal's turn are taken back to rest through the inverse of the vertex's blended linear map. The result is a corrective shape on the one rest mesh (`KeyShape`: moved vertices, offsets, normal turns), the form a glTF morph target takes.
- **Building it cheaply.** Each vertex reads only the members whose box comes near it at rest or, posed in the key's pose, near where the pose takes it, and their parents (`ReadSets`); a vertex where no changed member reads differently, and no changed blend reaches, is passed over with no Newton step; the vertices are worked in parallel. The builder lives with the session and keeps, from one build to the next, the part surfaces the fields are built from (`KeptSurfaces`, the parts any build asks for made at once in parallel, those no build asked for let go) and the run solids, so an edit builds again only the parts it changed and an unchanged run is the same member.
- **Only the keys an edit reaches.** Each key's shape is kept with what it read (`KeyReads`): the plain field's members its vertices read, and the map each bone its moved vertices hang on takes in the key's pose. The next build keeps a key, unchanged itself, when every member it read is the same member, joined the same way, its bones take the same maps, and the new mesh keeps every vertex it moves (`SurfaceMesh.change`), each on the same bones by the same weights: the shape is carried onto the new mesh's vertices and nothing is built for it. An edit of the head keeps a knee key; an edit of the arm builds the arm's keys again.
- **Posing never waits for a key.** The keys are a node of the session's graph after the skin, made again only when the skin or what the keys read of the character changes; a pose does not rebuild them. The posed surface reads the last keys the node settled on, none before the first, so it poses at once: while the keys of a new surface are being built it shows the last shapes carried onto the new mesh's vertices (`HeldKeys.onto`), and it poses again with the new shapes when they come. The graph is settled only once they have.
- **Weights by joint angle** (`keyWeights`). Each side of the body takes each key by its own bones: the bones the key's pose names on that side and on the middle, a key naming one side only driving the other through each bone's twin. Keys driven by the same bones are interpolated together: Gaussian radial basis functions of the distance between the bones' places in pose space, each bone's turn as its pose gives it (so two poses lie as far apart as the bone's turns, whichever of hinge, lean, turn and twist give them), as wide as the mean distance from each sample to its nearest other, solved so each key reads 1 at its own place and 0 at every other key's and at the rest. A weight below 0 reads 0, and a group's weights, the rest's among them, are shared out to sum to 1, so an arm held level between a T key and a raised-forward key takes the two of them rather than fading toward the rest. A vertex takes its side's weight, eased across a band 0.02 wide over the body's middle (`HeldKeys`).
- **Applied when posing** (`posedSurface`'s `correction`). After linear blend and the implicit refit, each vertex a key moves moves by its weighed rest offset skinned as the vertex is (the vertex blended from its rest place plus the offset, less the vertex blended from its rest place), and its normal, line normal and the marks it carries turn and move with it. A garment vertex takes the body's correction from the body's corners round where it lies at rest, those within two cells past the nearest, weighed by inverse squared distance, so across a crease it takes the move of the skin on both sides; and along its own normal at least as far out as the furthest of them moves out, so no skin a key moves out shows through it (`GarmentCorrections`). At the rest every weight is 0 and nothing moves; no key costs nothing.
- **Drawing a key.** A recipe's key is drawn as a figure outline redrawn in the key's pose (parts.md, Pose keys) and fitted to the changes, or given as the changes themselves.

## The derived-data graph

| Node | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| Slice row (per part) | Drawing to slices |
| Part mesh (per part) | The part's patches as a lofted mesh: what separate parts draw, and what sockets, anchors and the field's solids are swept from |
| Assembly | Parts placed by their sockets on each other's swept surfaces, then dressed and given the character's membranes, and a planned part on each open socket from its template with nothing drawn; those planned surfaces depend only on template, side, lean and length, so every session in the process shares them (`PlannedSurfaces`) |
| Character field | Members, blends and parents from the assembly, keyed by each member's solid and place |
| Character mesh and surface | The mesher's blocks and member channels; vertices, quads, coordinates, shares, shading normals, lines (`characterMeshNode`, `characterSurfaceNode`) |
| Garment surfaces | Per worn garment, its field and mesh over the covered blocks and its coverage cut, innermost first |
| Mouth recesses | Per mouth holder, from its surface work on its surface: what its open mouths cut into it, which the character mesh reads |
| Surface work | Anchors resolved on the character field; it does not wait on the mesh |
| Colour | Shapes cut on the surface mesh per block, by coordinates or by surface curves |
| Lines | The surface, the garments' meshes and the separate parts' meshes |
| Rig and posed | Weights per surface vertex from shares and coordinates; the pose keys' shapes on the rest surface; the posed surface, garments and separate parts, with their creases and wrinkles and the folds they raise on cloth (look.md, Creases and wrinkles) |

A vertex's identity is its cell, so a block an edit did not reach keeps every vertex and every cache kept per block.

## Template sampling

A field template's `topology` block (`rings`, `segments`, `focus`) sets how finely the drawing is sampled into the part's slices and so into its solid; it is not the mesh's topology. Changing the counts changes the solid only by sampling, so a template version that changes them does not move anchors, which are template coordinates and not vertices. `focus` samples a stretch of u more finely, as a face needs. A separate part's counts are the grid of its own mesh.
