# Look

The target is comic line work combined with cel shading, chosen per scene. The reference pages do five things:

- Line work carries the style: a thick dark outer outline, thinner interior lines, lines that taper at their ends.
- Flat color, little shading. The main depth cue is that far legs and the far ear are a darker tone.
- Fur is an outline shape. Chest ruffs, ear fluff and tail tips are jagged edges, not textures.
- Faces are graphic marks. Eyes, mouth lines, sweat drops and shock lines change freely between panels.
- Only characters are outlined. Backgrounds are painted, cool and soft, so warm outlined characters stand out.

A plain 3D toon shader gets the fills and an outline. It cannot do fur edges, faces or tapered interior lines. So every character has two layers:

1. **A 3D body underneath.** Solid colored shapes that are posed, lit and depth-sorted. This is what lets you turn a leg and see the underside of the paw.
2. **2D marks on top.** Fur strokes, interior lines, eyes and mouth, drawn as strokes on the body's surface. A mark can have versions for a few angles that switch as the view turns. They follow the pose and stay readable from the camera.

## Shading

Chosen per object, within limits the scene sets. Flat characters in a shaded world is the house look.

| Mode | Look | Depth cue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flat | One color per part | Far limbs darkened, outline weight |
| Two-tone | Lit color and one shadow color, hard edge | Light direction, plus outline weight |

In two-tone, each part's shadow falls as a clean drawn shape: the shader reads each slice's proxy normal (the normal of its ellipse, blended along the axis) instead of the mesh normal, which is the edited-normals technique with no hand editing. Automatic from the light; no painted shadows, no lookup image. The slice build computes the proxy normal once, in the ring's proxy normal frame, and carries it to a separate part's mesh per vertex and to the character's surface through each vertex's shares (see [surface.md](surface.md), Shading). A ring's slope along the axis turns its normals toward the axis, so a rounded end shades round. A patch with no slices, such as a pad, shades by its mesh normal. On top of that, each part's shading normals are smoothed as one field over the whole part, every patch together: each vertex takes the mean of the normals within a ball as wide as 0.8 of the part's thinnest extent, weighted by a Gaussian of distance, by the area round each neighbour and by how far its normal agrees, so a bust, a cheek or a jaw drawn into a part leaves the terminator one clean curve following the large form, and the facing sides of two fingers or a thin sheet never mix. The mesh's own normals, which outlines and contours read, are untouched. Garments and loose ends are smoothed the same way. The process keeps the meshes it smoothed lately, so a part built again the same way, in another session, on the other side or after an undo, takes its smoothed normals from there (`SmoothedMeshes`). The light comes from in front of the viewer, a little above and from their left: (0.7, 0.5, -0.5) in body space for a camera in front of the body (`PartMaterial.DEFAULT_TO_LIGHT`), turned round the body's up with the camera (`ObjectShading.lightFor`), so a side or back view is lit as the front is and a back view does not fall in shade. A view from straight above or below keeps the front's light. A shadow that would show only as a sliver along the silhouette is drawn lit (`tone` in `TONE_WGSL`): across the surface from the point facing the camera through a shaded point to the silhouette, the shade starts where that arc turns from the light, and where that leaves under 0.3 of the arc's width on screen the point is lit, so two-tone shows shadow as an area that follows the form, as on a side lit from behind or below, and never as a thin strip down a limb's or a torso's edge. A character's `light` can instead fix it to the body (`body`): lit from in front of the character whichever way it is seen.

The shadow tone is the slot's own, set by its material (below): a material can give a shadow color of its own or name another slot whose color is the shadow, and with neither its color shifts by how saturated it is (`shadeShifted`) rather than multiplying down, since a pale slot multiplied turns grey: a saturated color shifts warm, blue falling fastest and red slowest, and a pale, grey or white one shifts cool, green and red falling a little faster than blue, so white in shade is a light lilac grey and never tan. Saturation (most channel less least, over most) under 0.06 shifts all cool and over 0.22 all warm, eased between, so skin and coloured cloth keep the warm shift. A material may set how deep the shift goes and fix it cool or warm whatever the saturation. A head's skull and muzzle are one part with overlapping runs, so across the overlap their proxy normals blend and one shadow edge runs over both. A proxy normal reads the drawing's size, not a run's sinking inside another, so hiding a stretch does not turn the shading. Where a run covers another the covered one neither shows color nor fights it: the surface hands over at the middle of the overlap, the later run rising from inside to the drawing's size there and the earlier sinking inside right after, so one line divides them and either's color past that line lies out of sight. Marks take no shadow tone.

Far parts darken automatically by side: paired parts on the side facing away from the camera get the same shift of their color and of their shadow, by the character's `farSide` share, 18% unless set, up to a half. It ramps in as the camera leaves head-on, from a fiftieth of the way off the body's plane to about a fifth, so an orbit passes from one side to the other with no step in tone. Subtle in the references, so it can be switched off. The choice of flat or two-tone, of far parts darker and by how much, and of the light is stored on the character (`CharacterShading`); the Color drawer's Shading section sets them, and recipe renders read them, always in two-tone.

**Materials.** Every palette slot has a toon material (`SlotMaterial` on `PaletteSlot`), so everything painted in the slot, a part's base, a filled area, a region, a brush stroke, fur, a garment or its paint, takes the finish. A material is a preset and the settings it changes; a slot with none is matte. Its settings (`MaterialLook`):

| Setting | Does |
| --- | --- |
| `steps` | How many flat tones, 1 to 3: one is all lit; two is lit and shadow, as above; three splits the lit side where it turns under 0.45 of the way to the light into a middle tone halfway to the shadow |
| `shadow`, `shadowSlot`, `shadowDepth`, `shadowShift` | The shadow tone: a color of its own, else another slot's color, else its color shifted darker by the depth (the character's 0.24 where none) cool, warm or by saturation |
| `rim` | A light edge along the silhouette on the side turned from the light: its width, the share of the way in from the silhouette toward the middle it covers on a round form, and its color, the slot's lifted toward white where none |
| `shine` | A shine from the light where the surface faces halfway between the light and the viewer: a small round `spot`, a broad `band` stretched across the light's way on screen with a darker reflection just outside it, or a thin `streak` across it; its size and its color, near white in the slot's hue where none. Each is a hard flat shape, read from the proxy normal, so on a long part it runs along the part as a stripe |
| `seeThrough` | As See-through slots and See-through cloth say |
| `glow` | Its color whatever the light, no shadow and no far side, its middle, where the surface faces the viewer, lifted toward white by its strength; with a halo, two flat rings of its color round its silhouette, the outer as wide as the halo and the inner half that, each at 0.6 of its strength, drawn after everything else in the view so they tint what lies round it and never cover what stands in front |

The presets: matte (the default), skin and cloth, all three two tones exactly as a slot with no material draws; plastic, two tones and a sharp round spot; metal, three hard tones, a deeper shadow, a high-contrast band and a bright rim, as drawn toon metal reads; glass, see-through at 0.3 with a streak and a thin rim, both drawn whole over the see-through fill; glow, a glow with a sixteen pixel halo. A slot shows a preset's name in the app as its default: skin for the base slot of the body's root part, cloth for a slot a garment is coloured in, matte otherwise; they draw alike. The renderer takes a material as its draw's tones and finish (`tonesOf`, `Finish`): the lit and shadow colors, far side and all, are worked out on the CPU, and the finish's settings go to the fill and color layer shaders (`FINISH_WGSL`), which keep the two-tone path untouched where a slot's finish is plain and no key light is set. So characters that set no material draw pixel for pixel as before, and a material edit is a repaint: the palette resolves at draw time and nothing in the graph reads it but a garment's see-through cloth.

**Key light.** A character's shading may name a key light (`CharacterShading.key`), the shot's, a way in body space fixed to the body. With one, a surface is in shadow only where neither it nor the view's light reaches, and shines come from it, so a plastic spot and a metal band stay on the side toward it as the camera turns; with none, shines come from the view's light. See-through cloth shades by the view's light alone.

**Export.** A Squash file keeps a slot's material as its preset's name and the settings it changes, and a recipe writes it as the name alone where nothing changes. Materials are settings, never baked color, so an engine can draw them its own way: steps and the shadow map to VRM MToon's shade color and shade sharpness, the rim to its rim, the ink to its outline and a glow to its emission; in plain glTF, metal is metallic with a low roughness, plastic a low roughness, glass transmission and a glow emission.

**See-through slots.** A slot's material may be see-through (`seeThrough`), 0 to 1, how much of what lies behind it a part coloured in it hides; a slot whose material sets none, as with no material, is whole. A part of its own, outside the one surface, whose base slot has one draws at that opacity, as a lens over painted eyes: the renderer draws a view's whole draws first and those below full opacity after them, each laying its depth before it blends, so the eyes and the head show through it and its own far side does not. Its painted layers do not draw; parts within the one surface stay whole. Cloth coloured in such a slot is sheer at that opacity (below).

**See-through cloth.** An area of a garment may be sheer, with an opacity of its own, 0 to 1, times its slot's (`GarmentArea.opacity`, the slot's from its material), or knitted as a net (`GarmentArea.net`, `ClothNet`: diamond or square, the threads' spacing and width in body units and their slant in degrees); a garment with any such area is see-through and hides no skin and no inner garment (`HiddenSkin`), and its areas whole in another slot still draw as colour layers over it, so a stocking's band, toe or seam stays solid. Its cloth is read at each vertex of its surface (`SeeThroughCloth`): the area holding it most, that area's opacity and net, and the net laid on the body under it (`NetLayout`): each run under the garment read by the axis through its rings (`RunAxis`), its distance along the axis and its turn round it, turned and moved to meet its parent's run over the vertices both hold, running on away from the join, and a vertex several runs hold taking their places blended by their shares, then relaxed over a band six mesh sides round each join, so a leg's net runs on over the ankle onto the foot with no step and its cells change shape evenly across the bend. Each of the net's two sets of threads crosses round a whole number of times, counted on the girth of the run nearest the root that is within twice the run's own, so the net meets itself all the way round, and stretches with the skin in every pose since the vertices carry their place. The place is trusted as far as the run's girth there does not squeeze the threads together, as near a pole. The renderer draws see-through cloth (`ClothDraw`, `ClothPass`) after the view's ribbons, laying its depth before it blends so its own far side never shows, pulled 0.02 toward the camera with the colour layers and ribbons on it so skin a pose pushes past it stays under it, in the tones of the area holding each vertex darkened on a far side as the surface is, at an opacity that hides more as the cloth turns from the viewer: the facing opacity stacked as many times as one over the square of the cosine of its slant, so a sheer leg shows the skin's colour and shading tinted by the cloth and darkens over a broad band toward its silhouette. A net's coverage at a pixel is each set's threads averaged over the pixel's span along the net, the two sets crossed, fading to their mean coverage as a pixel spans a tenth to a quarter of the spacing, where a pixel spans two runs' counts, and where the net's place is not trusted, so a net too fine to draw reads as an even tone. The ribbons drawn before it show through it softened, as the body's ink under a stocking; they draw again after it, so a line in front of the cloth stays whole, and then the see-through garments' own contour, hems, seams, creases and prints draw over it (`RibbonDraw.overCloth`). The Dress inspector's garment section holds Sheer an area and Net an area, each with a row per area: the opacity, or the pattern, hole, thread and angle.

**Base color first.** A character has a palette of named slots (fur, cream, pads, nose, inner ear, ink by default, from the body plan), and every part carries a base slot, so blocking out the character is choosing a slot per part before any painting: Fill on a part sets its base slot, and the Parts tree shows each part's base color in its row swatch. Template blocks with a `color` field (pads, nose leather, inner ear) name a slot, so they are right as soon as the slot is. Editing a slot recolors everything that names it. Mirrored pairs share base slots like everything else. The default palette is the body plan's, not a constant in code.

No image textures. Color is painted in sections, and nothing about it depends on the mesh, so no one thinks about vertices:

- **Fill.** The bucket colors a whole section in one click. A section is a part, an area its template names (a paw's pads, an ear's inside, a chest, a tail's tip), or the closed shape a region border makes. The section stores a palette slot. A click fills what shows under it: a region, a filled area or a colored block, and otherwise the part. Set to Area, it also reaches an area the template names there that nothing has filled. The Color drawer lists the selected part's named sections by swatch.
- **Brush.** A section can also be partly painted. A brush stroke is stored in anchor space with its slot and softness and keeps its own edge, however few rings or segments lie under it. Softness runs from 0, a hard edge, to 1, a fade across the whole radius, for a cheek blush, a blend from fur into a cream belly or fur tips that fade.
- **Region border.** A curve drawn on the surface makes a crisp edge and a new section to fill. Each border can be inked or not.

Sections layer in a fixed order: the part's base slot, then filled areas, then regions, then brush strokes in the order drawn. Color is rendered as vector shapes cut to the mesh, with no color grid and no image or lookup texture at any resolution, so a hard edge stays crisp and a soft one smooth at any zoom, and neither follows a triangle edge. Anchors, areas and slots are the only stored form, and the shapes and their cuts are derived from them.

**Color is vector.** Every section and stroke is a shape in its patch's u and v: a brush stroke is its anchors and radius, swept to a closed outline, or with a softness a fade around them; a region is its border, a closed curve smooth through its anchors but at its corners (on a part joined into the one surface, straight between them only when the region is set `curved: false`); a filled area is the template's rectangle; a part's base is the whole patch, drawn by the mesh itself.

- **Flattening.** Curves flatten to a quarter of a pixel at the largest view's scale, in doubling steps, and flatten again only when the zoom has moved a step up or two steps down, so there is no resolution setting.
- **Shared borders.** A stretch of a region's border between corners is laid from its own anchors and their neighbours short of a corner alone, and comes out the same in either direction. Where two regions on a holder share an anchor and their borders part, the anchor is a corner of both, so two regions drawn through the same anchors meet along one edge with no gap and no overlap, sharp where three colours meet.
- **Cutting.** Each shape is cut through the triangles of the surface's mesh by a per-vertex function, the shape's signed distance in a part's u and v or its distance from a curve laid on the surface, so every piece lies in one of the mesh's own triangles and a vertex there is interpolated as the GPU interpolates it. The edges follow the shape and not the grid, so a hard edge stays crisp at any zoom. Filled means the non-zero rule, so a stroke crossing itself is one fill, and each vertex keeps its part, u and v for skinning.
- **Where a shape reaches.** A shape wraps across the v seam of a closed run, and near a pole it is tested in the pole's flat chart. A shape within one part is cut where that part holds a share of the surface; a border that crosses to another part arrives as anchors resolved on the field, and the mesh is cut along the curve laid between them, the inside the side the border's order gives. A brush measures its radius in space, so it crosses a join as it crosses the middle of a part, and keeps one width across a socket, as a share of the axis of the part that holds it. A part's base colour is where its share is the largest, cut where its share equals its neighbour's. A mirrored side cuts the same u and v on the mirrored part.
- **Fading.** A soft stroke's opacity is a field over its patch: full within its core, which softness leaves at the radius times one less the softness and never under a thirty-second of the radius, nothing past its radius, and linear in the distance to its curve between. The distance is in space, so a seam or a pole needs no case, and it is held from reaching further around v than a hard brush's edge unless a pole is in reach, which the brush then covers. Each of the mesh's triangles the brush reaches is split along its own edges until opacity runs straight across each piece. Whether an edge splits depends on the edge alone, so neighbours meet vertex to vertex, and each place is covered once however the stroke crosses itself. A stroke going on onto another surface keeps its strength past that end by a radius, along its last direction, so no dip shows at a socket as long as its anchors either side of the crossing lie within a radius of it, which the tools' drawing holds by finding where the pointer crossed. Only the far ends of the whole stroke round off. Each vertex carries its opacity, so the fade is geometry with no lookup, the GPU interpolates it with no steps at any zoom, and the blend runs on the surface's own values, so it is linear in sRGB. It does not depend on the zoom, except that a fade narrower than twice the flattening tolerance is a hard edge until a closer zoom resolves it.
- **Layers.** Later layers overdraw earlier ones in the order above, and solid same-slot neighbours on one patch merge. A soft stroke stays its own layer, so where fades overlap they build up to full color and a hard stroke over a soft one reads by order. A soft layer tones like a hard one, so the shadow edge is one line through it. A layer writes no depth and shows only faces toward the camera. It sits a sliver of a pixel toward the camera, more where the surface turns away and never under a few steps of the depth buffer, so it cannot z-fight its mesh at any orbit and fails against anything truly in front of it.
- **Slots.** A slot resolves to a color at draw time, so a palette change cuts nothing. A base slot the palette does not hold, or none, shows the look's own part color; a layer whose slot the palette does not hold is not drawn, so what is under it shows.

## Lines

Both modes share the same line rules: thick on the silhouette, thin inside, a line wherever one part overlaps another. The drawn width of an outline means nothing; line rules set rendered weight, and a width tool adjusts it along an outline afterwards. A line-weight adjustment lives on the body surface, so wherever that area is on the silhouette, from any angle, it uses the adjusted weight.

**Where parts meet.** The parts in the field are one surface: a part on a socket joins the part it sits on by a smooth union, its radius the part's `blend`, else its template's join, else wide enough to round the root over as thick as the part is ([surface.md](surface.md), The field), and the join is inked where the two parts' shares of the surface are equal, unless the part's `join.ink` parameter is 0. A separate part meets the part it sits on by overlap, and its crease is found where its surface crosses out of its parent's (`socketCrease`).

An overlap is inked by default, which is also how the reference draws a leg against a body. A separate part whose join is not inked draws no overlap crease either (parts.md, Hair). A lock group's locks have no join crease: where a lock lies over the face or another group, its contour drawn at the interior weight is the crease, and where it lies over a lock of its own group it draws none, its interior line lying just behind it, save that each lock of a crown draws its edge over the next so a whorl reads as a spiral (parts.md, Hair locks). A parting's strip of scalp draws no line over the hair round it: it shows as a thin line of its colour between the locks. An overlap's crease is the curve on the part where it leaves its parent, found on the part's grid, its steps from one grid edge to the next calmed along the surface, and draws as an even ribbon. A graph node finds it, so it follows the assembly and a surface edit does not find it again. A garment's hem is the open edge of its mesh, where its faces show, on by default with the garment's hem switch; a cuff's far edge and where it comes out of its branch are inked with the garment's cuff switch and the through change's own; where a part comes through a branch with no cuff, with the hem switch; and a join, where a branch comes out of the branch over its part's parent, only with that join's switch.

**The two weights.** Every part's contour draws as one ribbon twice: in the back slice of the depth buffer, where only what clears the whole figure shows it, and in the scene at the interior weight, so a part standing clear of another has the thin line. The thin line lies half inside the edge and the thick one shows only outside it, so together they measure the silhouette weight across. A selection in a locked pane does the same in the depth just behind and just inside the front slice, so it reads over what is ghosted around it. Pads are built with enough segments that a nose is not a polygon. A garment's contour, its hems and its seams draw at its ink weight, a share of the weights here, 0.7 where it sets none, so cloth reads lighter than the body it covers.

**The contour.** A contour runs along the edges of a mesh between a triangle facing the viewer and one facing away, interpolated across faces from each vertex's facing, on the character's surface, the garments' meshes and the separate parts' meshes. It runs on no edge where shown faces stop at hidden ones. The body's surface has no end rings, buried corners or cast points, so no rule for them exists. The ribbon's own curve through those points takes off the corners of the mesh, with the points held a couple of pixels apart on screen, since a stretch running along the view would otherwise leave the ribbon no direction. A connected piece thins with its length on screen below a short reach, so a small one grows from nothing rather than popping. A contour is camera work, so the frame finds it from the graph's grids, once per camera that moves.

**Line rules and line weights.** Silhouette, interior and taper are the character's, set by command; a mark with no width of its own narrows over its share of taper at each end. A line weight is a stroke of anchors on the surface with a radius and an amount. Every line crossing it, contour, crease or mark, takes the added weight at full strength within most of the radius, easing to none at its edge, and it reaches only the parts its anchors name.

## Necks

A character's neck style (`Character.neck`, `NeckStyle`; `SetNeckStyle` from the Neck row of a head's part drawer, a recipe's `neck`) says how its head meets its neck, from no neck to a slim one:

| Style | Look | The head's join |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blended | No visible neck: the head flows into the neck and shoulders in one smooth curve | Its default blend, its template's ink |
| Between | A short neck under a soft jaw line | 0.4 of its default blend, unlined, half its seam band; a shade under the chin reaching 0.1 of the head's least extent, and at least four body cells |
| Defined | A slim neck under the head's own closed outline, the jaw inked across the neck | 0.08 of its default blend, inked, no seam band; a shade under the chin reaching 0.15 of the head's least extent, and at least four body cells |

The style reaches the head as its join's `join.neck` placement value (`NeckJoin`): every part whose role is head and that sits on a socket carries it, and nothing else reads it. A part's own `blend` and `inkJoin` still win over the style's.

- **The jaw line is the head's own outline.** A defined head is drawn closed under its jaw, with the neck rising into it and sitting back under it, so seen from the front the head's outline lies in front of the neck and its contour runs across the neck, the interior line where the neck stands behind it; no ink rule draws it. The head joins the neck by a blend small enough to round the crease under the jaw within a cell or two, so the two never step apart or open a gap, and its join is inked where it shows from below and the side. How slim the neck is, how long and how far back it sits are the recipe's: its width, length and socket.
- **The head turns whole.** A defined head keeps none of its seam band (surface.md, The rig on the surface), so nothing of its underside takes the neck's bone and a nod or a turn carries the jaw's outline with the head; the bend lies in the neck under it, by the neck's rim band round the head's socket. A head between keeps half its band.
- **A shade under the chin** (`ChinShade`). Each vertex of the parts the head sits on, the neck and on down, takes, by their share of the surface there, how far inside the head's own solid the places up and a little forward from it lie (body space (0.35, 1, 0), the light's way from above), eased over the head's edge, whole over the nearest 0.45 of the reach and gone at the reach; the head and anything on it take none. The values are smoothed four rounds over the mesh's neighbours, so the border is one smooth curve and no stray tone stays on the throat. They are read at rest and carried by vertex, so posed the shade rides the neck. It draws as a crease layer (`castShadeTones`): where it is at least a half, its edge smoothed over a pixel, it multiplies what is under it by what takes the surface's lit color to its shadow color, and in a two-tone shadow by nothing, so it is the skin's own shadow tone over any paint and never darkened twice.

## Creases and wrinkles

A bending joint creases on its inner side, as toon reference sheets draw it: a short ink line and a band of shade, faint at rest where a sheet draws one, deepening and lengthening as the joint bends. Cloth creases at the same joints with a small fold either side, wrinkles where a pose bunches it, and gathers in a few short folds just above its hems and cuffs. Each is short and tapered, thick in its middle and pointed at its ends, never the length of a limb or a torso, and a straight limb at rest shows little. Everything is ink, a shade band and, on cloth, a little real fold shape; nothing is painted. The character's `creases` (`CharacterCreases`) hold `skin`, how strongly the body creases, `cloth`, how strongly every garment does, and `rest`, the share of a full crease a joint shows at rest (0.3); both are 1 by default, so every character creases until a strength is set to 0, off, and 2 is the most. Each garment's own `creases`, `wrinkles` and `drape` (1 each) multiply `cloth`.

**Which joints crease** (`creaseJoints`). Every joint of a part whose role creases, with a bone of its own past it at least half the joint's radius long, so a joint inside its neighbour's crease does not crease twice; a hand's, a foot's and a head's only where they sit on their parent part (wrist, ankle, the neck under a head); no digit's and no jaw's. The role sets its strength: arm and leg 1, neck and foot 0.8, hand, body and spine 0.7, head 0.6. Hair lies over the body and never creases with it: neither a joint's radius nor its reach counts a vertex a hair part wins, nor a quad's middle any of whose corners one wins (`skinCreasable`). A joint's radius is the middle distance from its axis of the body's corners near its plane that its bones weigh. Its rest side, where it creases at rest, is the inside of the bend it rests in where a limb's joint rests bent between 20 and 150 degrees off its parent (an ankle under a foot that points forward); else, at the base of a leg hanging straight down from an upright body or spine (both within about 53 degrees of straight), the back, under the seat; else the side a joint whose limits reach much further one way closes toward (an elbow, a knee, a shoulder); else, on a neck, the front. A joint with none creases only once bent.

**In a pose** (`PosedCreaseJoint`). The crease lies on the plane through the posed pivot that splits the joint's angle evenly, between its parent's direction where it rests bent, else its own rest direction carried by its parent, and its posed direction. Its inner side is the side it closes toward once bent past 4 to 20 degrees from rest, eased from its rest side; bent the other way, its rest crease opens and the other side creases. Its amount is its strength times the larger of its rest share, gone as it opens, and its depth, which eases from 0 at rest to 1 at a 70 degree bend. Its width along the limb is 0.2 of its radius at rest, 0.4 at its deepest, and never less than 1.5 of the body's cells at rest and 2.5 at its deepest, so a thin elbow's crease still spans enough of the mesh to draw. It reaches 28 degrees round the limb either side of its inner side at rest; at its deepest 78 on skin, full over the middle third of that, and 103 on cloth, full over three quarters, so a bent knee's or elbow's folds wrap onto the limb's sides and show from the front and the side; its middle stretch, where the lines beside it run, is the inner 0.7 of that. A vertex lies on the crease where it lies within 2.6 radii of the pivot at rest, the joint's bones weigh it at least 0.2 and its own bone at least 0.04, so the bend moves it (at a shoulder at least 0.4: its pivot lies deep in the trunk, so a raised arm's plane meets the surface well inside the arm's root, and the base of a collar, which the arm barely moves, takes no shoulder crease), and it lies between a quarter of the radius and 2.8 radii from the axis.

**Shade and ink** (`JointCreaseField`, `CreaseInk`). Each vertex of the drawn mesh carries a shade, the crease's strength round the limb there times a Gaussian along it, 1.8 times narrower than its width, so the band is a slim wedge about the ink. On skin the shade is 0.85 of that and on cloth all of it, so a band shows once a joint bends well past 45 degrees and never at rest; on cloth it fades out from 1.8 to 2.6 of the joint's radius from its axis, so cloth standing well off a joint folds there instead of taking a band. A crease layer draws it over the surface's paint and over each garment's: where the shade is at least a half it multiplies the colour under it by what shifts the surface's or the garment's own colour 0.3 into its shade (`creaseTones`), so a crease is that colour a little darker, lighter than the ink: a warmer, darker tone on skin and on saturated cloth, a cool grey on white and pale cloth, never tan; in a two-tone shadow by three quarters of that, so a band in shade is not darkened twice over. Its edge is smoothed over a pixel, so a band is a crisp toon shape that tapers to its ends and widens as the joint bends, darkening any colour or paint alike. An island of band of fewer than eight vertices is lowered under the half (`withoutSpecks`), so no band shows as a speck. At rest the shade stays under the half, so a rest crease on skin is ink alone. The ink is the zero curve of each crease's distance along the limb, across triangles whose corners lie on one crease, weighted by its strength round the limb, so it tapers to nothing at its ends; past a strength of 0.6 on skin and 0.3 on cloth over its middle stretch, a shorter line runs 1.4 widths either side of it at up to 0.75 of its weight, so a well bent joint on cloth shows three short tapered folds. Skin's ink is 0.7 of the crease weight, so skin creases stay subtle; cloth's is twice it, times the garment's ink weight, so its folds read at a whole figure's size, and is drawn in the cloth's own hue at 0.45 of its colour (`foldLineColor`), lighter than the ink, or on dark cloth (luminance under 0.2) lifted a third of the way to white, a thin highlight. Skin's is drawn in the ink. Ink is drawn only where the surface faces the viewer and shows, and a piece shorter than two and a half of the body's cells is left out.

**Cloth.** A garment creases at the same joints at `cloth` times its `creases`, showing the same rest share as skin, so a straight limb at rest shows only a faint line where a sheet draws one. Either side of each crease its posed mesh rises along its normals by up to 0.07 of the joint's radius, a ridge 1.3 widths off the crease's middle, so cloth bunches into a fold beside the crease and never sinks into the skin. A garment's looseness is its fit over 2.5 of the body's cells, held from 0.25 to 1.5 and taken at most 1; it scales its wrinkles and gathers from 0.85 for tight cloth to 1 for loose, so jeans still fold at a bent knee and bunch above their cuffs.

- **Wrinkles** (`clothBunching`): each corner's bunching is the mean of how much its mesh's edges shorten from rest in the pose, smoothed six times over its neighbours and eased from 6% to 25%, so it gathers in patches and not specks. Wrinkles are a wave along the rig's chains of bones, its distance carried across each joint, five of the garment's cells from crest to crest (its mesh's cell, no finer than a quarter of the body's, which sizes its gathers too), its phase wandering by smooth noise in space by up to 0.8 radians, so its crests run across the limb unbroken, across the way the cloth bunches, and no two repeat. Their envelope is the bunching times `wrinkles`, the looseness's share and `cloth`; each crest raises a fold of up to 0.6 of a cell.
- **Gathers** (`ClothPattern`): a part with a hem below the cloth at rest (down for a part resting more upright than flat, else toward its far end) has its hem where along its axis the vertices beside that edge lie. Above it, folds round the part, eleven cells apart round a body or spine and eight round a limb, a whole number of them round its middle girth, wandering by up to 0.6 radians, rise from a tenth of a gather above the hem to full at 0.45 and are gone by 1.1, clear of the cloth's edges by half a cell to two and a half, so each is a short fold thick in its middle and pointed at both ends, and a sweater gathers above its ribbed hem and cuffs and not under its neck. Slow noise, one and a half folds across, sets each fold's strength, from none to full, so a hem gathers in a few folds of different strengths rather than an even ring. Their envelope is the pattern times `drape`, `cloth` and the tight share.
- **A fold's shade and ink** (`SurfaceCreasing`): its envelope is eased in from 0.35 to 1, so a faint fold shows nothing. Its band is a sliver from its crease, where its phase is a whole number of turns, to 0.8 radians of its wave times the envelope past it, so each fold is one slim shape beside its ink that narrows to nothing where its envelope fades. Its ink runs along the crease only where the envelope passes 0.7, full by 1, so only the strongest folds are inked, and a crease whose ink nowhere reaches 0.3 is left out. A fold's band is its own layer, a lighter shift of the cloth's colour than a crease's (0.16, `foldTones`), so folds read soft; its joint creases keep theirs. Neither band keeps an island under eight vertices.

**Where it is worked out.** In the posed surface node, after skinning (see surface.md, The derived-data graph): the joints, their reach over the body and each garment, and each garment's pattern are read once a skin and kept while the rig and the surfaces are the same (`CreaseRig`); each pose reads its joints, the garments' bunching and folds before what the pose pushes through a garment is found, then the shade and ink on what is worn (`PoseCreasing`). A garment's strengths are not part of what its cut reads (`Garment.unprinted`), so changing one, or the character's, poses again and grows nothing. Panes that show the pose draw creases; locked panes at rest do not.

## Marks

Fur is tufts standing on the surface, which show where they break the silhouette. A fur stroke is its own kind of mark, a root on the surface and nothing else: the grain says which way it runs, and the part's fur rule how it shows.

An inked line along the grain reads as a loose hair against a thick silhouette, so a fur stroke is a tapered wedge filled in the slot under it, with ink on its edge, whose tip breaks the silhouette. A sawtooth drawn into an outline is swept round the part as a stack of ridges, which reads as tufts only side on and as a segmented body at three-quarter, so an outline carries a part's mass and its tufts are these strokes.

Long hair breaks into locks the same way, by its silhouette rather than ridges: a fall is several `hair` clumps side by side on sockets round the head, each its own mesh ending in the point a tuft at the end of its drawn outline gives it, overlapping its neighbours so their overlap creases ink the gaps between them, with strand marks inked down each clump (see [parts.md](parts.md), Mane, long hair).

- **Which tufts stand.** A part's fur rule holds spacing and length. Neither the camera nor zoom changes which tufts a part has or their size: only the rule does. Each root has a room on the body, worked out once with the camera aside: the first root in an order seeded from its id has all of it, and each next is the one furthest from those taken, by that distance. A tuft stands when its room reaches the spacing, starting small at 0.7 of the spacing and reaching full size at the spacing, its ink with it, so tufts lie the spacing apart on the body. The part's surface hides all of a tuft but what clears the rim, so it rises over the edge as the body turns and sets behind it.
- **Its length.** Length and spacing are set in pixels as a view of 280 pixels to a body unit shows them, and hold on the body, so a tuft keeps its size against the part at every zoom and in every pane. Each tuft's length varies a little by its id, so a row does not read as a saw.
- **Its shape.** A tuft is a fixed shape on the body: a blade from a base sunk into the surface to its tip, along the grain and rising from the surface, bowed slightly away from it. Its tip shows where that fixed point shows from every camera, so an orbit shows it turn with the body and never grow or swim. Only the way its width faces reads the camera: its width turns to the viewer, narrowing to a point as the tuft runs straight at them. Its fill takes the tones of the slot under its root, read from the part's own brush strokes, regions, filled areas and base in that order, with the normal at its root, so a two-tone shadow carries onto it. Every pane fills it from the palette at that pane's shading, as the part under it. A part's ink is one ribbon holding every edge, drawn before its fills, twice the width of the figure's contour with the fills covering the inner half. So strokes that overlap read as one jagged shape with one outline, and that outline stands in for the contour near the silhouette. Parts draw their fur furthest first.
- **Its depth.** A wedge sits back along its line of sight, just before the rim that makes the part's outline in the way it runs: the part is taken as balls along its length, and of those the wedge's root could cross, the one the line outward from the root leaves last, the rim of its own ring, or a ball that covers its tip, whichever lies furthest back. So that part's surface covers all of it but what clears the part's outline, from any angle, whichever ring it roots on, and no fur shows on the part's face. A wedge covers both weights of the contour where it crosses, so its ink takes over from the line with no line across its root, and it still shows in front of whatever lies behind the part.

Surface strokes are drawn the way WYSIWYG NPR draws its decals: control points on the surface, a curve through them, a screen-space width and tapered ends. Each stroke has its own color from the palette, the ink's until it is given one, and a pen's pressure sets its width along it. The rest of the paper's stroke, its alpha and its halo, Squash does not have.

A floating stroke goes where that paper does not, for what stands off the body such as whiskers: Lines' Stroke draws one with Floating picked in its options. Its root is an anchor, and each later place is held from the root in the directions of the surface there: along it as u grows, across it as v grows, and out of it. So it follows a refit and comes out reflected on a mirrored surface. It is drawn on the plane through its root facing the viewer, so one stroke does not bend in depth. With no surface under them its places carry no normal and it keeps the interior weight from every side.

Marks on a pair are shared by both sides. A mark on a part in the middle of the body has a mirror flag: it shows once as stored and once across the middle, with v turned to 1 - v and what it floats through turned over, and erasing either side takes the mark. The tools set the flag from Mirror.

Face marks are painted on the head's surface, with versions drawn for a few head angles that switch, not blend, as the head turns. Each expression has its own set of angle versions. Each mark's 3D position comes from the body it sits on, never estimated from the drawings. Marks anchor to template coordinates (see [data-model.md](data-model.md), Surface anchor), so they survive refits and oversketches. A face is drawn flat, as seen from straight ahead, and placed by the head's face map (`FaceMap`): a place x to the character's left and y up, in the head's drawn units, lands where a ray from in front, along the way the head faces, first meets the body patch as drawn, and the anchor there keeps its u and v when the head is drawn again. Props on the face, a pair of shades on the nose's bridge, take their sockets from the same map.

## Eyes and gaze

Eyes carry the acting, so they are not loose marks. An eye is painted on the head's surface from parameters, and its gaze moves the iris, pupil and highlight within the opening.

**The painted eye.** `PaintedEye` (in `Character.paintedEyes`) holds an anchor on the head, an `EyeLook` and a mirrored flag; [surface.md](surface.md), Eyes, says how it is painted. The look's lengths are shares of the head's own u and v at the eye's middle, so an eye keeps its look on a larger or smaller head. Its roles, in draw order, are the white, the iris, the pupil and the highlight, each filling a palette slot, then the lid line, the lashes and the brow as ink lines. The lashes are a count, 0 to 3 (`EyeLook.lashes`): the first flicks from the opening's back corner, each further one rises further forward along the upper lid and a little shorter, all following the opening's tilt; a saved flag reads as one flick or none (`LashCountSerializer`). The brow is `browWidth` times the line weight, full in its middle and thinning toward its ends when thicker than a line. Across lengths (width, iris size and offset) are shares of the head's coordinate that runs toward the face and down lengths (height, iris rise, brow raise) of the one that runs down it: on an upright head v and u, on a head resting forward u and v, so a unit across on a `head-round` spans about 2.2 times a unit down, and the reference prints both for every head template. The opening is the head's own colour outside it, so the lid is the head. The eyes' fills take no shadow tone. The look's detail, each part off by default, draws the eye as a toon sheet does rather than as one even ring: the opening's top lid can run flatter (`topFlatness`) over a sharper back corner (`outerCorner`); a lid mass (`lidMass`) replaces the lid line with a heavy upper band, even over the top, tapering a little toward the back and to a point at the front corner, and thinning away wherever its open lid runs steep, so on an opening taller than wide its weight ends at the shoulder where the lid turns down, a short flick running out from there past the back corner, and a thin line carries on round the back corner to meet the lower band, so the opening's outline is never bare; and a lighter lower band over part of the lower lid, growing to its weight just in from where that lid stops running steep, so it never trails down the side; lashes (`lashClumps`) grow out of the mass's outer part as strokes tapering to fine points, leaving the lid toward the back corner and curving up, rounding into it where they meet, a few thin ones spread wide reading as separate lashes, and take the lash flicks' place; the iris takes cel tones (`irisShade`), a darker top melting into the pupil, a lighter crescent along its bottom, thickest at the bottom and thinning to nothing below the iris's middle, and a darker rim, all hard-edged but the darker top where its `soft` asks, the darker top reaching down to the crescent where both are on so the iris shows two tones; and the upper lid casts a narrow shadow band on the white, hugging the lid and thinning toward the corners (`lidShadow`). A tone with no slot of its own is a fill's colour taken part way toward another: the iris's toward the pupil's or the white's, the white's toward a cool grey lilac, the mass the ink. The mass measures its thickness with the opening's own width for its height as it lies on the head, so it follows a slanted or tall opening's own top edge. Every length of the detail is a share of the opening or the iris, so it keeps its look as the eye is sized, and the kit's `animeEye` turns it all on at once. A mirrored eye is painted on both sides of the head's plane from the one look, so one set of controls poses both.

**Front look.** An optional second look takes over as a view turns to face the head (`fromFront` of the way, `shownFront`), so an eye can read rounder from the front than in three-quarter, as a drawn eye does. The facing's `lookOut` turns the eye outward on the head's surface, which is how a bird's eye sits on the side of its head and a fennec's three-quarter eye reads.

**Gaze.** `gazeForward` and `gazeUp` are shares of the opening's half width and half height, so an iris never leaves the opening; the opening clips it. A pupil is round or a slit, with its size a share of the iris.

**The pupil system.** A `Gaze` on each character in a shot, keyed over time like a pose, is planned:

- **Target.** A point in the scene, the camera, an anchor on another character (its eye landmark, so two characters look at each other), or free, where each eye is placed by hand.
- **Solve.** The direction from the eye's middle to the target, in the eye's plane, becomes the gaze shares, clamped to the opening. Near targets converge both eyes up to a convergence limit. A camera bias pulls both pupils a fraction toward the camera so they stay visible and reading at three-quarter views.
- **In motion.** Eyes lead the head by a few frames when the target moves, and an optional blink fires on a large gaze change.

**Angle versions.** A mark group, the mouth's among them, holds a version of its marks for each of three head angles: side up to 35 degrees of head angle, three-quarter up to 70, front past that, each holding 6 degrees past its edge in a view that can turn. Head angle is 0 side on and 90 facing the viewer. A mark holds a shape for each version it was drawn in and its own shape in the others.

**Lids.** Each eye has an upper lid, its opening's top curve, and a lower lid, its bottom, and stands them on its own (`PaintedEye.upperClose` and `lowerRise`, 0 open and 1 shut), so eyes in a list of any length blink or droop one by one, and a mirrored pair, one eye painted twice, stands both sides alike. Closing, a lid's edge moves from its open curve toward the look's closed line (`EyeLook.lids`: `closedAt` where it crosses the middle, `closedBend` its middle arched up as a happy closed eye's or sagging, `closedTilt`), which is drawn in toward the corners where the opening narrows. The white, iris, pupil and highlight are cut off past either edge, never squashed, and the lid line, the lash flicks, the lid mass with its flick and lashes, and the lid shadow ride the moving edges, so a half-closed eye reads drowsy and a shut one is a lid line along the closed line with every lid ink gathered onto it; lashes keep rising off the lid until it is nearly shut and then lower to hang short, down and out from the closed line; the mass thins a little as the lid shuts and, shut, ends at its shoulder with its flick running out and a little down and no line round the corner, so the closed line is clean. What a lid covers as it closes takes the colour of the head's paint the eye sits in, the colour showing on most of the opening and a margin round it, so a face painted in two halves counts as one and paint hidden under part of the open eye or ringing it never shows through a shut lid (`paintEyeSitsIn`); with none, the skin's. The lids can name a `slot` of their own: then that colour shows where the lid covers the opening and, with `above`, over the skin above the open lid, thinning to the corners, and `fade` fades it out going up from the lash line and toward the corners into `fadeTo` or the skin. Its `marks`, a liner wing or a painted pattern, are strokes rooted on the lid by a place along it and a height over the lash line, so they move with it, and never cover the open eye. All of it is look data in plain fields an expression can set. Standing the lids is a repaint of the eye alone: the paint node keeps each eye's layers as laid with its lids open (`EyeMemo`) and stands the lids in their shapes, which the renderer reads at each fragment, so a moving lid lays nothing again and rebuilds no geometry. A blink shown in the app (`SessionSurface.lidPreview`) stands every eye's lids without an edit, so it never reaches undo; recipe renders take `lids=` for the same view of `close=eyes`.

**UI.** The Face stage's Eyes section holds Roundness, the pupil and the colours; then Lids, folded away: Upper close and Lower rise, a Blink button that shows every eye shut while it is on, the closed line's place, bend and tilt, the lid's colour and fade-into colour (Skin clears either), Fade, Above and a liner Wing; then the rest under More, the detail's sliders among them. Each drag is one step of undo and a repaint of the eye alone; the panes show the eye's box, whose corners resize it, whose round handle turns it and whose inside moves it. A gaze gizmo in the Shot viewport is due with a Shot board for eyes.

## View keys

A toon sheet cheats the head in 2D: a muzzle pushed toward the viewer in three-quarter, ears turned to show their shape, a face that stays turned to the viewer while the head turns. No one 3D head matches every view, so a character's head carries keys against the view angle (`ViewKeys` in `document`, `character/viewkeys`), after view-dependent geometry (Rademacher, "View-dependent geometry", 1999) and blend shapes (Lewis, Anjyo, Rhee, Zhang, Pighin, Deng, "Practice and theory of blendshape facial models", 2014).

- **Key views.** Front, three-quarter, side, three-quarter back and back, by where the camera sits round the head's own facing, on either side: eight directions. Keys are written as seen with the camera on the head's left; the right takes them mirrored (slide, yaw, roll and twist turned the other way, a paired part's move given to its twin) unless a key is written for that side. The front and back have no side.
- **What a key moves.** A feature by name on each head (its muzzle run or the part on its muzzle socket, its ears, its nose piece, the head itself) or a part, one of its runs or pieces, or one of its template's areas: slid in the head's directions as shares of its length, turned (yaw, pitch, roll about the head's axes, twist about its own length) and scaled, whole or along the head's width, height and depth, about its root (a head's middle, another part's socket point, a run's first ring); and swollen along its normals, all of it or the half on the camera's side. A move on a part carries everything on it, composed up through the runs and parts it sits on.
- **Defaults.** With `auto`, every head's muzzle leans toward the camera (more from three-quarter behind, so its tip shows past the cheek) and its ears turn their broad side toward the camera; its back is drawn a little wider and lower; in the turning face style its near cheek fills out and its chin slides toward the camera. A key written for a view replaces the default for the same thing there and leaves the rest.
- **Face styles.** The face's features (eyes, brows, nose, mouth, muzzle, cheek marks) slide round the head's up toward the camera by `faceFollow` of the view's angle up to the side, and from three-quarter behind round to the back slide back onto the head and hide behind it. How much of the turn a place takes is a mask by how far it lies toward the facing from the head's middle, falling off over the crown: whole over the front half when the face keeps its size and slides as one piece, easing up across the whole front otherwise. Ears, horns and hair take half the turn wherever they sit, staying clear of the turned face. A turning face (the head template's default) follows part of the way and the head's outline changes by its shape keys; a fixed face follows most of the way and keeps its features' size, the head keeping one outline from the front round to the side. Each head template names its default style and follow; a character overrides both.
- **Built once, blended per view.** `ViewKeyShapes.of` reads the assembly and the one surface at rest once and builds, for every direction, each moved vertex's change: a vertex of the one surface takes the share-weighted sum of its members' maps (so a join blends parent into child and nothing tears at the neck), its area and swelling moves, then its face turn; a separate part takes its run's or piece's map whole. A view weighs the two key directions either side of the camera's way round the head's facing, eased between them, faded out as the camera rises over or sinks under the head, and adds the weighted changes turned by the head's own turn in the pose (`weightsFor`, `keyed`). The one surface keeps its vertices, quads and coordinates, so its paint is laid again on it, and its ink marks and eyes are carried with the vertex nearest each, as on a pose; a separate part keyed is shown moved and its lines found again.
- **Not yet keyed.** Garments over a head and fur keep their rest places; outlines per view are not keyed.

## Mouth

The mouth is a mark group like the eye, with roles: lip line, inner, teeth, tongue, corners. It rests closed. Its seven controls are open, which parts the lips on the head's surface as it stands, or, for a mouth on a beak's seam, drives the jaw joint that drops the lower beak; wide, which draws the corners out or in; round, which puckers it to a narrow round opening; smile, which lifts or drops the corners; upper lip, which raises the upper lip off the upper teeth; lower lip, which tucks the lower lip under the upper teeth at 1 and presses the lips together at -1; and tongue, which lifts the tongue to the upper teeth by 0.5 and out past the lower lip by 1, a blep or a Th.

**Spoken shapes.** Nine shapes are presets of those controls: rest, M B P, A I, E, O, U, F V, L and the rest (etc). Smile is a mood the character holds: a shape's own smile adds to it. Each head template names a mouth style (`templates/mouths/<id>.mouth.json`: lips, short muzzle, long muzzle, beak, wide jaw, minimal), which says where its line runs (front, muzzle or seam), how deep it opens, how much of the line parts (`span`, the rest of a muzzle's line staying shut back along its sides), how much each control does on it, how thick its lips' bands are, whether the shapes move it (`speaks`), and the nine shapes as data. Lips, minimal, short muzzle and beak speak; long muzzle and wide jaw hold their shapes as data only, a picked shape leaving their mouths as their own controls draw them and the Face stage showing them no Shapes strip. A group may name another style and override any shape (`MarkGroup.style`, `MarkGroup.shapes`, the command `SetMouthShape`). Picking a shape in the Face stage's Shapes strip, or `set=head:mouth.shape:<name>` in a build, shows every mouth saying it without an edit (`CharacterSession.mouthPreview`); its seven sliders then edit the group's override of that shape, each drag one undo step, and Reset takes the style's back.

**One shaper.** `MouthShaper` (in `character`) shapes a mouth for both the built surface (`openMouths`) and the Face stage's overlay (`mouthDraws`), so they match. It works in a flat frame laid along the lip line (`MouthFrame`): x is the distance along the line on the head's surface from its middle, the line's marks joined end to end, and y the distance down the face from the line along the surface, each line of constant x walked straight across the head from the line, starting mostly toward the body's down. A muzzle's line, running back along both sides of the snout from the front like the seam where the lower jaw meets the upper muzzle, lies flat in it, and nothing folds round the muzzle's tip: the frame reads the patch about its nearer end (`PoleChart`), so a line passing close by the tip runs smoothly through it. The lip line is a smooth curve through its marks' places. The upper lip lifts by upper lip and smile and arches by round; the lower lip drops below it by the open, deepest at the middle, its profile rounder as round rises; or, where the inner role names a mark, that closed shape is the fully open mouth. The opening fills in the inner role's slot over the head's regions and strokes. Where the lip line's role has a fill, the regions the head paints in that slot are the lips as drawn (`lipRegionsOf`): the upper lip carries those lying above the line and the lower lip those below, each place kept at its height off the line as the lip's band thins by press and tuck and fills by round, and drawn in or out with the corners, so the lips' color stretches, rounds, presses and parts with each shape, and a mouth at rest paints the regions as they are. With no such region, both lips show as bands in the fill that thin as they press and tuck and thicken as they round. Both lips are inked as the lip line is, and press shows a short crease between them. Teeth whose tops lie at the lip line ride the upper lip, the rest ride the jaw; tucked upper teeth rest over the lower lip. The tongue rides the lower lip, lifts behind the upper teeth's edge by 0.5 and past the lower lip by 1; with no tongue mark and a filled tongue role a plain tongue shows. Teeth and tongue are clipped to the opening and filled in their role's slot, a tooth sharp at every place, unless a mark is flagged over (`GroupRole.over`): an over mark draws over the lips, open or shut, and the lip ink breaks under it, so fangs show over a shut mouth. A beak's style has its line on the seam: nothing parts on the surface, the lower beak drops on its jaw by the open and shows the inside and the tongue on its top (`JawMouth`), the tongue reaching further as tongue rises. A mouth at open 0 with no lifted tongue and no over mark draws as drawn. An expression may open a group by id (`ExpressionSet.open`), in place of the group's own open, while it shows (`shownOpen`). The lip line is drawn on the muzzle's surface and wraps around it, so it reads from the side as well as the front. Smiles and puffed cheeks move the muzzle, not the mark: they are pose-driven shape fixes on the muzzle keyed to the open and expression controls.

**Whole first.** A spoken shape never breaks the mouth: from every view the head's outline stays unbroken, the lip ink a clean line broken only where an over tooth crosses it, and the opening clean edged, even where that means a shape gives up its sound. Each style holds limits on how far each control may go after its scale (`MouthStyle.limits`: open, corners out and in, round, smile, upper lip, lower lip, tongue), so where a sound cannot be made cleanly on its head its shape opens less or stays nearer rest; a muzzle's corners are never drawn out past its drawn line. The opening fills only where the lips part, so no sliver runs on along a line held shut. A pucker gathers drawn lips round its opening, thinning them as it draws them in. The shipped styles paint their openings (`carve` 0), as a carved recess notches the head's outline and leaves the lip ink standing off it. `carved` is lips with a carve, for a character that wants one.

**Carved.** Where a style carves, the mouth is cut into the head as a shallow recess where the lips part, so a side view shows a notch in the outline and a three-quarter view shows depth (`MouthRecess`, surface.md, Mouth recesses). The recess follows the shaper's opening, whatever line the style describes, the front's lips or round a muzzle, at most the style's `carve` of the lip line's width deep and never deeper than 2.5 times how far the lips part at a place, so a narrow opening stays shallow. Its walls take the inner role's fill, and its back, deeper than half way, the same fill taken a third of the way toward the ink, so it reads darker toward the back. The lip lines across the opening stay on the lips' surface. A style with `carve` 0, `painted` among the presets, opens on the surface only, as paint. A mouth that rests as drawn cuts nothing, so a closed mouth builds the head it always had. The recess is the wall teeth stand on: `MouthRecess.wallAt` gives the point and its normal at a place along the mouth line, 0 at one corner and 1 at the other, a share of the way from the lips to the back, on the upper or the lower jaw's wall.
**Teeth.** A mouth's teeth are a dentition: two jaws, each with a level and rows of slots from the middle outward, mirrored unless a slot names a side, each slot holding a tooth type (width, length, a flat, round, pointed or chisel tip, and a curve that hooks the tip) and any of its settings changed for that slot, with its gap from the slot before it or its own place along the row, and an over flag. Presets are data (`templates/teeth/<id>.teeth.json`: toon simple, human, rodent, cat, dog or fox, crocodile, dragon, shark, viper, horse, cow or deer, boar, bird, frog or fish); a mouth style names the one its mouths start from, and a group may name another. A group keeps only what it changes (`MouthTeeth`: a jaw's level, slot changes by row and index, slots and rows added, slots removed), so a later change to a preset still reaches every character on it. Levels: none; bar, each row one band from its first slot to its last as long at each slot as its tooth; blocks, one tooth per slot. Shaped teeth, each slot's type built as a part, sit beside them later and read the same slots. `layTeeth` lays the teeth on a `TeethSeat`, the one place placement reads: the upper jaw hangs from the lip line as drawn and stays as the mouth opens, the lower stands on the jaw's drop and goes with it, and each tooth reaches a little past its line, so a lip lifting off it uncovers tooth. The shaper's seat is the inner mouth's lip lines in the frame laid along the lip line, on carved and painted mouths alike, so a muzzle's teeth stand along its sides, at their own height on the head. A tooth flagged over shows over the lips, open or shut, cut to below the upper lip or above the lower so its root stays hidden, and breaks the lip's ink; while nothing has moved the upper lip, it is broken along its own drawn marks. Other teeth show only inside the opening, or rest over a tucked lower lip. Teeth fill in the teeth role's slot; with none they do not show. A person's lips start from toon simple, a bar on the upper jaw and nothing on the lower.

**Tongue.** A group's tongue level is none or flat: flat draws its tongue marks, or a plain tongue where its role is filled and has none, a beak's included; none draws no tongue. A shaped tongue part comes later.

## Surface work rules

- **Expressions carry poses.** An expression set holds marks, how far it opens each mouth group, and a small face pose: ear joints, jaw, and brows where they are boned. Ears back is an expression, not a hand pose. Sets blend where control counts match and switch on the character's timing where they do not. A character shows one expression, or its own marks with none, and that is saved with it. A joint turns its part's surface in the graph, so everything on that surface follows it: a hinge x runs across the part, so a jaw or an ear pitches, y about its up and z about its length. What lies past the joint turns, over a short stretch of u, and a joint with a v turns only the side of the part around that v. Outlines stay as drawn, so they lie off a part an expression has turned.
- **Fur has a grain.** Each part has a grain field. Its template gives the default: along the axis toward the end it names, rising off the surface by its lift, and further on a ruff, which rises outward over a stretch of u and v and fades past its edges. A comb tool overrides it, stored as anchor-space vectors on the part, each holding at its place and fading to nothing at its reach. Fur strokes and later hair follow the grain as it stands, so combing moves the strokes already there.
- **Paint and regions cross parts.** A brush stroke or a region border that crosses a socket continues onto the parent patch; a region is a set of anchors on any number of patches. A face mask runs from muzzle to skull and a sock from paw to leg.
- **Paired parts mirror surface work.** Paint, regions and marks on a paired part mirror to its twin by default, with per-side overrides on top, the same rule as outlines.
- **Strokes can leave the surface.** A mark whose anchors carry an offset renders as a 3D line off the surface and moves with its part: whiskers, lashes, drips.
- **Any mark can be camera-locked.** The eye highlight rule applies to a wet nose, a tooth glint and a claw tip.
- **Effect marks belong to the shot.** Sweat drops, shock lines, blush, speed lines and tears are keyed per shot and pinned to a landmark or drawn in camera space. They are not part of the character and do not travel with it to the library.

## World

Both a painted 2D backdrop for the distance and 3D sets and props for what characters touch or walk around. Shadows per scene: none, a flat blob under each character, or flat cast shadows from the scene's light. Only characters are outlined; whether props are inked is open.

## Export note

Outlines and 2D marks are drawn by this tool. A glTF export would carry the body meshes, flat colors and rig, with outlines as an optional inverted-hull mesh. Faces and fur marks need an export plan later. Not needed for the first version.
