# Parts

Every body part a cartoon animal or person might have, and the mechanism that makes it. Four mechanisms cover nearly everything: a **template** (an axis spline with section splines and blocks, see [data-model.md](data-model.md)), a **socket** in the body plan or on a parent part, a **slot** that switches a part on or off, and a **mark** (strokes on the surface with angle versions, see [look.md](look.md)). What is not a shape is a mark; what is a shape is a template on a socket.

When: starter is spike 1, first is the first version, later is after it.

| Paws | `paw` template: tube, a toe row of none or three to five starting where the tube ends, each toe flared at its root on an egg section, tapering, with two joints along it so the toes curl under, webs between the toes up to their pads, pads underneath, cap, and a tip socket at every toe's end for a claw. Version 5 builds the paw as a `field`, a round heel (`heel`) and a pad ellipsoid (`width`, `height`) cut flat at the sole, with its toes as a `digits` row joined to it, three, four or five, each count its own spread (`toes`; 0 none), two joints a toe, `toeLength` and `toeThickness` scaling them: `merge` runs from toes that stand clear (0.15 to 0.3, a fox's or a dragon's) to a mitten of shallow lobes (1 to 2, a house cat's), `web` how far the union fills between their roots; claws past every tip in the slot `claw` (`claw`, its length in toe lengths, `clawCurl`, `claws` 0 to retract them); a main pad and a bean under every toe in the slot `pad` (`pads`), the beans raised by `beans`. It has no tip sockets, since its claws are its own. Version 6 is version 5 with its field fitted to the drawing (`fit`): a drawn Side and Top set the paw's length, height and width along it, toes and all, and its parameters shape what lies within. Version 7 is version 6 with `spread` (0.3 to 1.8, 1 as before), which multiplies the toes' fan angle and the gap between their roots, so below 1 they sit closer together. Version 8 is version 7 with one to five toes (`toes` 0 to 5), each count laid out across the paw of its own, the toes thinner than the pad's height and fanned so they read apart from the front at every count. Version 9 is version 8 with a short wrist (`ankle`, 0.09 of its length) rising from the heel's top into the leg end and a template `join` blending it into the leg over a quarter of its length, so the leg flows into the paw with no step. Version 11 is version 10 with its `sock` area the whole paw, toes too, under its pads, beans and claws. Version 12 is version 11 drawn from the sheet, its heel narrower and its pad lower, ending in a lower fore pad so the top falls from the wrist to the toes: its field is `drawn` (see the Feet row), its wrist a capsule as thick as the leg's end (`limb`), and a drawn Front sets its whole section, a sole's flat-bottomed one where Front is not drawn | Starter; version 12 newest |
| Hands, fingers, palms, thumbs | `hand` template, role hand: the paw's blocks hanging down, a palm tube flatter than it is wide and a pad that is off by default, and a toe row of five long fingers flared where they leave the palm, on an egg section a little wider than thick, tapering toward the tip and swelling at each joint, whose first digit's override starts it lower on the palm, outside the row and turned out, a thumb, and a web between each finger and the next reaching a third of the way up, the thumb's half way. Each finger has three joints, the knuckle where it leaves the palm and two along it, and the thumb two, so the hand curls. It has no tip sockets: a nail lies on the back of the fingertip, so it wants a socket on the digit's top or a painted region. Version 4 builds its palm as a `field`, a smooth union of a palm ellipsoid narrowing into the wrist, a thumb pad and a wrist capsule, sized by `palmLength`, `palmWidth`, `palmThickness` and `wrist`, and its fingers and thumb as `digits` placed one by one from their own roots, apart at the tips, with three joints each (`fingers:i.1` to `.3`, index to pinky then the thumb) that curl the fingers about the hand's up and the thumb about its side, `fingerLength` and `fingerThickness` scaling them; it hangs with its palm toward the body's middle and its fingers spread forward. Version 6 is version 5 with its field fitted to the drawing, as `paw` 6's is. Version 7 is version 6 with a digit count: `digits` 2 to 5, the thumb among them while `thumb` is on (the row's `countsNamed`), so 4 is a cartoon's three fingers and a thumb and 3 two and a thumb; with `thumb` off every digit is a finger. Each finger count has a layout of its own across the palm: fewer fingers are each thicker and fanned wider, spanning the same palm, so three fingers read as drawn for three. The thumb is the named digit `thumb` (`fingers:thumb`, its joints `fingers:thumb.1` to `.3`), the fingers `fingers:0` on from the thumb's side. Its fingers are thinner than version 6's and taper further, apart at the tips at the defaults, its palm narrower than long, `merge` (0.1) sets how far neighbouring fingers fuse and `spread` fans them across the palm, 0 folding them together and 2 or more splaying them. At rest the palm faces the body's middle, `-side` in the hand's axes; a negative `twist` on the elbow turns the palm forward on either side of a pair, a positive one back; an elbow's one-way limits hold its twist at 0 unless the pose bone is `free` (`frames.md`). Versions 1 to 3 still build every character that pins them. Version 9 is version 8 drawn from the sheet, its thumb on a smaller web and a smaller thumb pad so it stands apart from the palm from every side and its fingers tapering further: its field is `drawn` (see the Feet row), its wrist a capsule as thick as the arm's end (`limb`), a drawn Front sets its whole section, and each digit takes its own length, thickness and spread from parameters named for its patch (`fingers.2.length`, `fingers.0.width`, `fingers.thumb.spread`), read after the drawing fits the solid | First; ships, version 9 newest |
| Feet, toes | `foot` template, role foot: the paw's blocks as a plantigrade foot resting forward, a heel run under the ankle, a longer sole and a row of five short toes flat underneath, flattened and tapering with webs between them, which a shoe sets to none. It ends a leg that bears weight, so the floor rule stands it on the ground. Version 3 builds the foot as a `field`, a smooth union of heel, arch and ball ellipsoids and an ankle capsule cut flat at the sole (`heel`, `ballWidth`, `ankle`), with five toes as `digits` on a joint each, which `toes` 0 leaves out. A shoe's Redraw sets `toeBox` on, a capsule from the ball to `toeTip` rounding to `toeRound`, and may pitch the foot down with `move.lean` and lay its forefoot back flat with `pitch`; its socket sits under the ankle, so a leg ending at its pole stands the ankle where a person's is. Version 4 is version 3 with its toes joined to the field as `hand` 5's fingers are, on one surface with no line at their roots, two joints a toe and a toenail on each in the slot `nails`; `merge` (0.35) sets how far each blends into the next and `web` (0.04) how far the union fills between their roots. A shoe's Redraw is the same, `toes` 0 leaving the field to close over the toe box. Version 5 is version 4 with its field fitted to the drawing, as `paw` 6's is. Version 6 is version 5 with `spread`, as `paw` 7's is. Version 7 is version 6 with one to five toes (`toes` 0 to 5), each count its own layout across the ball, five as version 6 lays them, fewer symmetric about the foot's middle and wider; `toeLength` and `toeThickness` make them short and wide, the toes joined to the field as `paw`'s are so they part from the front; `claw` stands each nail out past its tip as a claw, curling by `clawCurl`, and `pads` with `beans` a pad under each toe in the slot `pad`. Version 8 is version 7 with an ankle: the heel sits back from the ankle by half its radius (`$ankle*-0.5`), so a foot under a thick leg still shows its heel behind it, ankle bones swell a fifth wider than the ankle across it, the template's `join` blends the foot into the leg over a quarter of its length, and its `sole` area starts at u 0.14, so it stays underneath and does not wrap up the heel's back. Version 10 is version 9 drawn from the sheet, its heel narrower than the ankle and low, its arch low and narrowing toward the heel so the instep falls from the ankle to the toes, an instep solid on the inner side so the top stands higher over the big toe than the little toe, its ball low so the top runs nearly flat over the forefoot into the toes, and its five toes stepping down and smaller from the big toe out, tapering, apart to their roots (`merge` 0.12, `web` 0.02) with small nails, so a bare foot reads toe by toe. Its field is `drawn`: once Side or Top is drawn, the drawing is the solid itself (`geom/field/DrawnSolid`), Side and Top its silhouettes exactly and Front its whole section, the template's `sole` section where Front is not drawn, so the toe end is one smooth cap with `toes` 0, as a sock or a shoe-like toon foot, and the foot is as wide from the front as Top or Front draws it. The template's own solids only place the toes, joints, areas and sockets on it (`FieldFit`, as version 5's fit, gives each ring's u and carries each toe to the drawing); with toes the drawing is cut back across the axis half a toe's radius past each toe's root, the cut following the row of roots, rounded over that radius, so they stand from it apart. Its ankle is a capsule kept beside the drawing (`kept`), as thick as the leg's end: `limb`, a share of the foot's length, is read from the leg it sits on whenever the part leaves it unset (`LimbEnds`: half the leg's mean thickness one radius back from where its outline closes), so the leg runs straight on into the foot with no waist or collar, and the drawing is cut flat at Side's lowest point so the ankle never reaches below the sole. Undrawn, the heel sits back from the ankle by half that radius and the ankle bones are gone; its `join` blend is 0.06 of its length. The parameters stay as defaults and quick changes: with nothing drawn they shape the foot as version 8's do | First; ships, version 10 newest |

| Holding things | A hold socket on the palm of the hand and paw templates, with a grip pose library per template; a held prop attaches to the socket and rides with the hand | First |

## Hair

Hair is one mass: a volume over the skull, clumps blended into it and, on fur, tufts, all runs of the one surface, so the mass shades and inks as one and only its ends part into locks. Each template offers named presets (`presets`, a set of its parameter values a part takes in one step: a recipe's `preset`, the Shape inspector's Style chips).

- **The volume** (`hair-volume`, the `volume` block) is one closed run in the field from the back of the head (u 0) to the forehead (u 1), on a hand-made socket at the back of the skull standing off it by the hair's thickness. Undrawn, its rings are `VolumeShape`'s: each ring's top `crown` above the socket's height and its half width `width`, rounding off toward both ends by `fullness`; under it the sides hang `back` at the back and `sides` from the middle forward, and the middle hangs as the sides do at the back and rises to `fringe` at the front, so the front of the volume leaves the face clear between two curtains and stops above the brow; `sweep` lengthens the fringe toward one side and shortens it toward the other. The axis rises `rise` toward the front so the volume closes on the forehead. `flip` flares the hem out (a bob), `parting` and `partDepth` dent the top along a parting. Heights and widths are fractions of its length, and its length is its `size` times 0.3: a head's own `size` does not scale it, so a volume is sized to the head's depth (the kit's `hairVolume` takes the depth). Drawn, its Side outline shapes it as a skull's does. Its join blends into the skull over 0.1 of its length with no ink, so the hairline is soft, and its colour edge is the part's section, the hairline. Presets: `short`, `bob`, `long`, `ponytail`, the first three with layers of locks and lock lines over the hem; sockets `crown`, `fringe` and `tail` (a ponytail's root).
- **Its hem parts into locks.** Seen from above, the hem is split into `locks` locks round the head: under the back and the front's middle by how far across a ring lies, so the nape and the fringe part side by side, and along the curtains by how far forward, so they part one behind another. Between two points the hang is cut up `lockDepth` of the length, the cut fading in down each ring's lower half so the mass above stays whole; `lockJag` shortens some locks (picked by `lockSeed`) for a ragged, layered hem, `lockSkew` leans the points toward one neighbour, `lockRound` rounds them and `lockFlare` splays them out sideways.
- **How hair flows** over a volume (`HairFlow`): each place has a way round the head, the way its locks are laid out (`VolumeShape.azimuthAt`, the same for a place on the top and the place under it), and a height; hair falls down each way to the hem, the lowest place that way, which `HairFlow` keeps for every way round, cut into the locks' points and notches, and evened out over a lock. A place with a given way and height is found by Newton steps from the nearest of a grid of samples, on the outside of the volume only: a place facing in toward the head, or facing down under it, is never inked.
- **The volume's areas** follow its own shape: `hem`, the band within `hemShade` of the length above the hem each way round (`HemLevel`, a level read at each place of the one surface, `AreaSpec.level`), so it runs evenly along every lock's end, at its point and at its notches alike; `parting`, a narrow band along the parting over the front; `shine`, a band round the crown (Hair shine, below), a loop of places on the volume's (u, v) (`AreaSpec.loops`). An unfilled hem shows the hair's own colour to the tips.
- **A clump** (`hair-clump`, a `tube` with a `clump`, `TubeClump` and `ClumpShape`) is a run in the field, blended into what it sits on over 0.3 of its length with no ink: a flat lock of rounded section `width` across and `thickness` through at its root, swelling by `swell` toward its first third and tapering to `taper`, closing over its last `tipReach` to a tip pointed at `tipRound` 0 and round at 1. It turns `twist` degrees about itself, waves `wave` of its width `waves` times, curls its sides by `cup` so it curves round what it lies on, and its root flares `flare` wider. It sweeps along its axis, so a `curve` or `spiral` gives its path. Rooted on the volume and hanging past its hem, a clump is the volume where they meet and a lock of its own toward its tip. Its `tip` area covers its last `tipShade`. A clump shown as copies is its own mesh (copies stay out of the field): there `points` above 1 end it in that many lobes cut back `notch` between them, and its outline fades in over its first `rootSoft` (`rootFade`). A drawn Side replaces its undrawn heights as on any tube. Presets: `lock`, `spike`, `soft`, `fluff`, `wavy`, `strand`, and `fine`, a narrow lock tapering to a point with a wide flared root, which a fringe takes so its locks part at their tips while their roots blend into the volume.
- **Placing clumps.** Clumps sit on hand-made sockets of the volume or the head one by one, or as copies on a spread socket (data-model, Copies of one part), for fur and manes that need not merge. A spread's `droop` tilts each copy from the surface's normal toward the body's down along the surface and turns its up to the normal, so the copies lie down with their broad faces out. A fringe, a side-swept lock, a cowlick and a crown swirl are clumps on sockets over the front and round the crown, curved and turned there.
- **A tuft** (`hair-tuft`) is the clump's shape as a run in the field, curling up by `curlMid` and `curl` (the template's curve), blended into what it grows from over 0.35 of its length with no ink: a cat's crown tuft, cheek fluff, a point at an ear's tip. Presets: `crown`, `cheek`, `point`.
- **Hair shine.** A volume whose `shine` area is filled shows a band of shine on the sphere through its rings (`VolumeShape.sphereOf`, `ShineBand`): the points whose height there lies within `shineWidth` of `shineAt`, its lower edge cut into `shineTeeth` points `shineJag` deep. On the one surface it is a layer of the surface's paint over every vertex a hair part holds, read where the vertex lies (`hairShineLayer`), so one band runs over the volume and every clump and tuft blended into it with no break. On a hair part built as its own mesh it is a layer cut where the band crosses the part as placed (`shinedColorNode`, `hairShines`), each copy of a spread its own; those cuts are kept while the hair and the volume keep their places relative to each other.
- **Ink.** The hair's silhouette is the one surface's contour, and its parting locks are inked where the surface folds between them; no clump has an outline or a crease of its own. A volume inks from its flow (`strandRuns`, `FlowInk`), each line laid on the volume's own surface and standing just off it along the surface's normal: `strands` fine strand lines, each starting below the crown at its own place round it, staggered, falling straight down the surface as seen from above the head's middle, so they spread evenly from the crown, and over the last stretch above the hem gathering into their lock's point, stopping short of the hem by their own amounts, thin at both ends; the edges of `layers` layers of locks, each `layerGap` of the length above the last and cut into locks half a lock round from the layer under it, a little shallower than the hem, shallower still where a lock is narrower than the cut is deep, and as ragged, so each layer's points lie over the notches under it and the mass reads as locks lying over each other, kept to the lower part of the way from the hem to the crown and off the back's pole and the face; and lines `lockLines` of the length up from each notch of the hem between two locks wide enough to show them, heaviest at the notch, where one lock lies over the next. A hair part's placing carries the volume's shape parameters (`ClumpInk.FLOW_PARAMS`) so its lines can read them. A clump inks `strands` lines along its top face, from past its root to near the tip. Strands are part of a part's lines (`PartLines.strands`) and stay when the part is drawn as the one surface.

## Hair locks

Lock hair is built from locks rather than one blended mass: each lock is a separate swept part, locks come in groups, and they lie over a small inner mass that hides the scalp and never sets the outline. The character holds its lock groups (`LockGroup` in `document`, data-model.md, Hair locks); none of them enters the body's field.

- **A group** (`LockGroup`) grows on a head (`on`), its roots on the head or on its inner mass (`mass`, a `hair-volume` part on the head) where that part is there. Its kind says where it roots and, as far as its `flow` is under 1, which way it grows; the scalp flow says the rest (Scalp flow, below): `fringe` across the front of the top, high enough that its locks lie down over the mass's hairline, `left` and `right` along the sides in two staggered rows, `back` over the back in up to three staggered rows, `crown` over a disc round the crown laid out by the golden angle, `strays` round the top, `top` in two runs from behind the hairline back to the crown, one either side of the parting (or 5 degrees either side of the head's middle with none), each in two rows once it holds six locks, the second 11 degrees outside the first. `count` locks spread over `spread` of the kind's reach, turned `around` degrees and raised `height` up the head, each root wandering a little by `seed`; `tie` gathers the roots to one point, a tail's tie, and `band` wraps a band round it, a part of its own in its own slot. A fringe's locks over the eyes are shorter than those in the middle and at its ends, so the fringe parts to show the eyes. A higher `layer` lies over a lower one; within a group a higher row lies over a lower, and every other lock of a row stands a little off its neighbours, so no two locks lie in one surface and nothing flickers between them. A lower row's locks are shorter by the way along the head from the top row, so a group's rows end together.
- **The scalp** (`Scalp`) is the head and its mass read as a reach from the skull's middle (the middle of the head's box raised by 0.15 of its height, above the jaw) in every direction round and up it; a root sits where its direction meets the outermost of the two. Which way a lock leaves its root, how much it lifts and bends over its group's `rise` and `sweep`, and where a group's locks root are behind one interface, `LockGrowth`; the scalp's flow field (`ScalpField`) is the one a build grows by, and each kind's own way (`KindGrowth`, down the head along its meridian, a crown's away from its middle swirling 25 degrees round it) is what a group's `flow` blends it with.
- **Scalp flow** (`ScalpFlow`, one a head, the default where the character gives none; `ScalpField` builds it): which way hair grows over the scalp, after Yu's vector-field hairstyles, a sum of simple primitives on the head weighted by how near each is, projected onto the scalp. The background is the fall down the head along its meridians. The `whorl` (`around` and `up` in degrees, by default 180 and 68, the back of the crown) is a source with a vortex round it, its weight peaking at its middle and none past its `radius`, turning hair round it by `turn` times up to 80 degrees at its middle, easing to none at its edge, and bending each lock rooted within it round it as it grows by up to 1.3 of a full `sweep` times its turn and strength, so the locks there lie in a spiral. The `parting` (`none`, `centre` or `side`, `at` -1 to 1 of 42 degrees off the head's middle toward the character's right) is a line over the top in a plane beside the head's middle, from 40 degrees over the head from straight ahead (the hairline) back 72 degrees times its `length`, wandering up to 3.5 degrees across as its `clean` falls from 1: hair within 16 to 24 degrees of it (less the cleaner) takes most of its way straight away from it, the whorl and the sweep giving way there; no lock within 2.2 of that grows toward it, and the nearer it roots the further it turns away; its pull eases in over 26 degrees from its front, so a fringe under its start falls forward as it parts; past its ends it fades over 10 degrees. No root lies within 3.2 degrees of the line (`PART_GAP`): a root that would is moved straight across to that, so the scalp shows along the line and nowhere else. `sweep` and `sweepBack` comb the top toward the character's right and toward the back, a uniform flow projected onto the scalp, strongest over the top and giving way near a parting. Each of the `cowlicks` (at most 8) reaches `radius` degrees round its place: its `turn` 0 flips the hair there back against the flow, 1 or -1 swirls it round the point, it bends the locks there round it as a whorl does, and it lifts them `lift` degrees over their group's rise, most at its middle. Every primitive but the fall reaches only so far, and a lock past all of a cowlick's reach takes exactly the way it took without it. A lock's own values still win over the flow's.
- **The parting's strip** (`PartingStrip`): where a head with locks has a parting, a thin flat strip lies on the scalp along the line from the hairline to its end, as wide at the hairline as a quarter of the gap no root lies in, rounding up from a point there and thinning to an eighth of that toward its end, a narrow V of scalp; it lies on the highest of the head's and the mass's points within 4 degrees of each point of its line, eased along it, its middle 0.006 of the head's reach above them, so it never stands over the hair round it. It is a part of its own, `locks/<head>~parting`, riding the head's bone whole, coloured the parting's `color` slot, else a scalp tone: the head's base colour carried 0.4 of the way toward its first group's hair colour (`blendedSlot`, a slot name the palette reads as a blend of two slots). Its contour lies 0.03 of the head's reach behind it, so it draws no line over the hair round it: the scalp shows along the parting as a clean thin line between the locks lying away on each side.
- **A lock** is grown along a guide curve: from its root sunk 0.6 of its thickness under the scalp it lies along the head the way it grows, sweeping sideways by `sweep` with the flow's bend added and falling toward the ground by `fall` over its length, held on the head down to just below its widest, where it hangs free; past 0.55 of its length it lifts off by `rise` degrees, so only its end leaves the head. It curls `curl` turns about its side (negative rolls away from the head), starting at half its length and a tenth of it nearer the root for each turn up to one and a half, its turn growing as the 1.7 power of the way into the curl, so it rolls into a spiral, and its loop moving sideways a third of its width a turn; a curl under a quarter turn only turns its end in or out. A flicked tip turns out 50 degrees over its last 0.3. A tied lock leaves its tie at its `rise` instead and hangs free. Every step keeps its clearance off the scalp, rising from its sunk root no steeper than about one in two. Five to eighteen points spaced evenly along that path are its guide, a few-point curve; a lock with a `guide` of its own takes its points from its root instead. Its mesh (`LockBuilder` in `geom`) is the clump's shape (`ClumpShape`: `width` and `thickness` at its root, swelling a little and tapering to `taper`, `twist`, `wave`) carried along the guide, its rings keeping their size through a tight curl and, where it lies on the head, turned to keep facing away from the head, so a wide lock lies flat over it however it bends round it; its section is a lens cupped round the head (`ribbon`) or an egg (`clump`); its tip `pointed`, `round` (a dome whose last rings step evenly round it by angle), `forked` into `tipPoints` points or `flicked`; its root ring closes to a point inside the scalp, so the root is soft and never shows a seam. A lock has 14 rings, 8 more a turn of curl up to 30, by 10 points: a few hundred vertices.
- **Single locks** are pulled out by index (`LockOverride`): any value given replaces the group's for that lock, `guide` gives it a curve of its own, `bones` and `physics` its chain, `hidden` leaves it out.
- **Presets** (`HairPreset`, `presetGroups`) give a whole hairstyle as groups sized to the head's reach: `shortSpiky` (a fringe of spikes and a back group from the top of the head whose tips lift back and up), `bob` (wide ribbon locks with round tips, one length round the jaw, their ends turning in, over the mass's smooth top), `longStraight`, `ponytail` (a tail gathered at a tie with a band, swinging back and down and tapering, over a fringe and the mass), `sideSwept` and `curly` (fewer, bigger round-sectioned locks with round tips each rolling outward into a closed spiral, under a crown tied at the front of the top whose curls rise and roll forward over the brow: alone, as the kit's `curly({ tuft: true })` or a recipe removing the other groups, it is a tuft of curls). `longStraight` and `sideSwept` hold a `top` group too. Each preset has its flow (`presetFlow`): a crown whorl, high on the crown for `shortSpiky`; a centre parting for `longStraight`; a parting on the character's right, the top combed toward the left, for `sideSwept`. A tied group (`ponytail`'s tail, `curly`'s crown) keeps its kind's way (`flow` 0). docs/reference/hair.md lists each preset's groups and values. The inner mass under them is a `hair-volume` with no strands of its own, a close cap whose front ends above the brow under the fringe's roots and whose hem is cut into points; a preset given in the app to a head with no mass fits one over it in the same step (`fittedMass`), as a recipe's `fitMass` does (the kit's `lockMass()`), on a socket of its own, sized by the head's box: its length the head's depth and 0.07 of it each side, its crown from the head's height, so its top follows the skull's dome; parameters given replace the fitted ones.
- **Built** (`LockBuilds`, the session's `lockGroupsNode` after the membranes): each group is one part of its own, `locks/<group>`, of role hair, its mesh every lock's patch in one, so a group draws as one mesh; a lock built as one before is taken as it was and a group whose locks are all as they were is the same part, so editing one lock builds only that lock, editing a group only its locks, editing the flow only the locks whose root, way, lift or bend it changed (a cowlick only those rooted within its radius), and an edit elsewhere builds none; a parting's strip is built again only when the parting or the scalp changes. Its colour is its own slot, else its mass's base slot, else the palette's `hair` slot, else the head's base slot.
- **Ink.** A lock group is a separate part whose join is not inked: its silhouette is its contour. Its contour's interior line lies behind the lock by 0.45 of its locks' thickness (`LockPlacing.tuck`), so where a lock's edge lies over another lock of its group, within that, it draws nothing, and where it lies over the face or a group laid over another it draws at the interior weight, the overlap crease. An untied crown's lies behind by 0.12, short of how far every other lock stands off its neighbours, so each lock's edge over the next draws and the locks out of a whorl read as a spiral.
- **Physics-ready.** Each lock carries a chain of 3 to 6 bones along its guide (3 under half the head's reach, one more for each half reach past it), its first bone hanging from the head's bone, the authored shape its rest; the mesh is skinned to it (data-model.md, The rig). A group's `physics` (`LockPhysics`: stiffness, damping, weight, gravity, wind, angle limit, neighbours) holds for each of its locks unless the lock's own says otherwise, and each lock is tied to the locks either side of it in its group. The character's `colliders` are the shapes hair is kept out of. Nothing moves yet; a solver will drive these.
- **In the app** the Face stage's Hair section lists the head's groups, gives it a preset's groups in one step (with a fitted mass where the head has none, and the body's colliders, the first time), and sets a picked group's count, length, spread and curl, each drag one undo step. Its Flow, on a head with locks, sets the parting's kind (none, centre, side) and where across the top it runs, the whorl's place round the head and its turn, and the sweep, and adds and removes cowlicks: each choice and each drag one undo step.

## Blank mode

Templates are accelerators, not the only way in. A character can start from an empty body plan and generic parts, with anatomy added by hand.

- **Blank** is a body plan, `blank`, with one root `tube` named Body, no sockets and the default palette, listed in New character after the species plans as One tube, anatomy by hand. Its root has no socket plane, so the tube's open end closes round on it.
- **Generic templates** `tube`, `blob` and `sheet` are data files over existing blocks, each with one joint at its start, so one bone from end to end, and no role: `tube` one tube run with an open start and a round cap, resting forward; `blob` a skull run closed with a pole at each end, a sphere when nothing is drawn; `sheet` a lens-section tube run open in v (`openV`), two faces meeting at an edge, thin by default, resting back along the body. Their `generic` field makes each part choose its section profile, which views set it, and its rings and segments, in the Shape inspector's Generic section, the template's own being the defaults. Move's angles turn a sheet into place: up for a dorsal fin, out for a side fin, down for a fin underneath.
- **Anatomy by hand.** Move adds sockets, joints and landmarks on any part. Add socket places a socket where it is pressed, with an outward angle and a pair flag, and Add part offers every template on it. Add joint places a joint at the pressed u on the part's axis, with a hinge, limits and a stretch flag; it becomes a bone, so a generic tube given a knee and a socket at its end for a foot reaches, bends within its limits and plants as a leg. Add landmark places a named surface point for a face. Pair with… makes two unpaired parts of one template a pair, and Rest in Place part turns a generic part to rest forward, back, up, down or out, so a tube on the body's rear end points back. The Shape inspector's Anatomy section lists a part's joints and landmarks.
- **A generic part's role is guessed.** A generic template gives no role, so a part placed from it has its role guessed from where it sits: a part leaving the root's rear end back along it a tail, one hanging down a leg, a sheet on the body's top or sides a fin, one on the head's top an ear. The Shape inspector's role chip shows the guess dashed until it is chosen. A generic tube rests forward, as Blank's root must, so a tube on the rear end is guessed other until its Rest is set to back, which guesses it again as a tail; the pose tools read the role. See [data-model.md](data-model.md), Roles.
- **Re-templating keeps the drawing.** Outlines live in part axes under the same views grammar, so a generic part can take a typed template later, or a typed part can drop to generic, and the outlines, parameters and surface work stay, through the part section's Template menu, one `SetTemplate`. A part may change to a template whose views set height and width as its own do, so every outline it reads keeps its plane, and on which every part on it finds a seat (`swapSeats`): a socket the new template has by the same name keeps its parts, and where the two templates are of one swap family (`SwapFamily`: any limb for a limb, any hand or foot for a hand or foot, otherwise one role each), the parts on a socket it lacks move to its free socket nearest where they were, within a quarter of the part along it and around it and mirrored as it was, so a leg's paw moves to a bird leg's foot socket or an arm's hand socket, carrying where the Move tool put that socket; a template with no such seat for every part on it is not offered. The change, the role, the new seats (`SetSocket`) and the carried socket places are one step (`swapTemplateCommand`). An outline of a view the new template does not read stays stored and sets nothing, its row reading off, as a generic part's view turned off does. The part keeps a role in the new template's swap family, so a four-legged body's front leg stays an arm as a bird leg; otherwise a typed template gives its role. The root keeps its plan's template, since the plan hangs its sockets on that template by id. A newer version of the part's own template comes the same way, through Update from library at the head of the same menu; see [data-model.md](data-model.md), Update from library.
- **Save as template** turns a hand-built part, with its joints, sockets and landmarks, into a template file in the personal library: the saved templates' folder in the app's data folder, which the catalog loads after the shipped templates, so a part built once by hand is offered by Add part like any other template. See [data-model.md](data-model.md), Save as template.

Face mark groups on a generic head anchor to a landmark placed by hand, which is how the eye region is defined anyway.

Side-set eyes, a bird's: Add eyes puts one painted eye about the landmark on the head's side and its mirror across the middle, so a view shows the near one and the head hides the far one, since nothing stands off the surface. The eye's facing is the surface normal at its middle turned `lookOut` degrees outward, so an eye on the side of a head faces sideways and reads edge on from ahead.

## What this adds to the data model

- Built-in section profiles `circle`, `egg`, `keel` and `lens`, which a generic part chooses between; `lens` is the ear's, the wing's and the sheet's.
- A tube run open in v (`openV`), a sheet with two faces and an edge, and the `generic` template field.
- A per-template **view remap**, so a wing's planform drawn in Top can set height along its chord. The `views` map already allows it; the ear and wing templates use it.
- The **toe row**'s per-digit overrides, `digits`, set a digit's span, place across the row and angle, an angle past 90 degrees pointing the digit back, as a bird's hind toe, and its own joints; the row's `joints` give every digit a chain of joints, and its `tips` a socket at every digit's tip.
- A **row of sockets**: a hand-made socket with a count, sockets repeated straight along a parent's u, each carrying the size a part placed on it starts at, for spikes and plates.
- A **tip socket** on a run is a socket at u 1 facing along the axis, which `antler` carries; a toe row's `tips` put one on every digit it builds, lying on the digit's own surface (a socket's `patch`) and carried by its last joint, which `paw` carries from version 4 for claws.
- The **membrane** block, a web spanned between neighbouring digits of a toe row or a digits row, which hands, paws and feet use, or between a digit and the part's own run (`along`), which `wing-membrane` spans from its last finger back along the arm. A digit override's own `limits`, so each finger of a wing closes along its own path. Per-digit shape on the toe row: `section`, `width`, `taper` and `knuckle`. A template with webs offers a `webs` flag that switches them off.
- The skull's jaw block tags the mouth interior as an `inner` area with a color.
- New template files, each a data file over existing blocks: `nose`, `eye`, `neck` in the first version; `fang`, `teeth`, `tongue` later; `hair`, `hair-cap`, `wing-feathered`, `wing-membrane`, `spike`, `plate`, `horn`, `antler`, `claw`, `arm`, `hand`, `foot`, `head-round`, `torso-upright`, `beak`, `beak-lower`, `head-bird`, `leg-bird`, `foot-bird`, `tail-feathers`, `torso-bird`, `fin`, `hoof`, `pocket` and `scute` ship.
