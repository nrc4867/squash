# Face and clothes

Generated by `generateSchemas` from the document model and the recipe schema's descriptions.

## Mouth group

A part's `groups` entry `{"id", "name", "roles": [{"role", "marks", "fill", "ink"}], "controls"}`. Roles draw in the order listed; a new mouth lists them in this order:

| draw | role | holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `inner` | the opening between the lips, filled with its `fill` slot while the mouth is open: its first mark, a closed shape drawn below the lip line, is the fully open mouth, closed toward the lip line as `open` falls; with no mark, a lower lip drops from the lip line by `open`, from nothing at the corners to 0.45 of the lip line's width at its middle |
| 2 | `tongue` | closed marks filled with its `fill` slot, carried down with the lower lip and kept between the lips; inked when the role's `ink` is |
| 3 | `teeth` | closed marks filled with its `fill` slot, sharp at every place (a fang is three places), kept where drawn between the lips; inked when the role's `ink` is |
| 4 | `lipLine` | the line of the closed mouth, drawn on the muzzle and wrapping round it; a group with one is a mouth |
| 5 | `corners` | short marks at the mouth's ends |

Each role's marks are mark ids on the same part. The opening, tongue and teeth show only while `open` is above 0, on any head: the lips part on the surface, and a head whose template has a `jaw` joint also drops it. The lower lip is inked as the lip line is.

| control | meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `open` | How far the mouth opens: 0 closed, 1 open, and 1 when left out. Drives the head's jaw where its template has one and parts the lips on the surface either way: the inner, tongue and teeth roles show between the lip line and a lower lip below it. |
| `expressionBlend` | 0 shows the group's own marks, 1 the shown expression's. |
| `width` | Scales the mouth across. |
| `mouthShape` | Bends the lip line from its drawing: SMILE, FROWN, O or GRIMACE. |

An expression's `open` (`{"mouth": 1}`) opens a group by id while that expression shows, in place of its `controls.open`; `tools/recipe.sh` renders each expression's face close-up as `<outfit>-face-<expression>.png`, and `set=head:mouth.open:1` (or `set=head:open:1` for a part's one mouth) tries a control without editing the recipe.

## Regions

| question | answer |
| --- | --- |
| the shape of a border | a smooth closed curve through its places, so four or five places make a round patch; with `curved: false`, straight between them on a part joined into the body's one surface, as a head on a neck |
| a sharp point in a border | `corners`: indices into its places |
| a two-tone face | a light region and a dark one whose borders pass through the same places along their boundary; they meet along one edge with no gap and no overlap, and turn sharply where the two borders part |

### Eye look

How a painted eye looks. Lengths are shares of the head's own coordinates at the eye's middle: across (width, irisSize, irisForward) of the one that runs toward the face's front, down (height, irisUp, browRaise) of the one that runs down it. On an upright head (head-round) across is v, once round the head at the eye, and down is u, the head's length from chin to crown, so a unit across spans about 2.2 times a unit down; on a head resting forward (head, head-bird) across is u, along the head, and down is v, once round it. face-and-clothes.md gives both in body units for each head template.

## Garments

| question | answer |
| --- | --- |
| what cloth is | cells of each part's chart, 64 rows along the part (u) by 32 columns round it (v), a cell 1/64 of u by 1/32 of v; the cloth's edge is a smooth curve over the painted cells' edges, and paint under two cells wide drops out |
| areas or covers | `areas` paint the cloth as Dress does, stroke by stroke; `covers` are the simple form, a box of cells each, painted into the first area; a garment may have both |
| what areas write | with `areas`, `holes` or `tucks`, the parts covered and how far, the parts that come out through the cloth (a part on a socket inside the painted region and not painted itself), the tucked parts and the inked seams are read from the cells, as Dress writes them |
| a second colour | a later area with its own `color`: a collar, cuffs or a waistband; where two areas meet the cloth stays one piece and the seam is inked with the hems |
| a print on the cloth | a garment's `regions` and `marks`, written as a part's are but naming the body part under the cloth with `on`, in its u and v: a chest symbol as a region with `ink` (a filled shape, its border inked), a pocket as a region and a thin mark along its edge, a stripe as a long thin region; `mirrored` shows it again on the other side of the body |
| where a print shows | only on painted cloth: a region is cut to the garment's cloth and a mark ends where the cloth does; it rides the cloth's fit and pose and is not drawn while the garment is not worn |
| a thin or stitched line | a mark with `width` stops below 1 (0.4 is a thin stitch) in its own `slot`; a dashed stitch is a row of short marks |
| a curved hem or a side split | paint the band, then a stroke with `unpaint` (an ellipse or lasso) over the hem where it should open or curve |
| which way v faces | it depends on how the part rests; `tools/recipe.sh probe <recipe> part=<id>` prints where v 0, 0.25, 0.5 and 0.75 face |
| a sleeve or trouser leg | paint the limb from u 0; its root rows meet the torso's cells, so the cloth runs on from the shoulder or hip in one piece |
| what a cover covers | the cells of its part's 64 by 32 chart whose middles lie in its box, at least two rows and two columns; the hem follows those cells' edge, so a box edge moves by up to half a cell |
| a cover past its part's end | `from` below 0 or `to` past 1 runs the cloth on past that end, where the cells reach it |
| how far a cover stands off its part | the garment's `partFits` for that part, else its `fit`, else the cover's `ease` |
| where cloth stands off further | a cover's Side and Top `outlines`, along the part's own x, add to that |
| what a `redraw` lays over the part | outlines by view and parameters by key replace the part's; regions and marks replace by id, others kept; `color` replaces the base slot; `areas` by name |
| what a redraw starts from | the bare part; with several garments worn, the outermost garment that changes the part wins, its change alone applied |
| what a `swap` does | builds the part from the other template with only the swap's shape |
| a change on a pair | named by the left side, it holds for both sides, mirrored |
| order of garments | by `layer`, innermost first |

| --- | --- |
| garment `slot` | The wardrobe slot it goes in. |
| garment `layer` | Counted out from the skin: under a waistband 1, a waistband 2, over both 3. |
| garment `color` | Its palette slot. |
| garment `fit` | How far it stands off the body, in body units, 0 or more: about 0.005 skin-tight, 0.01 fitted, 0.02 to 0.03 loose on a person-sized body; each cover's own ease when left out. |
| garment `partFits` | A fit for one covered part (a pair by its left side), over the garment's. |
| garment `inkHem` | Whether its edges are inked. |
| garment `inkWeight` | The weight of its silhouette and inked edges as a share of the skin's line weight, 0 to 2; thinner than skin when left out, so cloth reads as cloth and not as a second body. |
| garment `hemRoll` | How far its hems, neck openings and cuffs roll out past the cloth, in body units, 0 to 0.03: a cuff or a rolled hem that reads as cloth with a thickness. Every hem stands a little off the skin whatever this is. |
| garment `creases` | How strongly it creases on the inner side of each bending joint it covers, with ink, a shade band and a small fold, 0 to 2, times the character's cloth creases. |
| garment `wrinkles` | How strongly it wrinkles where a pose bunches it (a seat, a knee, an inner elbow, a waist), 0 to 2, times the character's cloth creases; tight cloth wrinkles a little less than loose. |
| garment `drape` | How strongly it gathers in short folds just above its hems and cuffs, 0 to 2, times the character's cloth creases. |
| garment `eases` | How far it stands off a covered part along and round it, by part (a pair by its left side), over fit and partFits: keys in order along u, each [u, ease] or [u, front, back] in body units, read as one smooth curve with no steps and flat past the first and last key; the sides take the mean of front and back. A tee tight across the chest and looser at the hem, a sleeve snug at the shoulder and bloused at the forearm, jeans fitted at the hip and flared at the ankle. |
| garment `hang` | How the cloth hangs over each upright part it covers (a torso, a leg): from its highest covered place down, straight or flaring out, rather than following the body in; left out, it follows the body. |
| garment `drops` | Dropped seams, by part (a sleeve by its left side): how far down the part, in its u, 0 to 0.5, the seam sits where its cloth meets the cloth of the part it grows from; above it the cloth stands off as that part's does, with a pinched, inked seam, as a dropped shoulder. |
| garment `joins` | Covered parts whose join with the next covered part is inked. With areas, written from where two areas meet at a part's root instead. |
| garment `areas` | The cloth as named areas painted on the body, in order: each area's strokes take cells from the areas before it, so areas never share a cell. Where two areas meet the cloth is one piece with an inked seam (with inkHem). A part an area paints needs no cover. |
| garment `covers` | Boxes of cells, one a part: the simple form of cloth, painted into the first area. With areas, a cover on a part they paint only carries its outlines and ease, its range written from the cells. A pair's cover names its left side and covers both. |
| garment `holes` | Holes round a part's root: the part and every part beyond it come out through the cloth bare, or ringed by a cuff band. |
| garment `tucks` | Whether each small part inside the cloth (an ear, tail, horn, spike or fin it holds whole; the toes or claws at a foot's end it reaches) is tucked under smooth cloth or shaped by it. Left out: toes, claws, tails, horns and spikes tucked, the rest shaped. |
| garment `changes` | What it does to body parts under it, by part (a pair by its left side). With areas, a part the cloth says something of takes what the cells say (through where it comes out, hide where it is tucked); a change here holds on every other part. |
| garment `looseEnds` | Loose ends it hangs, as a scarf's tails. |
| garment `regions` | Regions printed on the cloth, as a chest symbol, a pocket or a stripe: filled shapes in a palette slot, the border inked with ink, given as a part's regions are but naming the body part under the cloth with on, in that part's u and v (or face places on a head, or anchors). They ride the cloth's fit and pose and show only where the cloth is painted, over its areas' colours. |
| garment `marks` | Lines printed on the cloth, as stitching or a pocket's outline: given as a part's marks are but naming the body part under the cloth with on; a line only (kind line), its weight along it from width (stops at 0 to 1 along, each a share of the line's weight: 0.4 a thin stitch), its colour from slot. A line shows only where the cloth is painted and ends where it ends. |
| area `id` | Unique within the garment, as area-1. |
| area `name` | Its name in Dress; blank when left out. |
| area `color` | Its palette slot, as a red collar on a blue tee; the garment's color when left out. |
| area `gather` | A ribbed band, as a cuff or a sweater's hem: the ease, in body units, the area pulls its cloth in to, eased in over two rows either side; the cloth stands off as the rest does when left out. |
| area `fold` | Cloth turned back over the garment at its hem, as a rolled cuff, a rolled jeans hem or a turtleneck's fold-over: how far, in body units, the area stands out over the cloth beside it, its far edge inked and its color the cloth's back face; lies flat when left out. |
| area `opacity` | Sheer cloth, as stockings, tights or a chiffon layer: how much of the skin under it the cloth hides where it faces the viewer, 0 to 1, times its slot's own opacity; it hides more as it turns away, so it darkens toward the silhouette. The skin's colour, shading and ink show through, tinted by the cloth. Whole when left out. |
| area `net` | A net of thin threads whose holes show the skin, as fishnets or a lace-style mesh, finer than a cell; plain cloth when left out. |
| area `paint` | The strokes that paint it, laid in order: a stroke paints cells into this area and takes them from every other; unpaint takes them from every area. |
| stroke | One Paint stroke on a part, as Dress lays it: the cells of the part's chart (64 rows along it in u, 32 columns round it in v) whose middles the shape holds. A cell is 1/64 of u by 1/32 of v; the cloth's edge is a smooth curve over the cells' edges, and an area under two cells wide drops out, unless its strokes are exact. brush: every cell within size of the path through points (or face), straight between them in u and v; one place is a dab. line: every cell within size of the straight lines in the body between its places. A brush or line reaches the parts beyond its part (from a sleeve onto the hand), never the parts it sits on. band: rows u[0] to u[1] the whole way round. box: u[0] to u[1] along and v[0] to v[1] round. ellipse: the ellipse inside that box. lasso: inside the closed polygon through points (or face), straight between them in u and v, or a smooth curve through them when curved. opening: the cells round where the part on comes out (a neck, an arm, an ear) within reach past its root ring, on every part but those beyond it, its front dropping depth below the ring in its cut: unpaint one for a crew or V neckline, paint a wider one and unpaint a narrower for a collar band round it. |
| stroke `on` | The part painted on (a pair by either side: both sides take it, mirrored). |
| stroke `shape` | brush, line, band, box, ellipse, lasso or opening. |
| stroke `unpaint` | Takes the cells from every area instead of painting them, as Alt in Dress. |
| stroke `u` | band, box, ellipse: [from, to] along the part, 0 at its root to 1 at its far end. |
| stroke `v` | box, ellipse: [from, to] round the part; from may lie below 0 to cross v 0. The whole way round when left out. |
| stroke `points` | brush, line, lasso: the places as [u, v] on the part. |
| stroke `face` | brush, line, lasso on a head: the places as [x, y] on its face seen from straight ahead, as a region's face. |
| stroke `size` | brush, line: how far from the path a cell's middle may lie and be painted, in body units, as Dress's Size. |
| stroke `mirrored` | On a part in the body's middle (a torso, a head): also painted mirrored across the middle, at -v, as Dress's Mirror. |
| stroke `clean` | Clean edges, 0 to 1: up to four rounds of smoothing the area's edge within two cells of the stroke, each cell taking what most of it and its neighbours are. |
| stroke `cut` | opening: round, a crew or scoop neck's curve, or v, two straight sides to a point. |
| stroke `reach` | opening: how far past the root ring of the part on it reaches all round, in body units. |
| stroke `depth` | opening: how far its front drops below the ring at its middle, in body units; 0 is a plain ring. |
| stroke `exact` | band, box, ellipse, lasso: the stroke is kept as a vector shape, and where every stroke on its part is exact the cloth's edge there is the shapes' outline itself rather than a smooth curve over the cells, however narrow: a tie, a collar's points, a strap, a bikini's cups, cut crisp and with no area dropping out. It paints every cell within half a cell of it and unpaints those more than half a cell inside it; a box or band reaching within half a row of an end of its part runs on over that end. A stroke on a pair names the side the pair is kept on; clean is 0. |
| stroke `curved` | An exact lasso: one smooth curve through its places rather than straight between them, turning sharply at corners. |
| stroke `corners` | An exact lasso with curved: the indices of the places where the curve turns sharply, as a tie's tip or a collar's point. |
| hole `part` | The part that comes out (a pair by its left side, both sides). |

## View keys

A recipe's `viewKeys`: head cheats that follow the camera. Squash builds each head once at eight directions round its own facing (front, three-quarter, side and three-quarter back on either side, and back) on the surface it already has, and blends the two nearest as the camera turns, so the head's paint, eyes and ink marks ride on it. Which keys hold is read from where the camera sits round the head's own facing, so a head turned over the shoulder picks its keys by where it points. Keys are written as seen with the camera on the head's left; for the right Squash mirrors them, a paired part's move going to its twin, unless a key names `side`.

With `auto` every head has defaults: its muzzle leans toward the camera, its ears turn toward profile, its back is drawn a little wider and lower, and, in the turning face style, its near cheek fills out and its chin slides toward the camera. Its face follows the camera as far as its style says, sliding back to hide behind the head past the side view.

| field | meaning |
| --- | --- |
| viewKeys `auto` | Whether every head's default keys apply: its muzzle leans toward the camera and its ears turn toward profile. On when left out. |
| viewKeys `keys` | Keys per view, each move replacing the default for the same thing at that view. |
| viewKeys `face` | How the face meets a turning camera: turning (the face slides part way round toward the camera and the head's shape changes from view to view) or fixed (the head keeps one shape from the front round to the side and the face stays turned to the viewer). The head template's when left out. |
| viewKeys `faceFollow` | How far the face's features (eyes, brows, nose, mouth, muzzle, cheek marks) slide round the head toward the camera, from 0 (fixed on the head) to 1 (centred on the viewer up to the side view). Past the side view they slide back and hide behind the head. The face style's when left out. |
| viewKeys `keepFace` | Whether the face slides as one piece and keeps its features' size, rather than gathering toward the far side as it turns. The face style's when left out. |
| key `view` | The view: front, three-quarter, side, three-quarter-back or back, by where the camera sits round the head's own facing. |
| key `side` | The camera's side of the head this key holds for: left or right. Left out, the key is for the left and Squash mirrors it for the right; the front and back take none. |
