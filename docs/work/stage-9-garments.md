# Stage 9: garments

A working note for the branches that build clothing: garments, layers, outfits and slots, body-part changes, loose ends and the Dress stage. Folded into the HLD or deleted when it lands.

## Goal

Clothing is drawn on each character, starting from the body it covers, with no garment templates. A garment is its own surface, a branch per covered part swept along that part's axis and pushed off it by an ease, reshaped with the outline tools Shape already has, weighted by the body under it so it follows every pose with no rig of its own. Garments stack in layers, change the body parts they cover, and switch by outfit and slot. Dress is a Build stage, boards W13 to W15 on the restructure canvas.

## What the design already holds

The Garments tab of the plan doc, and the notes on W13 to W15. In the code: a part is a row of slices (`Slice`: a frame on the midline, height, width, a closed section) and `SliceBuilder.loft` builds a patch from any row of slices; `PlacedPart` carries every built patch with its slices in the part's own space and places it in body space; the rig weighs every vertex by its part, patch and u (`Patch.boneUOf`), so a vertex that knows the body point it came from can take that point's weights; `socketFilletNode` grows a fillet where one placed part comes out of another with one side still (`ImplicitBlend`'s `moves`), and `PatchSamples` fits each patch a field whose sign is the inside test; `Character.resolved` lays a pair's values, then a side's overrides; anchors name a part and a patch.

What it does not hold: anything worn, a surface that belongs to no part, a part whose template or data changes with what is worn, a vertex hidden by another surface, a document list of named sets with picks.

## Decided

- **The wardrobe is on the character.** `Character.wardrobe` holds `garments`, `slots` (named and ordered, `GarmentSlot`), `outfits` (each a name and at most one garment per slot) and `shown`, the outfit Build shows, or none for the bare body. A garment's slot is its own value, so the outfit's pick for a slot can only name a garment in that slot. A palette slot is `SlotName`; a wardrobe slot is `GarmentSlotId`, so the two never meet.
- **A garment** is an id, a name, a slot, a layer, its branches, join ink switches, hem and cuff ink, its fillet, its colours as palette slots, its body-part changes and its loose ends. A branch names one covered part, the u range it covers, an ease, and its own Side and Top outlines and surface work, the part's panes being where they are drawn.
- **A branch on a paired part covers both sides.** It names the left part; the right side's branch is the same branch mirrored, as a pair's data is shared. Per-side garment overrides are out of scope; Mirror in Dress is how the board draws it and it stays on.
- **Worn is derived.** The garments worn are the shown outfit's picks, inner layer first. Layer is a number per garment, so a jacket stays over a shirt in every outfit; the board's reorder is one command over all layers.
- **A body-part change belongs to the garment.** Per part a garment covers or touches: Hide, Through (cuff on, cuff size, ink cuff), Redraw (a `PartData`: outlines, params and surface work on the part's own template) or Swap (a template ref and its `PartData`). Keyed by the part; on a paired part it holds for both sides. A part with no change is covered (its skin hidden under the garment) or through where it sits on a socket inside the coverage.
- **Resolution order.** Pair, then side, then each worn garment's change, innermost first, the outermost winning (tentative, as the plan says). Hide and Swap are read where parts are placed, Redraw where a part's data is resolved. One function in `document`, `Character.wornPart`, answers all three, so the slice build, the assembly and the rig read one thing.
- **The shell is built from the body's slices.** A branch's rings are its part's slices over the branch's u range, each kept on its frame and section and grown by the ease in height and width. A drawn Side or Top outline then sets the ring's height or width wherever it stands off further than the ease's minimum; where it hugs the body, the minimum holds. Past its part's end a branch continues along the part's last slice frame, at the last slice's size. The branch is lofted with `SliceBuilder.loft` at the part's segment count and its own ring count, so its topology is fixed by the garment, not the drawing.
- **A garment is placed parts.** Each branch is placed in body space as its part is, a `PlacedPart` with no part id of its own but a garment id and its covered part, so lines, shading, colour and picking read garments as they read parts. Branch ids are `garment/<garment>/<part>` wherever a `PartId` is needed (the rig, the mesh's tags); a document part id never starts with `garment/`.
- **Weights from the body.** A branch vertex takes the weights of its body part at the vertex's u, clamped to the part's end past it, so the branch rides the bones its part rides and the joint and seam bands hold. No garment carries bones, except a loose end's.
- **A branch can cover part of the way round.** A branch covers a u range and a v range of its part: the whole way round, as a sleeve or a hood, or a region of the surface, as a crown cap from the brow back or the back of an open-front jacket, the shell standing just off that region and its open edges hems. Its columns are as many as the part's over that span, plus one.
- **Stiff hats are parts the garment owns.** Brims, crowns and helmets are a part of their own, a generic blob, tube or sheet on a crown socket, owned by the garment, drawn like any part, with Hide or Through on the body parts it covers. A garment can carry parts on sockets. Designed under Parts a garment owns, not built.
- **Covered skin and inner layers hidden, in every pose.** A body vertex is hidden when a worn branch covers it, by the branch's own rings read from its grid as it stands: between two of them along their axis, inside the nearer one's polygon, and no nearer to an open end of the branch than to its wall, so what shows through a hem or a neck opening is skin, never a hole. A lofted shell needs no fitted field for this. Hidden vertices drop their faces from the drawn mesh and their lines; the mesh's grid is not changed, so anchors still land. An inner layer's vertex an outer garment covers is hidden by the same test. A cuff hides the skin and fur under it as a branch does. A part that comes out through a branch is left whole by that branch, as the branch's hole leaves it open there. Each hidden part keeps what hides it, and the posed body decides again in every pose (`hiddenAgain`), so a pose that carries skin or an inner layer out of every garment shows it, one that carries it in hides it, holes follow the part through them, and covered fur shows when carried out. Measured by `GarmentGapBenchTest` (slow), which counts a vertex as seen when rays from it along at least 10 of 96 ways leave the character crossing no shown face; a hole is a hidden vertex that is seen, and a pose counts only holes that were not holes at rest. The dressed fennec at rest 347 of 5,735 seen (the feet's Hide and the cap's front edge); knee 60°, 2; knee 110°, 0; neck 20°, 6; neck 40°, 11; person at rest 772 of 8,941 seen (most of them the feet under the sandals' soles); elbows, 0; shoulder 80°, 45 (hand 33, shirt sleeve 9), with 502 vertices hidden at rest shown again and seen, the hand, sleeves and arm the raised arm lifts out of the jacket's body. Deciding again costs about 3 to 5 ms of a drag step (`DressedPoseStepBenchTest`, slow), the step 3 to 10 ms slower in all on a busy machine. Pose-time projection between layers is not built: the garments themselves still part where two garments meet across a deep bend, as the sweater's sleeve and the boot at the fennec's bent knee, where the leg's skin shows between them.
- **Joins by socket fillet.** Where two branches of one garment meet, at a covered part's socket on another covered part, the branch on the child moves onto a socket fillet and the parent's branch stays still, as `withSocketFillets` does for parts, and the child's faces inside the parent branch are left out. The fillet radius is the garment's Fillet as a share of the child branch's length along its part. Ink at a fillet's foot is the join's switch, off by default at branch joins; hem and cuff ink are on by default. Outermost wins stays, tentative.
- **Holes from the socket tree.** A part on a socket inside the region a branch covers that the garment does not hide goes Through: the branch's faces inside the through part are left out, and unless its Through change turns the cuff off, a cuff is lofted over the through part from its root to the cuff size past where it comes out of the branch, as a share of the part's length (a short band, 0.1 by default), grown by the branch's ease, coloured as the garment.
- **Garment ink is lines like a crease.** A branch's or cuff's inked edges are found by the crease node and drawn as creases are: the hem (the grid's open edges where its faces show; a cuff's far end only), on by default; a cuff's far edge and where it comes out of its branch, with both the garment's cuff switch and the Through change's; where a part comes through with no cuff, with the hem switch; a join, where a child branch comes out of its parent branch, only with its switch, off by default. An outer layer's hem is a hem, so it is inked by default.
- **Loose ends are a bone chain.** A loose end is a chain of a few bones from a hem point, posed by hand as a tail is, with Follow-through, Spring and Collide body as stored settings. Simulation is out of scope; Spring and Collide body are kept for Shot's settle. Built as a flat strip with closed ends, hung from its branch's far ring (or its middle ring when not from the hem) at its v, down and a little out from the body, a bone's length (0.06 of the covered part's length) per bone, a sixth of the hem's way round wide; its bones are `end-1` to `end-n` on the loose end's own part id `garment/<garment>/end/<id>`, the first hanging from the body bone that moves the branch most at the root, hinged across the strip. Turn reaches them as any bone, and pose commands accept the loose end's part id. Its length and width are fixed shares, not drawn (open question).
- **Surface work on garments** lives in each branch, and its anchors name the garment's branch part id and its patch.
- **Dress is stage 6, Pose 7.** As the boards draw: Shape, Color, Lines, Face, Rig, Dress, then Pose on key 7 until Shot takes it. Dress's keys: Draw garment D, Edit curves E, Erase X, Smooth S, Cover C, Loose end L, Mirror M, Half outline H, New garment N.
- **The shown outfit is saved** with the character, in its document.
- **Outside Dress, a press passes through garments** to the body, in every stage. In Dress, a press on a garment picks it and selects the part its branch covers.
- **Painting garments is deferred.** No garment target for the Color, Lines or Face tools, and no selection that follows a garment across stages.
- **The fennec's scarf** sits at the torso's front end where the head joins, narrower than the sweater; round 7 hangs a loose end from it.
- **The View menu shows garments** Shown, Ghosted (translucent, ink only) or Hidden: view state, not saved, one setting for Dress, Shown by default, and one for every other stage, Ghosted by default; the menu shows and sets the one for the stage on screen. Shown is the dressed character. Ghosted dresses it with nothing hidden under the garments, and the panes draw each garment part faint in the look's own fill with its outline, after everything behind it. Hidden wears no branches, so the body shows as the garments' body-part changes leave it. The layers list's eye leaves one garment out of view the same way, not saved. The session reads all of it as a graph input beside the dressing (`GarmentView`), so lines, fur and picking follow. Stretch's MenuLabel and checked MenuItem draw the groups; no Stretch change.
- **The outline tools draw on the picked garment in Dress.** Draw garment, Edit curves, Erase and Smooth write the picked garment's branch over the selected part (the branch on either side of a pair), or its Redraw of the part while Redraw is pressed on that part's Body under row. With no garment picked, or none over the selected part, they do nothing. Outside Dress they draw the part's own outlines.
- **Garment slots in the inspector.** A new garment starts in no slot; its slot chip moves it to any slot or none. The Body under section offers Hide, Through, Redraw and Swap with none pressed while the garment holds no change (the part is covered, or through where it sits on a socket inside the cover); pressing the held kind takes the change away. Redraw starts from the part's own data. Swap offers the templates the part's socket takes, and is faint where it takes no other.
- **The View menu switches Posed and Rest,** beside the garments switch: view state, not saved, Posed by default, with a key if a free one fits the key rules. Rest shows every pane at rest except in the Pose stage, where the pose is what is edited. Posed is the panes as they are, with Single following the pose as Full does. The locked Side, Top and Front panes in Shape and Dress stay at rest in both, since outlines are drawn in rest axes. Single's free camera follows the pose as Full does, and Single snapped to a view follows the locked panes. One rule, `posesPane`, decides the tools' body (`showsPose`) and what the panes draw. Covered in `ShowsPoseTest`. Round 6. No key: none free fits the key rules.
- **Fillet speed:** fields cached per build where cheap, measured and reported, not blocking. Measured on a small fennec body (torso, head, ears, front legs): dressing in a sweater with both sleeves and a cap takes about 23 ms, of which about 9 ms is each sleeve's fillet; without the sleeves, 6 ms. A garment's joins share one field cache for the run (`FieldCache`), so a parent branch several children fillet into, as the sweater's body under both sleeves, is fitted once: three fits for the two sleeves where there were four. `GarmentFilletBenchTest` (slow) measures it; with another build running on the machine the numbers swing by two times, the sweater with sleeves and cap at 25 to 53 ms with the cache against 42 to 102 ms without, the sleeveless case 6 to 16 ms in both. Fields are not kept across builds, since every branch is lofted anew on each.
- **Examples.** The fennec in a Winter outfit (hat with the ears through, scarf, sweater over torso and front legs, boots that hide the toes), with Beach and Bare outfits beside it, and a person in a shirt, jacket and sandals with a redrawn foot, each built by a fixture and goldens like the other examples. The person (`examples/person-dressed.json`, `PersonDressed.kt`) wears Summer: a shirt on torso and arms, a jacket one layer out stopping short of the shirt's hem and cuffs, and sandals, a sole round the bottom of each foot whose Redraw gives the foot its five toes in the skin; Bare beside it. No Beach outfit is built for the fennec.

## Exit criteria

1. **A shell from the body.** A sweater on the fennec's torso and front legs and a hat on its head build through the real slice build, match their drawings within the ease's minimum, keep a fixed topology, and follow a pose with the body's weights.
2. **Joins and holes.** Branches meet by fillet with the inner one still, ears come through a hat with a cuff, hems are inked.
3. **Changes come and go.** Hide, Through, Redraw and Swap change the body while worn; taking the garment off gives the body back exactly; the outer garment wins.
4. **Layers hide what they cover.** At rest no inner layer or skin shows through an outer garment.
5. **Outfits switch** by one command that undoes in one step.
6. **Dress is built to W13 to W15.**
7. **They read as clothing** in the smoke renders, judged by eye.
8. **Tiers hold.** `./gradlew check` passes; goldens are regenerated on purpose.

## Out of scope

Garment templates and a garment library, garments moved between characters, per-side garment overrides, pose-time projection between layers, cloth simulation and Shot's settle, a garment on a planned part, per-shot outfits (Shot keys the outfit when Shot is built).

## Parts a garment owns: design

Stiff hats, brims and helmets are parts a garment owns, on sockets, drawn like any part. Not built; this is the smallest home found for them, and it waits on the questions below.

- **Where they live.** In `Character.parts`, as ordinary `PartInstance`s with one new field, `garment: GarmentId?`, the garment that owns the part, null for the body. Every graph node in `CharacterSession` (slice rows, meshes, blends, surfaces, surface work, colours) is keyed by a document part id, and so is every command (`SetOutline`, `SetParameter`, surface work, pose bones through `requirePart`), every anchor and every tool that selects a part. A part held in the character's list keeps all of that keying sound with nothing new: the Shape, Color, Lines and Rig tools draw it as they draw any part, and its anchors and bones name it as they name any part. Holding the parts inside `Garment` instead would need a second key (garment, part) on every node, command and anchor, which is the cost this avoids.
- **When they build.** `assemblyParts` leaves out a part whose garment is not worn, and every part on it, as it leaves out a part a worn garment hides. Its nodes stay alive and cached while it is not worn, so switching outfits back is cheap. The rig rigs it as any part on its socket, so a hat on the head rides the head's bone with no weights from the body.
- **Ownership by command.** `AddParts` takes the owner on the instance; `RemoveGarment` removes the garment's parts with it in the same undo step; `RemovePart` of an owned part works as for any part. A body part can never sit on an owned part's socket unless that part's garment owns it too, so taking the garment off never strands a body part.
- **What it changes on the body.** The garment's own `changes` keep holding Hide, Through, Redraw and Swap for the body parts under the owned part, as for its branches. A part on a socket the owned part covers is not cut through it: an owned part is a closed part, not a shell with holes.

Questions for Nicholas before it is built:

- Whether owned parts show in the Parts tree among the body's parts (named by their garment, faint while it is not worn), only under their garment in Dress, or both. W13's Parts drawer draws no owned part.
- What a body part does where it passes through an owned part (the fennec's ears through a brimmed hat): left overlapping as parts overlap at sockets today, hidden inside it by the same inside test the branches use, or cut with a hole and a cuff as a branch does, which means owned parts gain the holes that only shells have now.
- Whether an owned part may be a pair (earmuffs, a pair of horns on a helmet), which the field allows with no more work but the plan has not asked for.

## Open questions for Nicholas

- Whether Ghosted garments outside Dress read well, or Shown should be the default everywhere. Built Ghosted: the garments read as pale outlined shells and the body shows through them, but its own lines and fur under a garment are hidden by the garment's depth.
- A garment in no slot, or in a slot the shown outfit does not fill, is not worn, so a new garment shows nothing until it is put in a slot and picked. Whether Dress should show the picked garment while it is drawn even when the outfit does not wear it.
- W13's Draw garment rows Snap to body and Close end, and W15's Edit curves row Handles, have no model behind them.
- W15's Head marks row: marks are not parts, and a garment's change is per part; whether marks under a garment get their own change.
- The layers list's Show button hides a garment from view only; whether it should be saved.
- The three questions on parts a garment owns, above.
- A loose end's length and width are fixed shares of its covered part and its hem; whether they should be values of the loose end, drawn, or set in the inspector.
- Whether the fennec's scarf, which the head comes through, should keep the cuff round the neck that Through gives by default, or the head go through it with no cuff.
