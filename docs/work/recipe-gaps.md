# Recipe gaps

Where an author writing a recipe still has to read code, or guess and render, to learn what is possible. Each line: what was read or tried, the fact needed, and what tool or reference would have told it. A working note: entries close as tools and the reference grow.

## What the reference and probe hold

`docs/reference/` (generated by `generateSchemas`, checked by `schema-gen`'s tests) covers templates, plans, dotted part parameters with their named recipe fields, the part frame and bone axes by rest direction, mouth roles, and how garment fit, covers, redraws and swaps combine. `tools/recipe.sh probe` answers frame questions on a built recipe: u and v at a body point or a face place, the body point at a u and v, each part's extent, frame, joints, sockets, and a head's face reach.

## Left

- **Proportions.** No guide to how long a leg, tail or neck is against the body at `size` 1, so a first build is often leggy or the tail too high. A per-plan table of default part extents (the probe's `part=all` on an undrawn character of each plan) would do.
- **Face reach as a shape.** The probe gives the box of a head's face places; the face narrows toward chin and crown, so a mask's corners can still fall off. A face outline in face coordinates, or the nearest legal place in the error, would close it.
- **Rest directions between the five.** `frames.md` tabulates forward, back, up, down and out; a template resting between them (a tail back and up) needs the probe to read its frame, and which sign of `lean` lowers it is only in the probe's Side's +y.
- **Eye look ranges.** `look` fields (`tilt`, `openingTilt`, `irisSize`, ...) have no ranges or units in the reference; the schema holds them as numbers only.
- **Region layering.** A template patch region (`patch: "muzzle"`) and a `face` region on the same head show two shades of one slot with a seam; how regions on different patches meet is not said.
- **Fur sizes.** `fur.spacing`, `fur.length`, patch rows and columns have no guidance for a small character.
- **A leg without a paw.** Whether a quadruped's leg should always carry a `paw` is not said; without one it ends in a flat stump.
- **Painted garments.** Dress paints a garment's cloth as named areas of cells on each part's chart, with holes round a part's root and seams where areas touch, and writes its covers, changes and inked joins from them. A recipe holds covers and changes only, whose boxes are painted as cells as it builds, so a garment painted past its covers' boxes exports as its written covers and loses its areas and holes. Recipe `areas` (a brush, band or shape stroke on a part, as Paint's options take them) and `holes` on a garment would carry it.
- **`example-limits.md`** is long and mostly about bipeds; an index by plan would help a quadruped author skip it.
